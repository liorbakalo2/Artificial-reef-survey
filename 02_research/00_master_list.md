## 3. Lior's own proposal (Haifa surf reef)

Lior's core, original idea — set out mainly in `hebrew_surf_route_summary.md` (his own pitch PDF, addressed to Haifa Deputy Mayor Avihu Han) and grounded in field data from `lior_suggestions_and_swell_heights.md` and `pptx_goldcoast_swells_people_photos.md` — is a **submerged artificial reef off Haifa that reshapes wave refraction, like a natural headland/point break, to amplify existing sea swell into a rideable wave at one chosen convergence point, with zero added energy input** (pure seabed-contour reshaping), as a cheaper, always-on alternative to an energy-hungry inland wave pool.

Key claimed benefits, per Lior's own pitch: (1) calm water behind/around the structure enabling swimming, explicitly framed as "the same effect as a breakwater without harming the coastline" — i.e., without a visible above-water wall blocking the horizon; (2) possible increase in local fish population (uncited claim); (3) potentially more efficient wave-energy harvesting since waves break at one concentrated spot. He explicitly distinguishes Haifa's case from Gold Coast's: in Australia the reef reshapes already-big waves, but in Haifa the structure must *actively increase* wave height at the chosen spot — making an artificial wave pool, not a coastal-protection reef, the more relevant comparison.

Design basis (from field measurements at what is implied to be the Haifa/Shikmona area): minimum surfable wave defined as Significant Wave Height 0.45–0.6 m / Maxima Wave Height 0.6–0.8 m, swell direction 285–294° (WNW), cross-checked against Surfline/Magicseaweed and the Israeli "4Surfers" forecast service and against real Shikmona-buoy readings (IsraMar) for several July–October 2025 windows (observed SWH up to 0.98 m, max wave height up to 1.24 m). Target depth bands: "No Breaking" 1.45–1.7 m, "Wave Breaking Depth" 1.3–1.5 m, "Min Depth" 1.15–1.35 m, with a horizontal "2 m until break" (±0.5 m) length measurement. His page-5 concept sketch (an unbuilt design) proposes a cheap, light, rock-ballasted, deliberately temporary/removable frame that raises the sea floor only up to the natural "Wave Breaking Depth" line, chosen specifically so it can be deployed and removed fast (fast build, low permitting/environmental footprint, reversible).

Israel's small tidal range (explicitly called out in his own draft outreach email to the NGO Zalul, pptx slide 17) is treated as a structural advantage for reef stability and low long-term maintenance versus ocean sites with large tidal swings — this framing is Lior's own and is the central "why Israel is different" argument the final HTML page needs to carry through.

Market/economics figures used to justify the proposal (sourcing very thin — flagged in Gaps): Haifa/North active surfers >5,000 (of which >500 "surf nomads" traveling >30 min for a wave); national active surfers >13,000; average natural surfable days in Israel <1/week; projected Haifa tourism draw ~8,000 unique visitors/year (basis: 40-person wave capacity, 2-hour turnover, 1 operating day/week); wave-pool price benchmarks used for contrast — worldwide $60–200/hr, Kelly Slater's pool ~$10,000/day (~$450/wave), Hadera Israel surf-simulator 1,200 NIS/45 min per group of 10; Gold Coast reef cited as a $2.5M cost benchmark and its wave quality described as disappointing for serious surfing because the design prioritized shore protection over surf shape, despite a cited (uncredited) $8M/yr local surf-tourism value study that reportedly prompted plans for a second, surf-dedicated Gold Coast reef; Ashkelon's 10.5M NIS / ~10-dunam / 4 m project is cited purely as an Israeli unit-cost benchmark, not as a surf-reef case study.

**Israel skatepark budget benchmark** *(critic addition, from `lior_notes_geotubes_and_boscombe` — Hebrew geotube/Boscombe note, §3 "תקציבים והערכת עלות" / budgets and cost estimation)*: as a further, distinct Israeli budget anchor (separate from the Gold Coast $2.5M and Ashkelon 10.5M NIS benchmarks above), Lior uses municipal skatepark construction budgets in Israel as the closest local proxy for what a city would realistically invest in improving water sports — a target budget ceiling of ~8,000,000 NIS. Haifa's own skatepark carries ~500,000 NIS/yr ongoing maintenance. Lior separately notes a plain breakwater design alone runs ~500,000 NIS, and assumes a complex project like his proposed reef would need at least double that for design fees alone (~1,000,000 NIS).
