## Motivation and who pushed for it

ECOncrete was founded in 2012 by two Israeli marine ecologists, Dr. Shimrit Perkol-Finkel and Dr. Ido Sella, around the argument that standard concrete coastal infrastructure (its chemistry, smooth/featureless surface texture, and steep inclination relative to natural rocky shores) is ecologically "catastrophic" for marine recruitment, and that a bio-enhanced concrete mix and surface design can retrofit or replace standard units without sacrificing structural performance [R1][R2][R11]. The company's own framing treats each Israeli site as a pilot/demonstration opportunity that arose from other parties' unrelated construction or retrofit projects, rather than a sediment-transport or storm-damage program initiated by Lior's other Israel-coast targets (Ashkelon geotubes, Netanya/MCCP breakwaters, etc.):

- **Haifa/Polinom**: the pilot was possible because a new port section ("Polinom") was already being built in Haifa Bay in 2012 using standard Portland-cement "Antifer" armor units; ECOncrete's units were installed alongside the standard ones specifically to run a side-by-side ecological comparison, per the company's own case study [R1][R2]. Hebrew-language coverage separately identifies "Polinom"/"Polynom" as a large hangar/facility built for the Israeli Navy's submarines in Haifa Bay, and states that "ecological antifers" were placed there during its construction [R3][R4] — this dossier treats this as the same installation described in the ECOncrete case study (see disambiguation note below).
- **Herzliya Marina**: the marina, opened to the public in 1995 and one of the first in Israel to receive the Blue Flag eco-label, is described by ECOncrete as wanting to "elevate ecosystem function" consistent with that eco-branding when retrofitting its concrete seawall [R2][R9]. No named individual or council decision authorizing the ECOncrete retrofit was found.
- **Jaffa Port**: the Atarim Group (the Tel Aviv-Yafo municipal coastline corporation) commissioned three tide-pool units as an explicit trial. The port's own marine ecologist, **Rotem Trabitzky**, was quoted framing it as balancing conservation against development and using the installation to "adapt the system to the local environment" [R5][R6].

Lior's own project files do not discuss this ECOncrete Israel cluster in detail; the seed note in the project's own discovery sweep flags the Haifa Port pilot and the "Polinom"/Bay Port installation as possibly the same site described two different ways, and recommends a dossier author fetch econcretetech.com's own case-study page directly to resolve it [R14][R15] — this dossier's direct read of the company's full case-study PDF (which uses the name "Polinom Port – Haifa" throughout and explicitly says "In 2012, a new port named 'Polinom' was constructed at the bay of Haifa, Israel") resolves that flag: **the Haifa Port breakwater pilot (2013–2016) and the "Bay Port"/Polinom installation are the same project**, not two distinct ECOncrete sites [R1][R2].

## Design as planned

**Haifa/Polinom**: ECOncrete's armoring units were designed as a drop-in structural substitute for standard cube-shaped Portland-cement "Antifer" armor units, differing on three levels — concrete chemistry (bio-enhancing admix), surface complexity (textured mold to mimic natural rocky-habitat surface roughness), and macro-design (larger surface features offering refuge to bigger fauna) — while meeting the same load-bearing/armoring function as the standard antifers they sit beside [R1][R2]. The plan was a 24-month side-by-side comparative biological-performance study (2013–2015) against the standard antifers already comprising the breakwater [R2].

**Herzliya Marina**: precast seawall face panels using the same three-level design difference, intended either to retrofit an existing wall or serve as a fully structural, load-bearing wall for new construction, explicitly built to "comply with all the standard seawall requirements for structural performance" [R2]. The study design paired a scraped-clean 30×30 cm quadrat on the existing marina seawall (control/baseline) against the new ECOncrete units, tracked over time [R2].

**Jaffa Port**: precast tide-pool units set into the port infrastructure to create water-retaining intertidal habitat features absent from a standard quay/breakwater face, explicitly described by the port's own ecologist as an experiment whose purpose was to "adapt the system to the local environment" before wider use [R5][R6].

## What was actually built, and how it differed from the design

No source found for any of the three sites reports a deviation between what was designed and what was actually installed — all three case studies/articles describe the built installation in the same terms as the stated design intent, with no reported cost overrun, redesign, or construction problem:

- Haifa/Polinom: units were deployed and monitored for 24 months (2013–2015) exactly as planned [R1][R2].
- Herzliya: units were deployed November 2014 and monitoring was extended to 22 months post-deployment (through roughly September 2016), one of very few numeric deviations found (a longer-than-24-month stated duration compared with Haifa) [R2].
- Jaffa: 3 units were installed within one week (week of 7 Feb 2019) [R5]; no later phase-2 expansion or removal was found in any source reached.

## Outcome

**Haifa/Polinom (ecological)**: Over the 24-month comparative study, ECOncrete armoring units supported **more than double the invertebrate species richness and biodiversity** compared with standard Antifer units, with taxa including oysters, sponges, Sabellidae, Serpulidae, tunicates, bryozoans and coralline algae. ECOncrete units also showed **significantly greater fish diversity**, including post-larval transient fish (Sparidae) absent from the standard units. Roughly **two-thirds of species observed on ECOncrete units were native**, with the remaining third invasive — the **opposite ratio** appeared on the standard antifers, where invasive species were significantly more dominant [R1][R2]. No independent (non-ECOncrete) verification of these figures, and no structural/coastal-protection performance data (storm survival, settlement, cracking) were found for the Haifa site — the case study's outcome claims are entirely ecological, not structural [R1][R2].

**Herzliya Marina (ecological)**: at 22 months post-deployment, ECOncrete seawall units were "covered with a variety of invertebrates, including sponges, oysters, bivalves, bryozoans, and coralline algae," while the control (the existing, unmodified marina seawall) showed a low-diversity assemblage that was **even lower than its own original baseline** — i.e., the existing infrastructure's biodiversity had declined further over the same window, not merely stayed flat [R2].

**Jaffa Port**: no quantified ecological result was found in any source reached — only the ecologist's qualitative framing quoted above. This is flagged as a genuine gap: Atarim Group and namalyafo.co.il (the port's own news outlet, which carries the original article) are the likeliest sources of any follow-up monitoring data, but the port's own article page returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch in this pass [blocked].

**Surfing quality**: not applicable — none of these are surf structures; they are coastal-protection/port armoring and marina infrastructure retrofits.

## Why it worked / why it failed

No failure is reported at any of the three sites in the sources reached; all coverage is uniformly positive (largely because nearly all detailed sourcing is ECOncrete's own case-study literature, not independent third-party monitoring — see caution below). The company's stated mechanism for why the bio-enhanced units outperform standard concrete: (1) the admix chemistry is designed to be less hostile to marine larval settlement than standard Portland cement's high-alkalinity surface; (2) textured surface mimics the roughness of natural rocky shores, which standard smooth antifers lack; (3) macro-scale design features (crevices, refuges) provide shelter for larger fauna the way natural rock does. The company additionally argues that biogenic growth (oysters, barnacles, coralline algae cementing onto the unit) provides "bioprotection" — added structural strength/stability and reduced maintenance need — though no independently measured structural comparison (e.g. load testing, storm-damage comparison) between ECOncrete and standard units at an Israeli site was found in this pass [R1][R2].

One relevant independent caution: a 2025 peer-reviewed methodology paper on comparative biodiversity assessment for "greening the gray" coastal infrastructure — co-authored by three ECOncrete Inc. staff and one Tel Aviv University researcher (i.e., not fully independent of the company) — cautions that relying solely on species-richness/diversity metrics risks "oversimplifying complex ecological dynamics" and that "all diversity estimates are both scale-dependent and sampling effort-dependent," meaning headline biodiversity-multiplier claims (like the Haifa "double the species richness" figure) are sensitive to how and how long the comparison was sampled [R17]. That paper's own case study is a 12-month Port of Vigo, Spain deployment (EU Horizon 2020-funded), not an Israeli site, and it does not discuss the Haifa/Herzliya/Jaffa data specifically [R17].

## Unexpected results

The clearest unexpected finding across the sources reached is at **Herzliya**: the untouched control section of the existing marina seawall did not merely stay static as a biodiversity baseline — it measurably **declined below its own starting baseline** over the ~22-month study window, widening the apparent gap with the ECOncrete units beyond what a simple "before vs. after" comparison would suggest [R2]. At **Haifa**, the sharper-than-expected finding is the **reversal of the native/invasive species ratio** (roughly 2:1 native on ECOncrete vs. invasive-dominated on the standard antifers) — a specific compositional shift, not just a raw richness increase [R1][R2].

## Relevance to Israel / Haifa

All three sites (and the reported but undated naval-base, Ashdod, and Eilat installations) are already **in Israel**, so this is a same-country, same-wave-climate precedent rather than a foreign transfer case — which is unusual among this project's other targets. The direct relevance to a proposed ecological surf reef in Haifa is:

- **Direct precedent for bio-enhancing concrete on Haifa Bay infrastructure specifically**: the Polinom/Haifa Port pilot is, per the sources read, the same body of water (Haifa Bay) that a Haifa surf-reef proposal would sit in, and it already demonstrates that ECOncrete-style armoring units can be fabricated, installed, and monitored on Haifa Bay infrastructure with a measured ecological uplift (>2x invertebrate richness, native-species dominance) relative to plain concrete [R1][R2].
- **Tide range**: Israel's Mediterranean coast (including Haifa) is treated in Lior's own project files as effectively micro-tidal, which he notes as a structural stability/maintenance advantage relative to large-tidal-range ocean sites [R18]. No external numeric tidal-range source specific to Israel or Haifa was found in this research pass (a known gap already flagged in this project's own target list) [R18] — a dossier reader should not treat this as verified without a dedicated primary source (e.g. an Israeli tide-gauge or IOLR publication).
- **Wave climate**: not covered by any source in this pass; see this project's separate `israel-wave-climate-and-tides` dossier target (Shikmona buoy data) for Haifa-specific wave-height and swell-direction figures, which this ECOncrete-focused research did not re-derive.
- **What transfers**: the core transferable idea is using ECOncrete-style bio-enhancing armor units (rather than plain concrete or plain rock) as the *facing material* of a Haifa reef/breakwater structure — a way to get ecological uplift "for free" on a structure that would need armor units anyway, independent of whether the structure is shaped to also produce a surfable wave. The Haifa/Polinom case study specifically shows this works as a drop-in substitute for the same cube-shaped antifer units used in conventional Israeli port breakwater construction, which lowers the engineering-novelty risk of proposing it for a new Haifa structure [R1][R2].
- **What does not transfer / caution**: none of the three Israeli case studies measured wave-attenuation, surfability, or storm-structural performance — all measured ecology only. A Haifa surf-reef proposal would need separate hydrodynamic/structural design work; ECOncrete's own material addresses only the "what is the armor unit made of" question, not "what shape produces a rideable wave."
