## Motivation and who pushed for it

- **National sea-level/wave monitoring (IOLR, government mandate):** Israel's tide and wave monitoring network exists to support port operations, coastal engineering, and (increasingly) climate-driven sea-level-rise tracking. The 2001 review by Dov S. Rosen (IOLR) explicitly frames the effort as filling in a fragmented historical record — tide data had been gathered piecemeal since the 1920s by different authorities (Ports and Railways Authority, Meteorological Service, Survey of Israel, Geological Survey Institute, IOLR) with gaps, lost records, and moved benchmarks, and the Hadera GLOSS station (installed 1992, joined the UNESCO/IOC Global Sea-Level Observing System in 1994) was built specifically to get a "superior quality," offshore, continuously monitored reference free of surf-zone wave set-up contamination [R1].
- **Lior's own field study (project-specific, not institutional):** independently of any institutional coastal-engineering study, Lior conducted his own correlation exercise using the public Shikmona buoy feed (Jul–Aug 2025 readings) against two commercial surf-forecast services (Surfline/Magicseaweed and "4Surfers") to define an empirical "minimum surfable wave" threshold and matching sea-floor depth bands, specifically to size a candidate Haifa-area artificial reef. This is explicitly his own primary data-gathering exercise, not a citation of published science [R21].

## Design as planned

Not applicable in the conventional sense — there is no single "designed" structure here. Two separate efforts converge in this dossier:
1. IOLR's public buoy/tide-gauge network, designed as long-term public infrastructure (Hadera GLOSS station specifically sited 2.1 km offshore "beyond influence of wave induced sea-level set-up occurring in the surf zone") [R1].
2. Lior's field-measurement exercise (page 1 of his PDF), which defines a "minimum surfable wave" as Significant Wave Height 0.45–0.6 m / Maximal Wave Height 0.6–0.8 m, cross-checked against Surfline (0.6–0.9 m) and 4Surfers (0.65–0.8 m, "knee high"), with a target swell direction window of 285–294° [R21].

## What was actually built, and how it differed from the design

- The Hadera GLOSS station and the Shikmona buoy are both live and operating as designed; both still stream near-real-time data via the Isramar (isramar.ocean.org.il) portal at the time of this research [R6][R7].
- Lior's field exercise was carried out as planned across three date windows in Jul–Aug 2025 (his file is titled/dated 2025-08-20): 2025-07-27 to 2025-08-01, 2025-08-02 to 2025-08-07, and 2025-08-11 to 2025-08-15, with recorded buoy readouts of SWH 0.69 m / peak period 6.20 s / max wave height 0.88 m (2025-08-06 19:00 UTC) and SWH 0.81 m / peak period 5.70 s / max wave height 1.03 m (2025-08-15 09:00 UTC) [R21].
- **Source discrepancy — flagged per instructions:** the project's own master list (00_master_list.md) and the task brief for this dossier both state an "observed buoy peak SWH 0.98 m / max wave height 1.24 m" reading dated **2025-10-09** [R22]. Lior's underlying source PDF (01_source_notes/lior_suggestions_and_swell_heights.md), however, only documents three measurement windows through **2025-08-15**, with a maximum recorded value of SWH 0.81 m / max wave height 1.03 m — it does not contain an October reading. Both figures are given here; the October 1.24 m reading could not be independently verified against the underlying PDF content available to this research and may come from a later/different capture of the Shikmona buoy feed not included in the digitized source notes [R21][R22].

## Outcome

- **Design-basis figures produced:** minimum-surfable-wave threshold (SWH 0.45–0.6 m), target breaking-depth bands ("No Breaking" 1.45–1.7 m, "Wave Breaking Depth" 1.3–1.5 m, "Min Depth" 1.15–1.35 m), and a 2 m (±0.5 m) horizontal "until break" length — all derived by Lior from the Shikmona buoy cross-check [R21].
- **Tidal range for engineering purposes:** ~0.4 m spring / 0.15 m neap on the open Israeli Mediterranean coast, essentially uniform between Haifa, Ashdod and historically Jaffa (station benchmark differences estimated at no more than ~10 cm) [R1].
- **Sea-level extremes for structural design:** 100-year return high sea level ≈1.10 m above ILSD (excluding climate-driven sea-level rise, which Rosen (2001), citing Warrick and Oerlemans (1990), separately estimated at +18 cm by 2030 and +70 cm by 2100 globally) [R1].

## Why it worked / why it failed

Not applicable in a pass/fail sense — this is measurement infrastructure and a personal data-correlation exercise, not a structure that can succeed or fail physically. What can be assessed:
- The Hadera GLOSS approach (offshore, digital, continuous) is explicitly described by its own author as methodologically superior to the older harbor-based tide gauges it replaced, which suffered lost records, moved benchmarks, and manual-digitizing errors [R1].
- Lior's buoy/forecast cross-check approach is internally consistent (three forecast sources roughly agree on the same surfable-day windows) but is a small, short (three-week), single-season sample — not a multi-year climatology — so it characterizes a handful of borderline days rather than the full annual wave regime [R21].

## Unexpected results

- The Hebrew field annotation on Lior's pages 3–4 flags "yellow = borderline" days as surfable "only on the high(er) sets that arrived once every quarter hour" — i.e., even within his defined "surfable" SWH window, rideable waves at the Haifa site were intermittent rather than continuous, a real-world nuance a pure SWH threshold does not capture [R21].
- Historical sea-level records show Haifa and Ashdod can diverge sharply during the same storm: in the Dec 22–23, 1967 event studied by Rosen (2001), Ashdod (open coast) saw a large sea-level spike while Haifa Port (more sheltered, and subject to reducing effects from easterly winds in Nov–Dec) did not show the same spike — i.e. "the Israeli coast" is not one uniform storm-surge regime even though its astronomic tidal range is essentially uniform [R1].

## What could have been done better

- **Experts'/authors' view:** Rosen (2001) is explicit that pre-1990s Israeli sea-level data is compromised by moved benchmarks, unrecorded relocations (e.g. the Ashdod gauge moved at least twice between 1958–1984, with one move's exact date "lost"), and manual digitizing errors — the author's own recommendation, implicit throughout the report, is that only the newer digital, offshore, fixed-benchmark stations (Hadera GLOSS No. 80) should be trusted for datum-quality work [R1].
- **Surfers'/public view:** commercial/community sources describe Israeli — including Haifa — wave conditions as usable but modest: "the waves in Haifa are not super consistent and they lack the power of oceanic swells" [R13], and a general-Israel guide notes summer waves "average around 2-3ft" and are "smaller and less consistent," with winter (Nov–Apr) offering swells "up to 8ft" [R14]. From a design perspective, this public/surfer framing (small, seasonal, wind-swell-dominated) is broadly consistent with surfing4all.co.il's stated typical Israeli beach wave-height range of 30–200 cm [R16] and reinforces Lior's own choice to define a narrow, low "minimum surfable" threshold rather than assume ocean-scale swell. (An earlier version of this claim cited a published "mean SWH of 0.5–1.0 m" to [R2]; that figure was found on verification to describe Abu Qir, Egypt, not Israel, and has been removed — see Quick facts.)

## Relevance to Israel / Haifa

- **Tide range at the site:** ~0.4 m spring / 0.15 m neap at Haifa and along the whole Israeli Mediterranean coast — essentially uniform with Ashdod and historically Jaffa (bench-mark differences ≤~10 cm) [R1]. This is the smallest tidal range among all comparator sites researched for this project:
  - Gold Coast, Australia (Gold Coast Seaway): spring tide range ≈1.3 m (live tide-forecast data for the week of 25–29 Sep 2026 shows a range of 1.05–1.44 m across successive spring-tide days; an earlier "≈1.7 m" figure here compared a single high-tide *height*, not a high-minus-low *range*, and has been corrected) [R8]
  - Bournemouth, England: spring tide range ≈2.0 m (computed directly from listed high/low pairs: 2.43 m high − 0.40 m low on the 28 Sep spring-tide day) [R9]
  - Perth, Australia: spring tide range ≈0.7 m [R10]
  - Mount Maunganui / Tauranga Harbour, New Zealand: tidal range up to ≈1.5 m in the current forecast window (highest high 1.74 m minus lowest low 0.08 m over a 10-day window); an earlier "≈1.98 m" figure here could not be reproduced from this source and has been corrected [R11]
  - For general context, Wikipedia's "Tidal range" article lists the Mediterranean Sea (along with the Baltic and Caribbean) among the regions with the world's smallest tidal ranges, and the Bay of Fundy (11.7 m), Ungava Bay (9.75 m) and Bristol Channel (9.60 m) among the world's largest — consistent with Israel's ~0.4 m figure sitting at the low end of the global range. (An earlier version of this bullet cited an Australia-specific "<1 m southwest to >8 m northwest" figure to this same article; that figure does not appear on the page and has been removed.) [R12]
  - **What transfers:** an Israeli reef (or any fixed-crest structure) designed around the Shikmona depth bands does not need to account for the multi-meter waterline swings that Gold Coast, Bournemouth or Mount Maunganui reef/groyne designs must accommodate — the whole "No Breaking / Wave Breaking Depth / Min Depth" band stack Lior measured (1.15–1.7 m total spread) already dwarfs the ~0.25 m difference between Israel's own spring and neap tides, meaning tide is a second-order design variable in Israel where it is a first-order one at the ocean-coast comparator sites.
- **Wave climate:** no published mean-SWH or dominant-direction figure specifically for the Israeli coast was found on verification (see Quick facts); the only direct measurement is Lior's own Shikmona-buoy readings clustered at 284–294° (WNW) [R21], consistent with the qualitative "small, wind-swell-dominated, not ocean-scale" picture given by surfer-community sources [R13][R14b][R16] — reinforcing why Lior's low (0.45–0.8 m) "minimum surfable" threshold is appropriate for Haifa specifically rather than a threshold borrowed from an ocean-swell site.
- **Storms:** individual storm waves can exceed 5 m significant height with periods to 15 s (Carmel, Inman & Golik 1985) [R4], and a Gumbel fit to an 18-year Ashdod dataset gives a 100-year deep-water significant wave height of ≈8.70 m (Rosen, undated) [R2] — these, not the tidal range, are the governing extreme-load case for any permanent Israeli coastal structure.
