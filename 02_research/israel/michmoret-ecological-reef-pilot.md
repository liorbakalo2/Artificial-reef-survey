## Motivation and who pushed for it

MCCP was established in 2013 to address the collapse of Israel's ~45 km of soft kurkar (sandstone) cliffs out of ~190 km of Mediterranean coastline, concentrated between Hadera and Ashkelon [R11]. The Michmoret pilot was led by MCCP CEO **Ilan Lavi** (אילן לביא), quoted describing it as "a sustainable environmental solution built to hold up over time" that would allow marine protection of the cliff "even in environmentally sensitive areas" [R2][R3]. The Israeli marine-environment NGO **Zalul** has separately and publicly advocated for submerged/ecological artificial reefs (combined with sand nourishment) as a preferable alternative to conventional rubble-mound breakwaters — which block longshore sand transport and cause downdrift erosion — and names "C-Cell" technology by name as a candidate in its own position paper, though Zalul frames this as a recommendation for prototype testing rather than a description of an operating project it is affiliated with [R10]. This is Lior's-files-adjacent context, not a direct claim that Zalul initiated the Michmoret pilot. Per Lior's own source notes (a conference-talk transcript, not independently confirmed against MCCP/CCell material), MCCP's Head of Projects, **Yonatan Bruzin** (יונתן ברוזין), is himself a surfer based in Herzliya — relevant as an internal advocate profile, though no source ties him specifically to the Michmoret decision [R17].

## Design as planned

Per MCCP's own announcement and follow-up pages: three submerged, electrified metal-mesh structures were to be installed at Michmoret Marina, each carrying a continuous low-voltage current that mineralizes dissolved seawater calcium carbonate and magnesium onto the mesh, forming a limestone-like accretion roughly 100× faster than unassisted natural reef formation [R1][R2]. The system was to be digitally monitored and controlled remotely from England [R2]. MCCP's initial plan called for an assessment window of roughly 3–4 months to test durability and effectiveness and to refine modeling, ahead of a possible wider rollout "the following year" (i.e., 2023) [R1]. CCell's own generic marketing material (not Israel-specific) claims up to 97% wave-energy absorption for its reef units [R2 paraphrase of CCell spec].

**Unresolved identity question, flagged rather than resolved:** a conference-talk transcript in Lior's files (source: 01_source_notes/transcript_goldcoast_reef_company.md) describes an unnamed Israeli installation by the same company (transcribed "Seaell," almost certainly CCell) as its **largest reef built to date** at the time of the talk, using a **willow skeleton** (bamboo being unavailable in Israel) in the top two of three vertical tiers, with the bottom tier deliberately left open for fish passage and scour control, built "with MCC support" [R16]. No MCCP or CCell material found in this research confirms willow, three vertical tiers, or a "largest to date" claim for Michmoret specifically — nor do the MCCP/Maariv Michmoret articles mention a skeleton material at all (their described technology is bare electrified metal mesh, not a willow/bamboo armature). It is plausible "MCC" = MCCP and this is the same Michmoret project described from CCell's side with additional engineering detail omitted from the Israeli press coverage; it is also possible this is a separate, later or unpublicized Israeli installation. Treat as unconfirmed.

## Unexpected results

The clearest finding from this research is an **absence, not a result**: MCCP publicized the pilot three times in six months (25 Jul 2022, 28 Dec 2022, 8 Jan 2023) [R4], explicitly promising a post-pilot report on durability and effectiveness before wider rollout [R1] — and then the project disappears from public record. It is not listed among MCCP's current named projects (Netanya, Herzliya, Ashkelon, Bat Yam) on the company's own Hebrew Wikipedia summary [R11], and it does not appear among CCell's own current research partnerships or project portfolio, which list Bath University, Cornell, Imperial College, the Living Reefs Foundation (Bermuda), Exmouth (UK), and multiple Mexico sites, but not Israel [R6][R7]. A February 2026 Times of Israel investigative piece on Israel's cliff-protection funding crisis mentions MCCP "testing artificial reefs in Netanya" with EU funding — describing "metal infrastructure already in the water" connected to electricity "with the hope that it will be colonized within a few months" — which sounds technologically identical to the Michmoret approach but is attributed to a different town (Netanya) and a different funder (EU, not the UK/CCell collaboration) [R9]. This may be (a) the same technology redeployed/rebranded at a second site, (b) a journalist's imprecise shorthand for the Michmoret project, or (c) a genuinely separate initiative — not resolved here.

## Relevance to Israel / Haifa

**Tide range:** not found as a site-specific figure for Michmoret in this research; Lior's own project notes (00_master_list.md) flag Israel's Mediterranean tidal range generally as small enough to be treated as a structural-stability/maintenance advantage for reef-type coastal structures versus large-tidal-swing ocean coastlines, but this framing is Lior's own working assumption, not an independently sourced number, and remains an open gap per his own notes. **Wave climate:** Lior's source notes (from the Shikmona Buoy, Haifa, via IsraMar/IOLR) give a working "surfable" threshold of significant wave height 0.45–0.6 m, max wave height 0.6–0.8 m, with dominant swell direction clustered 284–300° (WNW) [R17] — this is Haifa-area data, not Michmoret-specific, but is the best local wave-climate baseline in Lior's files and the natural comparison point for any Haifa deployment of this technology. **What transfers:** the core technology (low-voltage electrified mesh, remote UK monitoring, claimed fast mineral accretion, low cost relative to rock/geotube structures) is presented by CCell as broadly site-agnostic and is a plausible fit for Haifa's kurkar-cliff sections given the shared coastal-cliff geology with Michmoret. However, the total absence of a published outcome from an 18-months-and-counting-old, publicly announced Israeli pilot is a direct and material caution for anyone (including Lior) considering citing this technology as de-risked for a Haifa proposal — it should be treated as unproven at the Israeli-coast scale until MCCP's own results (if they exist) are obtained directly.
