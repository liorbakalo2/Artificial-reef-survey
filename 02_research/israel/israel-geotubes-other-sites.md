# Other Geotube / Sand-Container Installations on the Israeli Coast — Israel

## Quick facts

This target covers *every documented geotube/sand-container structure on the Israeli Mediterranean coast besides the well-documented Ashkelon North project* (Ashkelon has its own dossier, `israel/ashkelon-geotubes-2018.md`). Research found **no second fully-built geotube coastal-protection structure** anywhere else on the Israeli coast. What exists instead is: one large **planned-but-redesigned** geotube (Tel Aviv, Hof HaTzuk), a **permitted-but-unbuilt** geotube option inside the Netanya cliff-protection master plan (built instead as rock breakwaters), and one **not-yet-deployed pilot technology** ("armored geotube") being tested by the national cliff-protection company. ## Motivation and who pushed for it

- **Tel Aviv (Hof HaTzuk):** The municipality, not the national Government Company for Protection of Mediterranean Sea Cliffs, drove this project — the State Comptroller's 2024 report explicitly separates it out as "activity at Hof HaTzuk carried out by Tel Aviv-Yafo Municipality" [R5]. In 2019 the municipality's Roads & Lighting director Tzfrir Kesler told a Knesset committee the city was "promoting a geotube at the northern cliff beach, currently in tender process," describing it as a design improvement on Ashkelon's ("in deeper water, further from shore") [R6]. The municipality justified it by citing worsening winter-storm damage to the cliff and beach facilities, and pointed to "successful French experience" with submerged geotubes [R1][R2][R3].
- **Opposition** was led by two named surfers: **Adi Gluska**, a 12-time Israeli surfing champion, and **attorney Eyal Szmulewitz** (spelled Shmulovich/Shmolevitz across outlets), described as a 30-year surfer, who organized an estimated 200–300-person protest on 4 October 2019 and threatened to petition Israel's High Court of Justice (Bagatz) if the project proceeded [R1][R2][R3]. The marine-environment NGO **Zalul** joined the protest and separately submitted a formal position paper to the Knesset's Interior and Environmental Protection Committee opposing breakwaters/geotubes as the default coastal-protection solution, at Hof HaTzuk and elsewhere [R7].
- **Netanya:** the geotube option there was written into the 2017 TAMA framework by the national Government Company for Protection of Mediterranean Sea Cliffs and the Vaadat Lochof (national coastal-environment planning committee) as one of several approved sea-protection tools for Netanya's cliff sections, alongside rock breakwaters, sand nourishment and rock placement [R5]. In practice the company (led at the time by CEO Hila Arnreich, who described the scheme to the Knesset as Israel's largest breakwater project) chose the rock-breakwater route, not geotube [R6][R7][R8].
- **The "armored geotube" pilot** was pushed internally by the Government Company for Protection of Mediterranean Sea Cliffs itself, seeking a durability fix after watching plain geotextile geotubes fail repeatedly at Ashkelon; the company requested and received a dedicated NIS 2.45M innovation budget from its steering committee in January 2022 to test it and related technologies [R5].

## Design as planned

- **Tel Aviv:** a 450 m-long (extendable to 900 m–1 km) submerged geotextile tube, sunk 3 m below the surface, 250 m offshore — deliberately sited farther offshore and in deeper water than Ashkelon's geotube, according to the municipality, specifically to reduce the kind of failure Ashkelon experienced [R1][R2][R3][R6].
- **Netanya:** TAMA 9/13 (approved by the Vaadat Lochof, Nov 2016 decision cited for the equivalent Ashkelon section) permitted, for qualifying cliff sections, a menu of: rock breakwaters; marine geotube; additional rock placement in the sea; and beach widening via sand feeding [R5].
- **Armored geotube:** a standard geotextile sleeve (as used at Ashkelon) but woven through with metal conductors; a low-voltage current is applied to the conductors so that dissolved minerals in seawater precipitate onto the sleeve's outer face, building a rigid mineral shell meant to resist tears, punctures and abrasion far longer than untreated fabric [R5].

## What was actually built, and how it differed from the design

- **Tel Aviv:** nothing was built to the 2019 geotube design. The tender process described by the municipality in 2019 [R6] did not result in construction before the 2019 protest and subsequent scrutiny; by the time of the State Comptroller's 2024 follow-up audit, the municipality's own written response (March 2024) stated it was pursuing a stone/rock breakwater instead, under TAMA 13/9 — a complete change of structure type, not merely a redesign of the geotube [R5]. No geotube has been placed at Hof HaTzuk as of the sources found in this research.
- **Netanya:** none of the geotube option was exercised. As of the State Comptroller's most recent audit, the company had built six rock breakwaters along Netanya's northern beaches (with a further six planned for the southern section, not yet under construction) plus sand nourishment — no marine geotube [R5][R8]. A separate 2016-era company presentation cited in Lior's notes and in general web reporting mentions geotube being used as a general company technique, but no source found here documents an actual geotube unit sunk off Netanya [R9].
- **"Armored geotube":** stayed at the design/lab-trial stage. No field installation in the sea is documented in the sources found; the company was still awaiting planning/environmental sign-off (from Israel's Ministry of Environmental Protection and the relevant statutory planning bodies) as of the report's cutoff (December 2023) [R5].

## Outcome

- **Surfing quality:** N/A for Tel Aviv — because the geotube was never built, Hof HaTzuk's surf break was never physically altered by it. Surfers' objection was pre-emptive: they argued a 450 m submerged structure reducing wave energy by "25 to 50 percent" (per the Times of Israel's characterization of the plan) would have ended surfing at one of central Israel's most-used breaks and closed three surf schools [R1].
- **Coastal protection:** Not measurable for Tel Aviv (nothing built) or for the Netanya geotube option (not exercised — Netanya's actual rock breakwaters are covered in the separate Netanya dossier). The "armored geotube" has no field performance data yet; its only documented test is the sister electro-accretion technology (metal mesh, not sleeve) trialled at Michmoret marina, which the Comptroller's report says produced "significant calcium carbonate accumulation on the meshes submerged near the marina quay within a few weeks" [R5] (see the separate Michmoret dossier for full detail on that pilot).
- **Ecology / longevity / damage / removal:** Not applicable — no geotube exists at any of these sites to assess.

## Why it worked / why it failed

Nothing here "worked" or "failed" in a structural sense, because nothing beyond Ashkelon was built. The relevant causal story is political/administrative:
- Tel Aviv's plan **failed to proceed** because of sustained, well-organized opposition from named surfers and an environmental NGO, combined with the municipality's own admitted lack of a completed environmental-impact assessment at the time of the 2019 protest [R1][R2][R3], and — per Zalul's Knesset submission — the broader argument that "the number of drowning victims on Israel's beaches each year is many times greater than the number of victims from coastal-cliff collapse," used to argue breakwaters/geotubes create a bigger safety problem than they solve [R7]. The State Comptroller's 2024 report shows the city ultimately abandoned the geotube approach in favor of a rock breakwater, without the report stating why rock was chosen over geotube specifically [R5].
- Netanya's national protection company **chose rock over geotube** in practice, even though geotube was a permitted option under its own master plan; no source found here states the company's explicit reasoning for that choice at Netanya, though the general pattern across the company's other reporting (see Ashkelon dossier) is that it came to see plain geotextile geotube as fragile and prone to storm/propeller/anchor damage, favoring engineered rock breakwaters for permanent (Phase B) works while reserving geotube for cheap, fast, "temporary/urgent" first-response protection [R5].
- The armored-geotube pilot **has not yet been tested at sea**, so no success/failure verdict exists; the Ministry of Environmental Protection's May 2023 response flagged it needed more environmental/planning detail (how the power cable would be laid, reversibility of the installation, monitoring plan) before it could be deployed [R5].

## Unexpected results

- The most notable "unexpected result" in this whole file is a **negative** one: despite Lior's original note referencing "planned geotubes facing Hof HaTzuk" with no further detail, that site turned out to be a *separate, distinct* case from Ashkelon (not a restatement of the same project, resolving the open question in his notes) — a genuinely different Tel Aviv-area project that was proposed, fought over publicly, and ultimately **not built as a geotube at all** [R1][R2][R3][R5][R6].
- The armored-geotube concept effectively imports the CCell/Michmoret electro-mineral-accretion principle (see separate dossier) into a *geotube sleeve* rather than a rigid mesh frame — i.e., Israel's cliff-protection company is trying to fix geotube's core weakness (fabric fragility) by borrowing from the ecological-reef technology being piloted at Michmoret, rather than switching to rock as Ashkelon and Netanya both eventually did for permanent works [R5].

## What could have been done better

- **Government/company side (per the State Comptroller):** the report criticizes the company generally (not this file's sites specifically, but the same institutional pattern) for pushing forward with sand nourishment and emergency works without first securing or repairing the sea protection meant to make that nourishment durable, and for proposing innovative technologies to its steering committee (including presumably the armored geotube) without first answering the Ministry of Environmental Protection's environmental and planning questions [R5].
- **Surfers'/public view (Tel Aviv):** Adi Gluska and Eyal Szmulewitz argued the "better" option was simply not to build any submerged breakwater/geotube in an area municipal officials themselves, per the protesters, could not show was on the government's own list of at-risk cliff sections — i.e., their view was that the intervention itself was unjustified for that specific beach, not that a different design would have been preferable [R2]. Zalul's institutional view is closer to Lior's own hypothesis in his notes (steep seabed transitions destroying fragile geotube fabric): the NGO recommends soft/bioengineered alternatives (vegetated slope stabilization, controlled managed retreat/demolition in limited urban spots, submerged reefs paired with direct sand replenishment, and porous bioengineered structures) over both geotube and traditional rock breakwaters [R7].

## Relevance to Israel / Haifa

- **Tide range:** Israel's Mediterranean coast (including Tel Aviv and the Haifa/Michmoret area referenced above) is effectively micro-tidal — astronomic tidal range on the order of tens of centimetres, far smaller than the multi-metre tidal ranges at many overseas geotube/surf-reef sites in the world-reefs list (e.g. UK, NZ). This is treated in Lior's own notes as a structural-stability advantage for any Israeli reef/geotube design versus large-tidal-swing ocean sites, though this file did not locate an external general Mediterranean tidal-range reference beyond that framing (flagged previously in `01_targets.md` as project Gap #6 — still open) [R9].
- **Wave climate:** the same general Israeli Mediterranean wave climate used elsewhere in this project applies (Shikmona buoy baseline: minimum surfable SWH ≈0.45–0.6 m, observed peak SWH ≈0.98–1.24 m in the dataset cited in `01_targets.md`) [R9]. Both failed/stalled cases in this file (Tel Aviv, and the implicit Ashkelon comparison) involve the same storm-driven wave-breaking dynamic Lior hypothesizes as the root failure mode for fragile geotextile structures placed where the seabed transitions sharply in depth [R9].
- **What transfers to Haifa:** the clearest transferable lesson from this file is procedural rather than technical — a coastal municipality (Tel Aviv) proposing a submerged geotextile structure without a completed public environmental/safety case attracted immediate, well-organized surfer and NGO opposition that stopped the project; and Israel's national cliff-protection company itself moved away from plain geotextile geotube toward rock breakwaters for permanent works after repeated fabric failures (Ashkelon), while still researching a hardened ("armored") geotube variant as a possible future fix. Any Haifa-area proposal invoking geotube technology should expect the same public-consultation and durability scrutiny, and could look to the SECURE Ltd armored-geotube concept as the most current Israeli attempt to solve geotube's core weakness [R5][R6][R7].
