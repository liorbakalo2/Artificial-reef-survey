# Discovery sweep — Academic literature angle

Search angle: Google Scholar / ResearchGate / journals (Coastal Engineering, JMSE, Ocean & Coastal Management,
Coastal Engineering Proceedings, Journal of Coastal Research), theses (Delft, Waikato, Griffith), review papers on
"artificial surfing reefs" / "multipurpose reefs" / "multi-functional artificial reefs" / "submerged breakwaters for
surfing", plus the Raised Water Research site and coastalmanagement.com.au (ICM), which several academic reviews
themselves cite as the industry's project registry.

Method note: WebSearch and WebFetch (page-summarizing) tools were used; no dedicated browser session (agents run in
parallel and share the pane, per instructions). Every URL below is one actually returned by a search or fetched
directly — none invented. Some numeric details (cost, exact year) come from a fetch-tool's summary of a source page
rather than my own reading of the raw HTML; where a figure looks fetch-summarized rather than page-verbatim it is
flagged "(per site summary)". A dedicated writer agent should re-fetch the flagged URLs to pull exact wording before
building the [R#] reference block for each dossier.

Cross-reference: read `02_research/00_master_list.md` first. Items already listed there (Narrowneck, Cables Reef,
Pratte's Reef, Mount Maunganui, Young-Jin Korea, Yucatan Mexico, Al Aqah UAE, Sigandu Indonesia, Living Breakwaters
Staten Island, Boscombe, Ashkelon, Portuguese-coast reef [Voorde 2009], Mediterranean-coast reef [López 2016]) are
**not** repeated as new candidate rows below unless this sweep found a materially new fact (e.g. resolving the
Voorde 2009 Portugal site to Leirosa, or finding Pratte's Reef's alternate name "Chevron Reef"). Those resolving
facts are noted in "Notes on existing master-list items" at the end instead.

## Notes on existing master-list items (resolved/enriched, not new rows)

- **`prattes-reef-el-segundo`**: confirmed alternate/official name **Chevron Reef**; official 1998 California Coastal Commission permit language; funded partly by a $300,000 Chevron contribution; built 2000–2001; removed per permit requirement, sources disagree on exact removal year (2008 vs. 2009/2010 across different pages — needs one more check to pin down).
- **`cables-reef-wa`**: now datable and locatable — Cable Station Reef, Perth/Leighton area, Western Australia, built 1998–1999, granite boulders on limestone.
- **`gold-coast-second-gen-rock-reef`** (currently unnamed in master list): very likely **Palm Beach Reef**, Palm Beach, Gold Coast, built 2018–2019, quarried rock. Needs a confirming read of the Raised Water Research Palm Beach page against the ICM transcript details already in the master list (cost, "harder surface" complaint) before merging.
- **`portuguese-coast-reef-voorde-2009`**: very likely the **Leirosa** multifunctional reef, south of Figueira da Foz — dune-recovery works from 2000, later modeled/studied 2009–2012 by Ten Voorde, Antunes do Carmo and Neves (the same author group as the bare citation already in the master list).
- **`mediterranean-coast-reef-lopez-2016`**: site identified as **Babilonia beach, Guardamar del Segura, Alicante, Spain** — but this sweep could not confirm whether it was actually built or remains a design study; flag for a dedicated check.
- **`boscombe-surf-reef-uk`**: additional detail found — reopened/rebranded as a "multi-purpose reef" in 2017 after early failure (per coastalmanagement.com.au summary); should be cross-checked against Lior's own note, which says it broke apart ~1.5 years after construction from a boat-propeller strike.
- **`mount-maunganui-reef`**: additional detail found — removed in 2014 per coastalmanagement.com.au; caused hazardous rip currents; achieved ~20 m beach widening despite surf-performance failure. Needs primary-source confirmation (these specifics came from a fetch-tool summary of a secondary page, not a primary document).
