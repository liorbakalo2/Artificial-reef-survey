## Motivation and who pushed for it

The only source found is International Coastal Management's own case-study page, which frames the project as commissioned work for a private client: it names "Waagner Biro Gulf LLC / RTA" as the client and states the goal was "to stabilise the shoreline and protect the road while creating a safe, usable lagoon" for what the companion Safi-fish case study (a separate, later project at the same harbour — see below) calls "a private residence" [R1][R2]. No politics, tourism board, or public-erosion-crisis narrative is described — this reads as a private landowner/developer commission, not a government or community-driven coastal-defence programme, though no independent (non-ICM) source confirms or adds detail to this framing (unreferenced — needs source for who specifically initiated/paid for the project).

## Design as planned

ICM's own summary is the only design description found: "an integrated coastal engineering approach using rock structures," combining "a series of rock groynes and breakwaters, dredging of a lagoon and access channel (including very hard rock zones), revetment walls, a marina pontoon and jet-ski berths with a timber walkway" [R1]. ICM lists its scope of services as site surveying, coastal-process analysis, structural design, dredging design, pontoon-installation supervision, construction staging, and maintenance guidance [R1]. No design report, wave-climate model, or beach-width target was found — the page gives outcome language ("stabilise," "protect," "safe, usable lagoon") rather than a quantified design brief (unreferenced — needs source for any quantified design targets).

## Outcome

ICM states the work "stabilised the coast, created an area for swimming, and a small-craft berthing for private use," converting the site into "a protected lagoon with safe access and improved amenity" [R1]. No surf-quality data (this was not a surf project), no measured shoreline-accretion or erosion-control figures, no ecological survey, and no storm-damage or longevity record post-2008 were found in any source reached. A separate, later ICM/EcoCoast project at the same harbour — 52 "Eco-Mat" geotextile reef modules installed in 2012 to help restock the endangered Safi fish — reports that "post-installation monitoring confirmed the modules successfully fostered algae growth and provided protective habitats for juvenile Safi fish, contributing to population recovery" [R2]. That is evidence the harbour created by the 2008 works was still an active, maintained site four years later, but it is a distinct intervention with its own purpose (ecological restocking, not coastal protection) and should not be read as a performance measurement of the 2008 coastal works themselves.

## Unexpected results

The clearest "unexpected"-adjacent fact found is that the same harbour needed a dedicated ecological intervention four years later: EcoCoast and ICM installed 52 geotextile "Eco-Mat" reef modules in the Al Yasat Ali Island harbour in 2012 specifically because the endangered Safi fish was suffering "low survival rates during restocking," and the modules were designed to grow algae as a food source and give juvenile fish shelter [R2]. This shows the 2008 coastal-protection works (revetments, dredged lagoon/channel, marina pontoon) were not designed with fish-habitat outcomes in mind and needed a follow-up ecological retrofit — but no source states this explicitly as a causal link, so it is presented here as a juxtaposition of two ICM projects at the same site, not a confirmed cause-and-effect finding (unreferenced — needs source for an explicit causal statement).

## Relevance to Israel / Haifa

The UAE's Arabian Gulf coast (where Abu Dhabi and this project sit) is meso-tidal by regional standards: live tide-table data for Abu Dhabi shows daily highs typically around 1.0–1.7 m against lows of roughly 0.15–0.9 m, i.e. a typical tidal range on the order of 0.5–1.5 m, with spring tides reaching about 1.7 m high [R3]. This is clearly larger than Israel's Mediterranean coast, which is effectively micro-tidal (well under 0.5 m, per Lior's other project files, not re-derived here). That gap matters for transferability: any crest depth, revetment height, or lagoon-entrance design calibrated to a ~1–1.5 m Gulf tidal envelope is not a like-for-like template for Haifa's much smaller tidal range — the same structure would sit more consistently exposed or submerged on Israel's coast. What plausibly transfers is the general design logic ICM describes elsewhere in Lior's notes for combining groynes, a dredged/protected lagoon, and revetment to convert an exposed shoreline into a stable, usable one [R4] — but no wave-climate data for this specific Al Yasat/Aali Island site was found, so no quantitative comparison to Haifa's wave conditions is possible (unreferenced — needs source for site-specific wave-height data).
