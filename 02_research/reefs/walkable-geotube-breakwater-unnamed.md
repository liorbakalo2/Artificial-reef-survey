## Candidate real-world matches (not confirmed — flagged for a future identification pass)

A prior research pass on this project already searched for this structure's identity and reported it "still unidentified" [R3]. Independently fetching ICM's own project pages for this dossier turned up two ICM-designed Queensland structures that match the transcript's stated logic (geotextile chosen over rock specifically for public accessibility) unusually closely. Neither is confirmed as the structure the presenter meant — the talk names neither — so both are reported here as leads, not as an identification:

- **Munna Point Groynes, Noosaville, Queensland, Australia** — seven low-crested groynes built from sand-filled geotextile "mattresses," completed 2014–2018, designed by ICM for Noosa Shire Council. ICM's own case-study page describes them as "accessible, user-friendly groynes" chosen to preserve public beach access at the adjacent Noosa River Holiday Park; cost and financing not stated on the page [R4].
- **Maroochydore Groynes and Seawall, Sunshine Coast, Queensland, Australia** — four groynes (50–100 m long, using 2.5 m³ sand-filled geotextile containers) plus a seawall, built 2001–2003 for Sunshine Coast Regional Council, designed by ICM [R5]. A 2017 condition assessment and community consultation (Maroochy River Estuary Consultation Group) reportedly concluded: "the decision was made to retain geotextile bags instead of switching to rock, prioritising user-friendliness and accessibility" — near-identical language to the transcript's "walk along it and access them" [R5][R6]. The project won the 2019 Australian Coastal Award for Community Engagement [R6]. Cost not stated on either ICM page.

Both are real, ICM-attributed, geotextile-over-rock, accessibility-motivated structures — but the transcript talk (recorded years after both were built) presents its "walkable geotube breakwater" example as one throwaway illustrative sentence with no name attached, so this dossier cannot state that either site is the one meant. A future agent with continued web-search budget should try to fetch ICM's full project archive and Queensland local-news coverage to look for a project described in the same terms and timeframe as the talk.

## Relevance to Israel / Haifa

The transferable point is the material-choice logic itself, not this specific unidentified site: ICM's stated rationale — choosing sand-filled geotextile over rock specifically so the public can walk on and access a coastal structure — is a direct, low-cost design lever also illustrated at Narrowneck (geotube surface described as "not a bad place to land" for surfers who fall on it) and, per the candidate matches above, at Munna Point and Maroochydore [R1][R4][R5][R6]. Israel's Mediterranean coast has a small tidal range, which Lior's own notes treat as a structural advantage for reef/structure stability and lower long-term maintenance versus large-tidal-range sites [R7] — a smaller tidal range also means a walkable geotube crest stays at a more consistent height/accessibility relative to the waterline year-round, compared with large-tidal-range sites where a "walkable" crest could be submerged or high-and-dry depending on the tide. No wave-climate or tidal data specific to this unnamed structure exists to compare directly (not stated).
