# Xala Reef (Costalegre artificial surf reef) — Costalegre, Jalisco, Mexico

*Slug: `mexico-reef-2026-unnamed`. Name/location resolved this sweep: the reef sits inside the **Xala** private resort/residential development on the **Costalegre** coast of **Jalisco, Mexico** (between Puerto Vallarta and Manzanillo). Raised Water Research's own image caption for the unveiling post reads "Mexico's first artificial surf reef at **Xala**" [R1]. No single source yet gives the reef itself a distinct proper name (e.g. "Xala Reef") — it is described as "Xala's... surf break" / "the reef at Xala."*

## Motivation and who pushed for it

The reef is one amenity inside Xala, a 3,000-acre low-density luxury development pitched explicitly as a "regenerative," conservation-branded alternative to conventional Mexican coastal resorts. Developer **Ricardo Santa Cruz** is quoted framing the whole project — reef included — around ecological restraint over density: *"Let nature be the protagonist... Now people are actually realizing it's more profitable to preserve"* [R3][R4]. His stated read of the target buyer (largely Californian tech-wealth) is that they explicitly don't want "Mom and Dad's country club resort," i.e. the reef/surf amenity is part of positioning Xala against older-style, non-surf luxury resorts [R3][R4]. Marine conservationist **Stefanie Brendl** (Sierra a Mar co-founder), interviewed for the same piece, frames the broader Costalegre restoration push in similar terms — *"Turning [overbuilt land] back is infinitely harder than doing it right in the first place. But that requires vision — and guts"* — though she is not quoted specifically about the reef [R3][R4]. On the design-industry side, Raised Water Research (an artificial-reef/wave-pool trade press site) frames the reef as newsworthy because of its designer's pedigree (Palm Beach Reef) and stages the public reveal as an industry event ("fireside conversation" covering "artificial reef science... and the future of reef projects along the California coast") rather than a local-community or government announcement [R1] — i.e., this is developer/industry-driven, not a municipal coastal-protection or erosion-mitigation project like most reefs in this project's master list.

## Design as planned

No detailed design brief (target wave height, period, break type, or beach-width promise) was found in sources located this sweep. What is documented:
- **Material choice**: quarried rock/boulders (3,000 truckloads), not geotextile sand-filled tubes [R3][R4] — this is consistent with Mortensen's stated design philosophy from his Palm Beach Reef interview: "every single reef using geo-textile has suffered problems with bags breaking, shifting or sinking," with removal costs sometimes exceeding placement costs, whereas rock is "natural and provides many small crevasses for marine habitat" [R12].
- **Stated design intent**: coastal protection/beach nourishment combined with a "consistently great wave," explicitly contrasted with reef designs that would "flatten" rather than "restore" the beach [R3][R4][R5] — language that echoes known failure modes of earlier reefs elsewhere (sand starvation/erosion changes) but no Xala-specific hydrodynamic modelling, wave-height target, or beach-width target was found.
- No public engineering report, environmental impact statement, or design paper for the Xala reef was located this sweep (unlike, e.g., the Narrowneck/Palm Beach reefs, which have published design papers — see `narrowneck-reef-gold-coast` dossier and R7 below for comparison).

## Why it worked / why it failed

No outcome data exists yet to assess this directly (see Outcome, above). The only available engineering signal is designer pedigree: Simon Mortensen led the design of Australia's **Palm Beach Reef** (Gold Coast; design phase completed 2013–2014, construction April–September 2019 [R6][R7] — corrected this sweep from an earlier "built 2018–2019" that Raised Water Research's own spot page does not support; ~$18.2M AUD / ~$12.5M USD, financed by the City of Gold Coast; 4–8 tonne rock boulders plus 470,000 m³ of beach-nourishment sand), which Raised Water Research assesses as a design success — "the reef appears to be a success," producing a consistent ~60 m right-hander and a shorter left in bigger swell, built from "lessons learned from past projects, to overcome the many problems that had limited their performance" [R7]. This is offered here as **background on the designer's method, not as evidence about Xala's own performance** — no source directly ties Palm Beach's outcome data to the Xala build.

## Relevance to Israel / Haifa

Tide range and local wave climate at the Xala/Costalegre site: **not found** in sources this sweep (no tide-gauge or swell-climate data located). What transfers conceptually, independent of Xala's own unverified outcome:
- **Material choice precedent**: the designer's shift toward quarried rock/boulder construction (as also used at Palm Beach Reef, 4–8 t boulders) rather than geotextile sand-filled tubes is directly comparable to the geotube-vs-rock materials question already live in this project's Israel dossiers (Ashkelon's 2018 geotube, per `jmse2023_bar_drimer_paper` notes) — a rock-reef alternative is one materials option Israeli planners could weigh against geotube failure modes (undersized tubes, sand burial) already documented for other sites in this project.
- **Governance model contrast**: Xala's reef is privately financed and privately accessed (members-only resort), unlike Israel's public-agency-funded structures (e.g. Ashkelon, Michmoret) — a useful contrast case for "who pays / who gets access" framing if this project's synthesis addresses financing models, but not a coastal-engineering transfer.
