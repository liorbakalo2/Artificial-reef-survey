## Notes / disambiguation flags for the dossier-writing pass

- **Michmoret (item 1)** is now a much richer dossier candidate: technology = CCell Renewables' mineral-accretion/electrochemical process (low-voltage current on submerged metal mesh precipitates CaCO₃/limestone ~100× faster than natural reef growth), previously tested in Mexico and England before Israel, started 2022-07-15, remotely monitored from England. Update the existing `michmoret-ecological-reef-pilot.md`/`.json` card rather than creating a new slug.
- **ECOncrete in Israel is more widespread than Lior's notes suggested**: not just "Herzliya Marina and a Haifa naval base" (per one WebSearch summary) but also a documented Haifa Port breakwater-armoring pilot (2013–2016) and the 2012 Haifa Bay "Polinom"/Bay Port construction. Sources conflict on whether the Haifa Port pilot and the Bay Port installation are the same site described two ways or genuinely distinct — a dossier author should fetch econcretetech.com's own case-study pages directly (only one was fetched this pass) to resolve this before writing final claims.
- **Haifa Port breakwater dimensions conflict across sources**: one Hebrew snippet said "600 m and 350 m," English Wikipedia-derived snippets said "~3,000 m main breakwater, 765 m lee breakwater, 1933 opening, dredged 1929+." Needs a primary-source read (Wikipedia "Port of Haifa" page or haifaport.co.il, which returned 403 to WebFetch this pass — try a different fetch method or the Wayback Machine).
- **Two distinct State Comptroller reports appear to exist** (items 21 and 22) — a ~2010 one (beach cleanliness/safety audit, cities inspected Apr–Dec 2009, ~700M NIS cliff-protection cost estimate for urban areas) and a 2024 one (cliff-collapse maritime/land protection gap audit, 8/13 km and 14/27 sections figures). These should NOT be merged into one row — confirm both exist independently via mevaker.gov.il before citing either.
- **Ashkelon sand-nourishment volume conflict**: one search snippet said "up to 70,000 m³" and the direct MCCP-page fetch said "approximately 50,000 m³" for what appears to be the same July–August 2024 episode. Flag as unresolved in the dossier; cite whichever number the primary mccp.co.il page states when re-fetched directly (WebFetch summarized rather than quoted verbatim).
- **Tel Aviv breakwaters (item 7)**: confirmed the general list and the Gordon Beach tombolo mechanism, but exact per-beach construction years were NOT found in this pass — the only dated source was a 2015 "maintenance procedure" document, not a construction record. Still a partial gap.
- **Nahariya, Caesarea**: searched specifically; Nahariya's "Gali Galil" beach has a breakwater-protected lagoon (modern, exact date not found — low priority, minor beach amenity). Caesarea's breakwaters are ancient Roman/Herodian-era (collapsed within ~100 years of construction) — historically fascinating but likely out of scope for a modern-structures list; flagged for the assembling agent to decide on inclusion.
- **SRF Park TLV (item 18)** and **Bat Yam artificial island (item 19)** are edge cases: neither is a sediment-retention/reef structure in the strict sense. Included because they surfaced repeatedly under "artificial reef/structure Israel" search terms and could be relevant to the "surfing-reef idea proposed in Israel" angle or as contrast points — final inclusion is an editorial call for whoever assembles the master list.
- **HaTzuk Beach disambiguation** (existing gap `hatzuk-beach-planned-geotube`): NOT resolved this pass — no new search targeted it specifically; still open.
- **Herzliya/Ashdod/Poleg sand-bypass mechanics**: no dedicated "sand bypassing system" (mechanical pumping plant, as opposed to one-off dredge-and-nourish episodes) was found for Herzliya or Poleg. The clearest true "bypass" (moving sand from the updrift to downdrift side of a fixed structure, recurring) found this pass is the **Ashdod Port programme**: ~180,000 m³/year moved from south of the breakwater to beaches north of it (search-snippet sourced; not yet fetched from a primary Israel Ports Company page — recommend a follow-up fetch of israports.co.il for exact figures/contract details).
