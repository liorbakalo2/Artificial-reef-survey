### What it means for the 3D model (nothing was rebuilt; `model.js`, `build_3d.py` and `index.html` are untouched)
- The model draws a rigid spherical-cap shell (base D 12 m, H 1.6-2.0 m, optional ramp asymmetry) on the 2009 lidar seabed. It carries no fill material, no mass and no wave behaviour, so none of the four claims changes its geometry, location or seabed; the seabed (2009 lidar) is 10 years older than the December 2019 install (9 years if Waveco's "2018" were right), which is no change in practice.
- Fill material: sand slurry is corroborated by independent sources and by Bottegal (row b). The reef should be described as a sand-ballasted bladder (sand slurry, water and air), not an "air-inflated dome"; ABC's "filled with air" is a simplification. No anchors or cables should be drawn: the bladder is described as self-ballasted by its sand.
- Mass: 140 t (Tracks 2021, Bunbury Mail 2022; both relay Bottegal) vs 150 t (Waveco page; forum summary of his Feb 2020 talk). Credibility check, my own arithmetic with assumptions, not from a source: spherical-cap volume with base radius 6 m, V = pi h (3 a^2 + h^2) / 6, is 92.6 m3 at H = 1.6 m and 117.3 m3 at H = 2.0 m (same formula as VERIFY.md Check 3). Beach sand, grain density 2.65 t/m3, porosity about 0.35-0.45 gives dry bulk about 1.5-1.7 t/m3 and saturated about 1.8-2.0 t/m3; using 1.6 (dry) and 2.0 (saturated) as bounds:

| Sand mass | Settled volume | Share of cap, H = 2.0 m (117.3 m3) | Share of cap, H = 1.6 m (92.6 m3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 140 t | 70-88 m3 | 60-75% | 76-94% |
| 150 t | 75-94 m3 | 64-80% | 81-101% |

  Both figures are physically credible, but they imply a bladder that is mostly sand-filled with only a modest air/water share, which fits Tracks 2021 ("partially inflated, sand-filled rubber dome"). They cannot tell 1.6 m from 2.0 m: at 150 t and dry-bulk density the sand alone would just fill a 1.6 m cap (about 100%), so either the density is higher, the dome is taller, or the round number includes water. The 7% gap between 140 and 150 t is smaller than the density uncertainty (about 25%), so it changes no model input.
- If 150 t is taken as true, the changes to the model's assumptions would be small and none were made: (1) add a text-only provenance row "fill: sand slurry, 140-150 t, developer-sourced"; (2) mean load on the seabed, 150 t over the 113 m2 footprint = about 1.3 t/m2 (about 13 kPa), is not used by the model and matters only for settlement/scour discussion; (3) inference, not sourced: a sand-heavy fill slumps to a flattened mound rather than a symmetric air dome, which fits the "skateboard ramp" profile (Tracks 2021) and the 1.6 m as-built height (Tracks 2019, Bunbury Mail) against the 2.0 m design height (ABC, RWR), so the 1.6-2.0 m slider and the optional ramp asymmetry should stay and the default symmetric cap should keep being labelled an estimate; (4) the viewer shows a finished dome under the water, but the as-built was about 90% installed, not fully anchored and torn in the back quadrant [R8]; a caption such as "geometry of the installed bladder, not a working reef" would avoid implying it ever operated. The wave claims (row c) are outside what the model represents.

(The SonarChart contours show a gentle seaward bulge of the 2.5-3.5 m lines near x = 0 to +30 m, y = 55-75 m; it is 0.5 m of relief over about 40 m, not a reef signature.)
- Method (nav_annotate.py; METHODS_3D.md 3.6): 9 shore-normal transects (alongshore offsets -120 ... +120 m) through the site; 0 m line = green/blue edge, then 0.5 m lines counted outward (17-18 lines per transect, monotonic and mutually consistent); each crossing is placed in the model frame and the lidar elevation read from the 5 m grid; implied datum D = z_AHD + d.
- Datum RMS test (valid lidar lines only: y >= 30 m, inside the grid, not gap-filled; RMS of lidar minus Navionics-implied elevation): all 9 transects (n = 111 lines) LAT -0.60 m AHD 0.32 m; MLLW -0.20 0.67; AHD (and MHLW) 0.00 0.87; MSL +0.10 0.97. Main transect (n = 12): LAT 0.35, MLLW 0.73, AHD 0.93, MSL 1.03. Free-fit datum -0.84 m AHD (sd 0.20 m over lines). => the Navionics depths are below LAT (chart datum), the Australian convention; MSL, AHD rejected, MLLW disfavoured (not excluded by the 0.48 m lidar uncertainty alone). Independent anchors: the 0 m Navionics contour lies at lidar -0.69 +- 0.19 m AHD over the 9 transects (main transect -0.59; surf-zone strip, lidar partly gap-filled, indicative; DoT index: LAT = AHD - 0.57 m); one Nautical spot sounding 3.5 m inside the lidar grid (x = 107 m, y = 76 m, lidar -4.02 m AHD) implies datum -0.52 m AHD; the Nautical 2 m and 5 m contours imply -0.78 and -0.69 m AHD.
- Residual: lidar is 0.24 m deeper than Navionics-on-LAT on average (bias -0.24 m) - inside the lidar vertical uncertainty (0.48 m) and the 10-year gap between the 2009 survey and the chart's undated sources.
- Annotated (private research copies): annotated/N1_navionics_sonarchart_contours_transects.png (transects, crossings, counts), N2_navionics_datum_rms_test.png (profile and RMS bars), N3_navionics_nautical_soundings.png (soundings and 2/5 m contours with implied datum), N4_navionics_reef_site_zoom_no_structure.png (no ring at the reef site).
