# METHODS_3D - Palm Beach Reef (Gold Coast, Queensland): three-dimensional model

Model files: `model.js`, `index.html`, `docs.js`; regenerated by `contours_s7.py`, `navionics_capture.py`, `navionics_isolines.py`, `build_3d.py` (which calls `write_provenance.py`), `annotate_3d.py`, `render_previews.py`. All numbers below are read from `validation_3d.json` (written by `build_3d.py`). Source log: `SOURCES_3D.md`. Items the author could not read: `REQUESTS_FOR_LIOR.md`.

## 1. Summary

The model shows the Palm Beach artificial reef (a submerged basalt/greenstone rock mound, 162 x 91 m, 11,989 m2 in the model grid, about 225-374 m off the beach at 19th Avenue) **as built in May-September 2019**; no later works, damage, loss or renewal were found (monitoring papers to 2023 report a stable structure; the rock is visible on Esri imagery of 2025-12-01), so no second state is modelled. The plan outline, the crest outline (59.2 x 5.9 m) and the crest level (-1.5 m below mean sea level) are sourced; the tide planes come from Maritime Safety Queensland. The seabed and the rock surface are **inferred**: the seabed from the Garmin Navionics SonarChart (read in a headless browser by the author; datum not stated, tested to be LAT), and the rock surface from the 10 unlabelled contour lines drawn on a Nearmap aerial of the finished reef (interval assumed 0.5 m, confirmed independently to within 10 % by the Navionics seabed). The two inferences agree to 0.27 m on average (standard deviation 0.55 m, 427 points) but no surveyed depth value was read. **confidence_3d = medium**: the geometry is good enough for camera-angle estimation (plan +-1-2 m, crest level +-0.2 m, seabed and reef height +-0.5 m), but the council's asset register (HEIGHT_M 5, VOLUME 33,000) describes a reef 20-33 % larger than the model (crest height 3.9 m, envelope 22,000 m3), and that gap is unresolved.

### 2.1 Chronology and state modelled

Concept design 2013-2015 (DHI; 175 x 144 m, 53,319 m3, offshore toe 560 m from shore in 11.4 m); detailed design (Royal HaskoningDHV) moved the reef about 50 m shoreward and reduced it to 160 x 80 m (Raised Water Research, 2019); construction April/May-September 2019 (Hall/Heron joint venture, 60,000 t, barged from the Port of Brisbane); engineering certification September 2019; later condition "comparable to the as-constructed certification" (EPW 2020); monitoring to 2023 stable (Prenzler et al. 2022). Searched for top-ups, repairs, extension, damage or removal and for the effect of cyclone Alfred (March 2025): none found; the dark mound is present on the Esri image of 2025-12-01. **The viewer shows the as-built state, with the caption "no later change found (searched 2026-10-05)".**

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Coordinate frame, handedness and datum

The model frame is the canonical frame of `shape.json`: metres, origin at the foot of the perpendicular from the reef centroid to the waterline, `x` alongshore toward bearing beta_x = 334.3 deg true (NNW), `y` offshore toward beta_y = 64.3 deg true (ENE), `z` up with z = 0 at mean sea level (MSL). Because beta_y = beta_x + 90 deg (clockwise on a north-up map) the triple (x, y, z up) is **left-handed**: drawing x to the right and y up the screen mirrors the reef. The viewer therefore converts to east-north-up, which is right-handed:

```
E = x sin(beta_x) + y sin(beta_y)        N = x cos(beta_x) + y cos(beta_y)        U = z          (E1)
x = ( E cos(beta_y) - N sin(beta_y) ) / sin(beta_y - beta_x)     (inverse; beta_y - beta_x = 90 deg)
```

and draws it with three.js axes (X = E, Y = U, Z = -N), so north is up on screen in the plan preset and offshore points to the right. **Check (build_3d.py, `frame_check`):** canonical vertex 0 = (-27.98, 373.92) m is converted with (E1) and an azimuthal-equidistant projection centred on the canonical origin (-28.108526, 153.468119) (Snyder 1987) to (-28.1072903, 153.4716713); `shape.json` `geo.polygons_latlon[0]` is (-28.1072904, 153.4716713). All 47 vertices agree to 0.024 m (mean 0.016 m); with x mirrored the mean error is 82.4 m. The earlier `geom.py make-canonical` rotation bug (fixed 2026-10-05) is not involved: the canonical block was built by an explicit rotation and re-verified in `VERIFY.md`.

Pixel to canonical metres (aerial s7): a least-squares similarity transform of the 47 traced outline vertices to `canonical.polygons_m`

```
c = s R (p - p_mean) + c_mean       s = 0.117647 m/px (= 1/8.50),  R = rotation by 115.70 deg (no reflection; image y points down),  max residual 0.007 m   (E2)
```

Navionics screenshots (Web-Mercator, 256 px tiles, 982 x 655 px map window centred on the reef centroid 28.107334 S, 153.470913 E): `X = 256 2^z (lon + 180)/360`, `Y = 256 2^z (0.5 - ln tan(pi/4 + lat/2)/(2 pi))` (E3); metres per pixel `2 pi R cos(lat) / (256 2^z)` = 0.527 m at z = 18, 1.054 m at z = 17. Pixel to lon/lat by the inverse of (E3), then lon/lat to canonical by the inverse of (E1) (`navionics_isolines.py`).

**Vertical datums.** Tide planes are heights above Queensland Port Datum (LAT 1992) at the Gold Coast Seaway; with MSL = 0.88 m above LAT:

```
z_MSL = z_LAT - 0.88            z_LAT = z_MSL + 0.88            depth below LAT d = -z_LAT = -(z_MSL + 0.88)           (E4)
LAT -0.88, MLWS -0.66, MLWN -0.37, MHWN +0.36, MHWS +0.65, HAT +1.15 m  (heights vs MSL)
z_MSL = z_AHD - (MSL - AHD)     with MSL - AHD = 0.88 - 0.76 = 0.12 m (AHD = LAT + 0.760 m at the Seaway standard-port benchmark, MSQ 2014 "Standard port datum levels", read at the source in `07_scale/bathymetry/gold_coast/REPORT.md` section 3 and confirmed 2026-10-10; it applies at the benchmark only, so +-0.1 m at Palm Beach)
```

The Navionics app states no datum; the chart depth `d_chart` is converted by `z_MSL = -d_chart - (MSL - CD)` where CD is the chart datum above LAT (CD = 0 for LAT; assumption A5, tested in 4.3).

### 3.2 Plan shape

Toe outline: the 47-vertex polygon of `shape.json` (traced from the aerial s7, IoU 0.995 with the City of Gold Coast polygon, area 11,972 m2; 11,989 m2 on the 1 m model grid). Crest outline: the 8-vertex innermost contour (59.2 x 5.9 m, axis 104.8 deg true, hand-read on 3x zoom grids; the concept design gives 60 m at 105 deg).

### 3.3 Contour lines of the aerial (reef surface input)

The aerial carries 10 thin grey lines inside the toe outline (`annotated/s7_contours_ranked_elevations.png`). They are not labelled. `contours_s7.py` traces them in two parts, both deterministic:

1. **Line filter.** Red channel, mean along a 31 px segment minus the mean of two parallel flanks at +-4.5 px, maximum over 30 orientations; lines respond 30-200, rock and foam texture < 25.
2. **Flank part (A).** Along the crest outline (smoothed 12 px, arc step 0.5 px) with outward normal `n(s)`, the response is resampled on `p(s, d) = B(s) + d n(s)`. Line k is the ridge `d_k(s)` that maximises (dynamic programming, periodic in `s`)
   `sum_s [ min(R, 60) - 30 - 15 (d_k - d_{k-1}) / l_loc ]  -  1.0 sum_s |change of spacing between neighbouring rays|` (change limited to 3 px per 0.5 px of arc),
   where `l_loc` is the local spacing of the two previous lines; the nearest-ridge prior makes a skipped line impossible, the smoothness term keeps the line smooth; fragments shorter than 16 m or with mean response < 30 are dropped; where the room left to the outline is < 1.4 x the local spacing the line has merged with the outline. Pieces whose slope exceeds 0.45 px/px (corner chamfers, foam streaks) and the 60 px nearest each end of the crest are dropped.
3. **End fans (B).** From the centre of each end of the crest outline, rays 0.25 deg apart over +-80 deg about the crest axis; on the axis ray the response peaks counted outward are lines 0, 1, 2, ... and the outermost peak is the toe outline itself (E end: 12 peaks = lines 0-10 plus the outline, 12.3 m between lines 0-3 and 6.0 m thereafter; W end: lines 1-5 plus the outline, 1.9-2.5 m); each line is followed ray by ray (nearest peak within 7 px of the prediction, up to 10 missed rays) so that it keeps its number. A and B parts of one line are separated by 7-15 m (`splice_check`); the model interpolates across the gap.
4. **Result.** 29 polylines (`annotated/s7_contours_ranked_elevations.png`): lines 1-5 close all round the crest, lines 6-10 peel off the outline toward the seaward end; the outline is line K + f with K = 4 ... 10.

### 3.4 Contour elevations and the reef surface

Assumption A3: the lines are at constant vertical interval `Delta` = 0.5 m and the crest outline is the crest (z_c = -1.5 m MSL):

```
z_k = z_c - Delta k                                                                                    (E5a)
z_toe = z_c - Delta (K + f),     f = min(1, d_K / l_K)                                                 (E5b)
```

where K is the number of traced lines inside the outline at that point, `d_K` the distance from the outline to the nearest line K and `l_K` the local spacing between lines K-1 and K (A4: the outline lies at most one interval below line K). Side slopes follow from the line spacing, `tan(beta) = Delta / l`: 6.00 m per line = 1:12 (north flank, outer east end), 2.47 m = 1:4.9 (south flank), 12.3 m = 1:25 (three lines near the crest at the east end), the concept figure's 1/12 and 1/5 (`annotated/s7_side_slopes_from_line_spacing.png`).

Reef surface: all line vertices (thinned to 1 m), the 427 toe-outline points (1 m apart) with their z from (E5b), and a 1 m fill of the crest slot at z_c form a point set; `z_s(x, y)` is the linear Delaunay interpolation of this set (Delaunay TIN, `scipy.interpolate.LinearNDInterpolator`; nearest-neighbour outside the convex hull). The surface is evaluated on a 1 m grid inside the toe outline.

### 3.5 Seabed from the Navionics chart (primary) and the toe elevations

*Access.* The Navionics ChartViewer (webapp.navionics.com) was decommissioned by Garmin in July 2024 and redirects to Garmin's Marine Maps viewer. In the author's own headless Chrome (OS-assigned random DevTools port, fresh profile in %TEMP%, process terminated by PID; never a shared browser) the page https://maps.garmin.com/en-US/marine opened without login, CAPTCHA or consent dialog; nothing was accepted or bypassed. The map was centred with the page's geohash key (r7j0h74ngmnv) and Leaflet `setView`; options: View = Map, Depth units = Meters, Chart type = SonarChart Maps or Nautical Charts, Seabed areas = Hide, Shallow shading = 0 to 10 m (`navionics_capture.py`; screenshots in `../src/navionics/`, 44 of the 80 captured; "Garmin Navionics, not for navigation", private research copy, reuse rights unchecked).

*Iso-depth lines.* With "shallow shading = v" the chart paints water shallower than v blue, so the edge of the blue area is the v-metre contour. Check: the printed contour labels 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 m lie 0.5-1.2 m from the extracted lines of the same value; the SonarChart prints a label every 0.5 m (contour interval 0.5 m; the app states no interval). The 1-10 m lines (zoom 17, 1 m steps) are extracted as the boundary of the blue mask (`navionics_isolines.py`, blue-minus-red > 9, closing 5 px) and converted to canonical metres with (E3).

*Seabed surface.* Points on the iso-lines more than 25 m outside the toe outline (A6; 1,041 points, every second boundary pixel), 25 waterline anchors (y = 0, z = 0 per A7) and, in the model's "hybrid" seabed, 107 toe-outline points (every 4th, z from E5b, smoothing 0.2) are interpolated with a thin-plate spline (Duchon 1977; `scipy.interpolate.RBFInterpolator`, kernel `r^2 ln r`, smoothing 1.0)

```
z_b(x, y) = -d_nav(x, y) - 0.88 ,      d_nav(x_i, y_i) = v_i + CD  (v_i = iso-line value, CD = 0 for LAT) ,   rms of the fit to the lines 0.08 m   (E6)
```

on a 4 m grid, x -300..300, y -40..620 m (`model.js`, `seabed.z_cm`). Seaward of the 10 m line (y about 503 m) the surface is an extrapolation. Landward of the waterline a visual beach face rises at 1:15 (not surveyed). Because the reef itself bends the 4-6 m contours seaward by 47-81 m (section 4.5), the chart seabed under and within 25 m of the reef is a smooth interpolation, not a measurement.

### 3.6 Reef thickness, volume and the rendered surface

```
h = max(0, z_s - z_b);      V = sum h dA over the toe outline (1 m cells);      zr = z_b + w h,  w = smoothstep(dist_to_outline / 2.5 m)        (E7)
```

`V` is the rock *envelope* (voids included), 22,020 m3 (21,966 m3 with the 2.5 m edge ramp). The viewer draws `zr` on a 1 m grid (+0.07 m visual lift against z-fighting), colour from rock grey, lighter toward the crest, faded into the seabed colour at the edge. Rock layers (core 300-1000 kg; armour 1-8 t, crest 6-8 t; EPW 2020) are not drawn separately: no thicknesses are published.

### 3.7 Water, grid, camera

The water surface is a flat plane at the selected level (LAT, MLWS, MLWN, MSL, MHWN, MHWS, HAT), translucent, with the datum labels of (E4). Metre grid 10 and 50 m, 10 and 50 m scale bars, north arrow, waterline y = 0. Vertical exaggeration `v` (1-5x, default 1x, always displayed) multiplies z only (`world.scale.y`); labels are counter-scaled so text is not distorted. Photo-match mode (local image as a semi-transparent overlay; sliders for x, y, height above MSL, heading, pitch, roll, vertical FOV; the 35 mm-equivalent focal length `f = 43.27 / (sqrt(1 + a^2) 2 tan(vfov/2))`, a = aspect ratio, is shown) forces v = 1; the copied JSON holds the camera in the canonical frame (x, y, z above MSL) with heading as a true bearing, pitch positive up.

## 4. Validation

**4.1 Frame.** Vertex check and mirror test in 3.1 (0.024 m; 82.4 m).

**4.2 Contour interval (independent test of A3).** The Navionics seabed at the toe outline `z_nav` is regressed on the number of intervals below the crest, `s = -sum[(z_nav + 1.5)(K + f)] / sum[(K + f)^2]` (E8, crest fixed): `s = 0.459 +- 0.003` m per interval (formal; the real uncertainty is larger because the seabed is not a function of the rank); by rank band 4-6: 0.65, 6-8: 0.51, 8-11: 0.43 m (the seabed is not exactly proportional to the rank, which is why the early ranks give larger values). The assumed 0.5 m is 9 % above the all-rank value, within the stated +-10 %. With 1.0 m the seaward toe would be at -12.5 m MSL, 5 m below the chart. The spacing-derived slopes 1:12 and 1:5 reproduce the concept figure labels (Mortensen et al. 2015, Fig 3), a second, independent argument for 0.5 m.

**4.3 Datum of the chart (test by RMS against the aerial-derived survey).** `RMS(o) = sqrt(mean[(z_toe - (z_nav + o))^2])` over 427 toe points, `o` = chart datum above LAT (m):

| chart datum | LAT (0) | MLWS (0.22) | MLWN (0.51) | AHD* (0.76) | MSL (0.88) | MHWS (1.53) | HAT (2.03) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RMS (m) | 0.61 | 0.74 | 0.96 | 1.17 | 1.28 | 1.88 | 2.37 |
| mean residual (m) | -0.27 | -0.49 | -0.78 | -1.03 | -1.15 | -1.80 | -2.30 |

LAT is best; MSL and AHD are rejected (> 1 m). The RMS minimum lies at o = -0.27 m (not resolvable against the 0.55 m scatter). Caveat: the "survey" here is the aerial's own contour lines under A3, so the test is not fully independent; it confirms that the two sources are consistent with a LAT-type datum, not that either is a survey. `annotated/nav_isolines_and_datum_test.png`.

**4.4 Toe elevation vs Navionics seabed.** Mean -0.27 m (the rock toe is deeper than the chart seabed), sd 0.55 m; by sector: SSE end -0.72 (sd 0.14), seaward end -0.51 (0.22), south flank -0.65 (0.26), NNW end and north flank +0.39 (0.59). The negative side may be scour or an apron, or the smoothing of the chart contours under the reef.

**4.5 Does the reef show in the chart?** In the SonarChart the 4, 5 and 6 m contours bend seaward by +65, +81 and +47 m relative to their chord between x = -250 and +250 m, and touch the toe outline (minimum distance 1.6, 0.4 and 9.3 m); no contour shallower than 4 m lies within 40 m of the toe and none closes over the crest. So the chart carries the reef's toe region but not its crest or height. `annotated/nav_sonar_z18_labels_read_with_reef_outline.png`.

**4.6 Crest depth vs the nautical chart.** The nautical chart labels the structure "FISH HAVEN 1.5MT" (annotated/nav_nautical_z18_fish_haven_label.png). The design crest is 1.5 m below MSL = 0.62 m below LAT. If the chart datum is LAT (A5, supported by 4.3) the chart puts the top 0.88 m deeper than the design; the figure 1.5 equals the design number, which suggests it was copied without a datum conversion. Not resolvable from the app; `REQUESTS_FOR_LIOR.md` A1, A2. The model uses the design value (four sources) and records this difference.

**4.7 Volume and height vs the council register (unresolved).** Model: crest height above the seabed 3.89 m (3.51 m with the Navionics-only seabed), maximum 4.17 m at (-8, 291) m, envelope volume 22,020 m3 (16,358 m3 Navionics-only). Register: HEIGHT_M 5 and VOLUME 33,000 (units and reference not stated; the layer has empty TOP/BASE_REDUCED_LEVEL fields). Other figures: 25,000 m3 "quarried rock" (International Coastal Management / Salyer 2024), 60,000 t (EPW 2020). 60,000 t / 33,000 m3 = 1.82 t/m3, a normal bulk density of placed armour rock, so 33,000 m3 reads as an as-placed envelope; 60,000 t / 25,000 m3 = 2.4 t/m3 is nearly solid-rock density, so 25,000 m3 reads as a solid volume. The model envelope is 33 % below the register envelope. Sensitivity of V to the seabed level `z_b + o` (o = +1.0, +0.5, +0.25, -0.25, -0.5, -1.0 m): 11,957, 16,537, 19,158, 25,013, 28,010, 34,004 m3; the register volume is reproduced with a seabed 1.0 m deeper, the register height 5 m with a seabed about 1.1 m deeper under the crest. All independent seabed evidence (chart, toe elevations, GEBCO) is shallower than that. Possible reasons not tested: the register carries design or pay-line quantities; rock below the original seabed (toe trench, settlement); the interval is above 0.5 m on the crest side. The model is not scaled to the register.

**4.8 Offshore anchors.** Model seabed at y = 560 m: 11.8 m below LAT (-12.68 m MSL); the concept design gives 11.4 m at "560 m offshore" (a different datum/origin; context only); GEBCO 2024: -13 m in a 450 m cell centred at (23, 534) m, the Navionics seabed there is -11.88 m MSL (1.1 m apart; extrapolated).

**4.9 Re-check against the registered images (2026-10-10; registry ids `palm-beach-gold-coast-img-NN`).** Nine images were flagged "needs 3D check". Tolerance: 2 m in plan (4 m for a georeferenced picture) and the source's stated vertical accuracy (none is stated by any of them, so the model's own +-0.5 m seabed and +-0.2 m crest uncertainties are used). Computed by `build_3d.py` (`recheck_2026_10_10`, stored in `validation_3d.json`) except where marked "by eye / pixel measurement". Result: **no change to the geometry**; provenance rows, the AHD note and this section were updated and the model was rebuilt (seabed, reef grid, contours and every volume identical to 1e-4 m3).

## 5. Uncertainty

Independent input uncertainties (1 sigma, author's judgement stated with its basis):

| input | sigma | basis |
|---|---|---|
| plan outline | 1-2 m | aerial vs council polygon (Hausdorff 0.55 m), geo placement 1-2 m |
| crest level z_c | 0.2 m | rounded figure in four sources; MSL vs AHD 0.12 m |
| MSL - LAT | 0.1 m | MSQ table at the Seaway (not Palm Beach) |
| contour interval Delta | 0.05 m (10 %) | 4.2 |
| chart datum offset | 0.25 m | 4.3 (RMS minimum at -0.27, LAT best) |
| seabed interpolation under the reef | 0.35 m | Navionics-only vs hybrid at the crest (0.37 m) |
| chart line quantisation | 0.15 m | 1 m lines, spline smoothing |

Propagation (first order, independent terms):

```
sigma_zb^2     = sigma_datum^2 + sigma_interp^2 + sigma_q^2                  = 0.25^2 + 0.35^2 + 0.15^2   ->  sigma_zb = 0.46 m  (stated as +-0.5 m)
sigma_H^2      = sigma_zc^2 + sigma_zb^2                                     ->  sigma_H (reef height at the crest, 3.9 m) = 0.54 m
sigma_dLAT^2   = sigma_zc^2 + sigma_(MSL-LAT)^2                              ->  crest below LAT 0.62 m +- 0.22 m
sigma_ztoe     = K sigma_Delta (+ sigma_zb for the absolute toe depth)       ->  seaward toe (K = 10) -7.0 m +- 0.5 m (interval) , +- 0.7 m (with the seabed)
sigma_V        = A sigma_zb = 11,989 m2 x 0.5 m                              ->  +-6,000 m3 (28 % of 22,000 m3)
```

The register's 5 m / 33,000 m3 lies 2.0 sigma (height) and 1.8 sigma (volume) above the model. **Vertical exaggeration** `v` multiplies heights and depths only: the apparent slope is `atan(v tan beta)` (the 1:12 slope looks like 1:12 / v), the apparent reef height `v H`, and a camera heading/pitch read from an exaggerated view is wrong; the photo-match mode is locked to v = 1 and the exaggeration is always displayed.

## 6. Confidence

**medium (3D).** Rubric used: *high* = crest, height and seabed each from a surveyed value or two agreeing surveys; *medium* = the geometry rests on sourced plan/crest/tide values and on depth inferred from two mutually consistent but unlabelled or datum-less sources, with a stated uncertainty and no contradiction larger than twice that; *low* = depth guessed. Plan (verified, high), crest level (four sources) and tides (MSQ) are sourced. The seabed and the reef height are inferred from the Navionics SonarChart and from the aerial's contour lines, which agree with each other (mean 0.27 m, sd 0.55 m; interval 0.46 vs 0.5 m; datum LAT best, MSL rejected) and with the concept slopes, so *low* is not warranted. *High* is not warranted because no surveyed depth value was read, the interval and datum are inferred, the chart is crowd-sourced and not for navigation, the chart shows the reef's toe region but not its crest, and the council register's height and volume are 20-33 % larger than the model (about 1.8-2 sigma). For camera-angle work the plan, crest level and the visible rock outline are reliable; absolute depth (and so the apparent reef height under water) is +-0.5 m.
