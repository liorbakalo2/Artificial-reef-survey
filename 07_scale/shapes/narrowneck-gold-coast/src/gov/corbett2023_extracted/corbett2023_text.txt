Abstract 
The Narrowneck Artificial Reef was constructed by the City of Gold Coast in 1999-2000 as a key component 
of the Northern Gold Coast Beach Protection Strategy.  While primarily intended to enhance coastal protection, 
its secondary objective was to improve surfing.  It was constructed out of approximately 400 mega Geotextile 
Sand Containers using innovative filling and placement methods. Extensive performance monitoring and 
condition assessments were undertaken over subsequent years, but only minor maintenance was undertaken. 
Almost 20 years later, the City of Gold Coast undertook the Narrowneck Artificial Reef Renewal.  This resulted 
in an additional 84 Geotextile Sand Containers being added to reinstate and optimise performance while 
extending the service life of the structure.  The design phase included consideration of two design options - 
the previous design shape and an amended shape intended to enhance performance against project 
objectives, including coastal protection, surfing amenity and safety. The “for construction” designs were tested 
in a physical model which allowed the final construction design to be optimised and performance expectations 
to be evaluated under a range of conditions.   
The works were undertaken as a Design and Construct delivery model with provisional construction quantities.  
This allowed significant Contractor input, facilitating safe and efficient construction in what is a very challenging 
environment.  It also allowed flexibility to consider alternate designs and to revise the construction design in 
response to changes in seabed levels, actual placement locations and available budget.  The works including 
design, physical modelling, material supply and construction, were successfully delivered within a 6 month 
timeframe.  
This paper provides a summary of the Narrowneck artificial reef renewal, including design investigations, 
project delivery model and ongoing monitoring.  
Keywords: coastal protection, submerged, reef, Narrowneck 
1. 
Introduction 
The Narrowneck Artificial Reef (Figure 1) was 
constructed in 1999-2000 as a key component of 
the Northern Gold Coast Beach Protection Strategy.   
While primarily intended to enhance coastal 
protection along a narrow and vulnerable section of 
coast, its secondary objective was to improve 
surfing.  It was constructed out of approximately 400 
mega Geotextile Sand Containers (GSC) using 
custom plant and innovative filling and placement 
methods.  
Figure 1  Narrowneck Artificial Reef (2001) on Gold Coast 
coastline.   
The Narrowneck Artificial Reef was a prototype 
structure. Extensive performance monitoring (refer 
Section 4.1) showed that the reef has been effective 
in widening and increasing the resilience of 
Narrowneck beach in response to storm events, 
providing additional surfing opportunities and 
providing a substrate that supports a diverse marine 
habitat. 
Condition assessments and minor maintenance 
were undertaken up to 2007.  After almost 20 years 
since construction, the reef has been subject to 
significant seabed changes which has resulted in 
burial of sections of the original structure as well as 
the damage and subsequent loss of individual 
GSCs. This has resulted in a reduction of the 
effective reef crest level which reduced its 
performance as a coastal protection structure and 
reduced surfing amenity.  
Renewal of the reef was undertaken by the City of 
Gold Coast (City) in 2017-2018 to reinstate and 
optimise performance while extending the service 
life of the structure.

2. 
Project objectives and constraints 
The objectives of the Narrowneck Reef Renewal 
project (ICM 2017) were: 
• 
Renewal of the reef, consistent with the original 
design intent and statutory approvals. 
• 
Making use of the latest geotextile fabrics in the 
construction of the GSCs to ensure durability. 
• 
Optimisation of the placement of the GSCs to 
work with the existing structure to promote local 
and global stability, maximise the service life of 
the structure and maintain coastal protection, 
with consideration of surfing amenity. 
• 
Ensuring the works are in accordance with 
Safety in Design legislation considering all 
aspects 
of 
the 
asset 
lifecycle 
(design, 
construction, 
operation, 
maintenance 
and 
decommissioning). 
The existing development approvals required the 
renewal design to be within the originally approved 
footprint, profile and construction material. The 
renewal works also needed to be delivered within 
the available budget.  Constructability was also 
critical.   
3. 
Delivery model 
The 
project 
presented 
a 
very 
challenging 
construction environment.  Given the open coast 
wave climate, shallow crest and variable seabed, 
designs needed to enable safe, practical, and 
efficient construction by the Contractor.  Timing was 
also critical to allow design to be complete ready for 
works to be undertaken during periods when calmer 
conditions where expected.  
A Design and Construct delivery model with a 
performance-based scope was adopted to facilitate 
successful project delivery.   
The scope was not constrained by a specific 
reference design but focussed on project objectives 
and performance outcomes. An initial desktop 
design stage allowed for the development of two 
renewal options and subsequent physical modelling 
enabled the selection of a preferred option based on 
Key Performance Indicators. This delivery model 
allowed the design process to consider in detail 
relevant budget constraints and detailed input from 
the construction contractor to ensure that it could be 
successfully installed, while achieving project 
objectives. As the design was not known at the time 
of Contract Award, the construction scope provided 
provisional quantities of three different sizes of 
GSC, corresponding to sizes adopted for the 
original construction of the reef. 
The works were undertaken by dredging contractor 
McQuade Marine in conjunction with design by 
International Coastal Management and physical 
modelling 
by 
the 
Queensland 
Government 
Hydraulics Laboratory. 
4. 
Renewal design 
The renewal design considered the following 
scenarios:   
• 
Existing reef with lower crest due to loss of crest 
containers – condition of reef prior to renewal 
works. 
• 
Renewal Option 1 based on 2004 modified 
design. 
• 
Renewal Option 2 based on 2017 modified 
design (developed as part of these works).  
The 
desktop 
design 
included 
review 
and 
development of the concept shape as well as 
detailed construction designs for both renewal 
options (ICM 2017).   
Physical modelling was undertaken of all three 
scenarios to establish performance under a range 
of conditions and allow selection of the preferred 
Renewal Option to be constructed (ICM 2017 and 
QGHL 2018).  
4.1 
Desktop design 
As part of the desktop design development, the 
following was formally reviewed and summarised: 
• 
Original design (ICM 1998) 
• 
Formal monitoring of the structure during the 
period when it was regularly maintained at the 
design crest height (GCCM 2004, 2007 and 
2011). 
• 
Published works summarising independent 
monitoring undertaken, including Jackson et al. 
(2001, 2007, 2010 & 2012), Tomlinson et al. 
(2008) and Strauss et al. (2017). 
• 
Available literature, particularly that published 
since the development of the 2004 modified 
design, including works relating to shoreline 
response (Ranasinghe et al. 2006 and 2010) 
and safety (Corbett et al. 2005 and MacMahan 
et al. 2011). 
• 
Project objectives and constraints (Section 2). 
• 
Constructability constraints for placement of 
GSC using nominated split-hull hopper barge. 
A modified design developed in 2004 was reviewed 
and considered to be suitable for adoption as 
Renewal Option 1 (Figure 2), including: 
• 
Crest level of -2.5m AHD (-1.5m LAT). 
• 
Cross-shore extent of crest matches approval 
or previously placed GSCs. 
• 
Alongshore reef width of 120m. 
• 
Crest planform shape including flared shape of 
north and south reefs with lower bridge across 
30m paddle channel (Figure 2).

Detailed design was undertaken for both Renewal 
Options, including a GSC placement layout based 
on the pre-works multi-beam survey (Figure 3).  The 
multi-beam 
survey 
provided 
an 
accurate

valuable tool to inform the detailed design process.  
This provided a high level of confidence compared 
with previous maintenance campaigns which were 
necessarily based on traditional single-beam 
hydrographic surveys validated using underwater 
site inspection and physical measurement.

The detailed design also considered the filled 
container geometry, flexibility of the containers 
when placed on a variable seabed and construction 
constraints including available water depth and 
placement orientation relative to predominant swell 
direction. 
The detailed designs were developed considering 
the constraints of the available budget to achieve 
the concept design shape as far as possible.  The 
north reef design shape was almost fully achieved 
for both renewal options, however only partial 
completion of the south reef and the bridge was 
achievable for Renewal Option 2 within the 
available budget. 
As maintenance requirements are largely driven by 
individual GSC damage from anchors and knives, a 
more durable and robust geotextile was very 
desirable to reduce whole of life costs for the 
structure.  Unfortunately, a review of commercially 
available products and correspondence with 
manufacturers did not identify any significant 
advances in this regard.  As a result, the renewal 
works necessarily adopted products developed 
during the original construction of the Narrowneck 
Reef (Jackson and Corbett 2019).   
4.2 
Physical modelling  
Physical modelling was undertaken in a 12m x 14m 
basin at Queensland Government Hydraulics 
Laboratory (QGHL 2018).  A length scale of 1:43 
was adopted.  The general bathymetry was 
constructed using standard techniques; however 
the 
existing 
reef 
was 
Computer 
Numerical 
Controlled (CNC) router machined out of high 
density polystyrene foam to achieve the pre-works 
multi-beam survey with a 1mm model accuracy 
(Figure 4).  The renewal designs were constructed 
using specially fabricated and filled GSC (Figure 5). 
Preliminary model runs were undertaken as a final 
design tool prior to the commencement of formal 
physical model testing.  This allowed the detailed 
design to be optimised through minor amendments 
to the container layout in response to observed 
performance.

The 14 model runs for each of the 3 scenarios 
included a range of irregular and monochromatic 
wave heights between 1 - 3m and 2 different water

evaluation of performance over a range of incident

the performance between the renewal options,

safety.  Larger wave heights were not tested as 
coastal

benefits 
were

reasonably well from monitoring of the existing

would have required a significant reduction in the 
model scale within the basin, which would have 
made assessment of many aspects difficult.

• 
Frequency of wave breaking (during irregular

• 
Transmitted wave height in the lee of the reef 
• 
Breaking wave height 
• 
Breaker type  
•

•

•

• 
Wave-induced currents 
•

4.2.1 Frequency of wave breaking 
Breaking was observed on the existing reef at 
higher wave heights and lower tides only.  As 
expected, both renewal options (with the crest 
reinstated to the design height) experienced wave 
breaking in a broader range of wave heights.  During 
average wave conditions at low tide, frequency of 
wave breaking was increased by ~30%. 
4.2.2 Breaker Type 
Breaker type was classified broadly as spilling, 
plunging or surging. This was assessed visually 
during each model run, supported by oblique video 
recordings and photographs. Both Renewal Options 
experienced breaker types that were suitable for 
surfing (generally plunging). (ICM 2017) 
4.2.3 Ride Length and Peel Angle 
Peel angle and ride length was determined based 
on aerial video recordings (Figure 7) and generally 
extended from the break point probe to the 
intersection with the beach break (ICM 2017).   
Ride lengths were similar for south reef, however for 
north reef (where the full design shape was able to 
be achieved within the budget for both Renewal 
Options), Renewal Option 2  (Table 4-1) was 
characterised by significantly longer ride lengths 
(35-90m compared to 35-128m, being 40% higher 
on average).   
Table 4-1. Physical modelling results for peel angle and 
ride length for Renewal Option 2 
Peel angles (Figure 7) varied depending on 
conditions but were similar on average between 
both options and were within the surfable range for 
all tests (Table 4-1).  Ride lengths and peel angles 
are expected to be variable in response to changes 
in adjacent sand bars that affect the bathymetry 
surrounding the reef.

4.2.5 Shorebreak Characteristics 
The wave field in the lee of the reef was assessed 
primarily on the presence of a peeling shore break.  
While close-out conditions (simultaneous breaking 
along the wave crest with no evidence of peeling) 
was observed for the existing reef configuration due 
to uniformity of the bathymetry, peeling shore 
breaks were observed for both Renewal Option 1 & 
2 in smaller conditions when breaking on the reef 
was not observed.   
4.2.6 Summary 
As 
expected, 
both 
renewal 
options 
were 
significantly superior to the existing reef in terms of 
energy dissipation, frequency of wave breaking and 
surfing amenity.  This emphasises the importance 
of the crest section of the structure in achieving the 
performance objectives and the need for the design 
crest height to be achieved and maintained. 
Where the theoretical design shape was largely 
achieved by the container placement (i.e. north 
reef), Renewal Option 2 provided consistently and 
significantly improved surfing amenity on north reef 
compared with Renewal Option 1, including more 
frequent, better quality breaks and considerably 
longer ride lengths (up to 128m with the break 
extending landward of the structure as shown in 
Figure 7).  Where the theoretical design shape was 
only partially achieved (i.e. south reef), similar 
surfing amenity was achieved between the two 
renewal options. 
As a result, construction of works proceeded based 
on the Renewal Option 2 layout. 
5. 
Construction 
Construction of the renewal works was undertaken 
using the methodology and plant developed for the 
initial reef construction. A Trailing Suction Split-Hull 
Hopper Dredger fills GSCs laid out in the hopper by 
dredging sand and pumping it directly into the GSC. 
The dredger is positioned with the hopper directly 
over the design GSC location using accurate on-
board navigation systems and the GSC is placed by 
opening the hopper (Figure 8).   
The construction was closely monitored throughout 
the works, with underwater visual inspection and 
RTK GPS staff survey of individual GSC undertaken 
every few days to evaluate compliance, determine 
water depths and final location to ensure safe and 
appropriate placement of adjacent GSC.   
The delivery model provided flexibility that allowed 
rapid and effective revisions to design GSC 
locations in response to changes in the seabed 
levels due to storm events, achieved position and 
levels of adjacent GSC throughout construction and 
increases 
to 
the 
available 
budget, 
with 
consideration of both design performance and 
constructability. 
The works, including design, physical modelling, 
material supply and construction, were successfully 
delivered in a 6 month timeframe and within budget.  
The available budget and detailed design ultimately 
resulted in the placement of an additional 84 GSC 
to the reef structure.

6. 
Monitoring 
6.1 
Coastal protection 
While seasonal variability in beach width is high 
(approximately 40m), detailed analysis of shorelines 
has previously shown a salient feature about 100m 
to the north as well as a small groyne effect 
associated with the structure. 
Beach widths in 2009 (when the reef was last 
maintained) were around 50 – 70m at Narrowneck 
(ETA 67 & ETA 68) (Jackson 2012). More recent 
analysis of beach width (September 2018 to March 
2019) is showing beach widths of 70 – 95m (WRL 
2019). It is noted that this is only 8 months after the 
renewal of the reef and as such the shoreline may 
not have reached equilibrium after construction. It is 
also possible that there may be influences from 
massive mega nourishment of Gold Coast Beaches 
although placement did not extend as far north as 
Narrowneck.

6.2 
Surfing 
The surfing amenity provided by Narrowneck Reef 
is provided by:  
• 
Direct breaking of incident waves across the 
reef itself, typically during lower tide conditions 
and suitable swell height (Hs > 1 – 2m).   
• 
Focusing of unbroken waves across the reef 
can often result in a more peaky, powerful 
beach break in the lee of the reef.  
• 
Influence of the reef structure on the alignment 
of adjacent sand bars can often extend the reef 
break and/or enhance the quality of breaks in 
the lee of the reef and/or on the adjacent bars.  
Monitoring of wave breaking on the reef through the 
ARGUS coastal imaging cameras (WRL 2019) 
indicates that (Figure 9): 
• 
Before the reef renewal (with lower crest due to 
loss and/or damage of crest containers), waves 
would only break on the reef regularly when the 
RMS wave height was greater than ~0.8 m at 
low tide and ~1.2 m at high tide. 
• 
Following the reef renewal, waves break on the 
reef regularly when the RMS wave height is 
greater than ~0.5 m at low tide and 0.8 m at high 
tide. 
Figure 8  Comparison of wave and water level conditions 
where most waves were breaking on the reef before and 
after the reef renewal, based on coastal imaging data 
analysis (Source: WRL 2019) 
Surfing amenity on the reef itself has been 
evaluated on an ad-hoc basis using surfer reporting, 
GPS tracking and photo and video capture.  Wave 
breaking characteristics remain variable depending 
on prevailing conditions, including tide level, wave 
height and direction and wind speed and direction.  
The reef has been observed to provide high quality 
waves for surfing when conditions are suitable 
(Figure 10). 
Optimal conditions on 6th July 2018 (soon after 
completion of the reef) resulted in a powerful and 
plunging break (Figure 10) with ride lengths up to 
122m and 19 seconds with maximum speeds of 
30.5kph recorded.  Surf tracks commenced up to 
62m seaward of tracks previously recorded (Figure 
12).  This is comparable to the results of physical 
modelling. 
Conditions on 6th July 2018: 
•

• 
Tp = 9s 
• 
Wave direction = 85 degrees 
• 
Wind = 10 knots (NW) (offshore) 
• 
Tide = 0.4m LAT 
Narrowneck Reef still provides enhanced surf 
quality in the lee of the reef (Figure 12) and 
opportunities for a wide range of surf craft, including 
longboards, SUPs and kite boards (Jackson, 2001). 
More recently, hydrofoil surfing (Figure 11) has 
become popular at Narrowneck Reef as they are 
able to make use of the unbroken and re-forming 
waves, shoaling across the reef in conditions that 
are not optimal for typical shortboard surfing.

6.3 
Safety 
The original construction of the Narrowneck Reef 
resulted in improved safety outcomes in the form of 
reduced frequency of rescues by surf life savers.  In 
the 5 years since the Renewal was completed, the 
average 
number 
of 
rescues 
has 
remained 
reasonably similar.  Given the variability in the data, 
a more detailed long-term assessment would be 
beneficial. 
7. 
Summary 
The Narrowneck Reef was a novel prototype design 
that has proven the concept of multi-functional reef 
structures, with the lessons learned incorporated in 
the Narrowneck Reef Renewal. 
The Narrowneck artificial reef renewal restored the 
crest levels and made slight modifications to its 
shape to reinstate and optimise coastal protection 
performance, surfing amenity and safety while 
extending the service life of the structure. 
The Design and Construct delivery model achieved 
an optimised performance-based design validated 
through comprehensive physical modelling with 
contractor input ensuring safe, practical, and 
efficient construction in what is a very challenging 
construction environment.  It also facilitated 
successful delivery of the project (including design, 
physical modelling, material supply and placement 
of 84 containers) within 6 months.  
Monitoring of wave breaking on the reef has shown 
waves break more often and in a wider range of 
conditions, providing more surfing opportunities.  
Ad-hoc monitoring of surfing amenity indicates that 
while the surf quality is naturally variable depending 
on prevailing conditions, the reef performs well 
when conditions are suitable, with performance 
consistent with physical modelling.
