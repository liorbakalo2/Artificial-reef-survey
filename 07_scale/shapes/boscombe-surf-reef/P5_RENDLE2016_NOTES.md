## 1. Short answer to the three questions

1. **As-built bag layout and crest.** The thesis has NO as-built plan. Fig. 5.3 (p. 95) is the ASR Ltd *design* bag layout of 18 Dec 2006 (no scale, no coordinates, about 46 labelled bags A1-A10, B1-B8, C1-C18, D1-D10). The text says 32 containers were built (p. 68) and that some bags of the plan were missing in the final construction (p. 122). Built crest about +0.5 m chart datum (p. 70), higher than the design (0 m CD in Table 4.4, 0.3 m CD on the Fig. 5.3 drawing). Datum: chart datum for the design; the council surveys are plotted "relative to MSL" (Fig. 5.6) but their content matches chart datum (section 3).
2. **Surveys.** Four council bathymetry surveys were supplied: Oct 2009 (as-built), May 2010, Oct 2010 (panel label "September 2010" in Fig. 5.6), April 2011 (p. 90, PDF 93). A spring 2012 survey exists but the council withheld it (p. 90, p. 96). Only the April 2011 survey is shown in plan (Fig. 5.7, with OS-grid axes), the Oct 2009 survey only as oblique surface and two profiles. So a measured plan of the AS-BUILT state is not in P5; the April 2011 survey is the same one the model already uses (Rendle and Davidson 2012 Fig. 9).
3. **Card facts:** section 5.

## 3. Datum notes (why A3 stays and what P5 adds)

- Design values: chart datum (Table 4.4, p. 70; depth 2.7-5.0 m CD, p. 68; 3 m CD isobath, p. 69).
- Surveys: council bathymetry "relative to MSL" in the Fig. 5.6 caption; Fig. 5.8 y-axis "Elevation (m)" with no datum; CCO beach and bathymetry surveys in m ODN (Figs. 5.9-5.11, p. 102-106).
- Rendle states twice that MSL equals chart datum (Fig. 5.11 caption) and gives "1.66 mODN = MSL" (p. 70). Neither is reliable (at Bournemouth CD is 1.40 m below ODN per NTSLF, S4; at Poole Bay the offset 1.66 would be a different gauge). They look like loose wording.
- Test with her own numbers: seabed -3 to -5.5 m and crest -0.2 to +0.6 m on the plots fit "design depth 2.7-5.0 m CD" and "built crest 0.5 m CD"; they do not fit MSL (crest would then be 1.4 m above chart datum, 1.9 m above the design).
- Net: P5 supports A3 (zero = chart datum) with the caveat above. It does not supply a conversion that is better than the NTSLF 1.40 m.

## 5. Card facts (each with page and figure) - also written to the card

Dates and programme (timeline, p. 64, PDF 67)
- Feasibility study approved 11 Oct 1999; cost-benefit estimate by D. Weight (Curry and Brown) 2000; Cabinet approved leisure content incl. the reef 19 Nov 2003; authority to procure field studies and initial design from ASR Ltd 6 Nov 2004 with no evidence of a tender (p. 64); planning application to DEFRA Feb 2007; fishermen objected Jun 2007; DEFRA approval Oct 2007.
- Construction started 30 Aug 2008 (note: the card says June 2008), suspended 13 Nov 2008, resumed 18 Apr 2009; official opening 2 Oct 2009 (p. 64); the text says completion Nov 2009 after 18 months of construction (p. 65, PDF 68). Storms in 2008 moved anchoring and navigation markers (p. 65).
- April 2010 hydraulic reworking noted ("pillowing"); March 2011 container failure found by the annual bathymetry survey; closed 31 Mar 2011 (p. 64; the text on p. 96 says April 2011); Aug 2011 repairs discussed, ASR Ltd liquidated Sep 2012; April 2012 further failure; 2013 insurance payout GBP 306,531 after a two-year wait; 2013-14 marine park incl. repairs and safety checks GBP 700,000 (GBP 254,000 Coastal Communities Fund plus insurance; source of the rest unclear); HR Wallingford safety check declared it safe; April 2014 rebranded Coastal Activity Park.

Cost (p. 65-67, PDF 68-70)
- Original cost GBP 600,000 (Black et al. 2000), GBP 1.1 m (ASR Ltd 2006, with 20 % contingency for weather downtime and unforeseen equipment), final construction cost about GBP 3.2 m; causes: unrealistic approach to visa requirements, shipping and equipment procurement in the UK, weather downtime (p. 65). Repair work a further GBP 700,000 (p. 65, p. 64).
- Weight (2000) estimate (Table 4.3, p. 66): two reefs GBP 480,000 (A. Jackson, ICM Australia) + design fees GBP 135,000 (ASR Ltd) + MAFF flood-protection licence GBP 3,000 = GBP 618,000. Cost:benefit 5.63 / 9.42 / 15.61 (pessimistic / moderate / optimistic), payback 2.27 / 1.66 / 1.12 years (Table 4.2, p. 66); moderate case: surf days double to 150, surf tourists 84 to 2,025 a year, GBP 373,000 a year, present value about GBP 5.8 m over 25 years at 4 % (p. 66-67). The "1:20" ratio in the media is thought to come from the optimistic case plus extra benefits; its breakdown was not available (p. 67). Rendle calls the estimate not objective (made by an enthusiastic surfer using the contractor's figures, p. 68).
- The claim that one reef could replace two groynes at similar cost is not supported (p. 65, citing Mead 2009).

Performance (p. 70-71, PDF 73-74; p. 143-144 PDF 146-147)
- Davidson (2010) six-month assessment: 4 of 11 design criteria met (surfability, wave form, peel angle, wave height amplification), 2 partly (ride length, physical shape) (p. 70). Wave steeper and more frequently plunging than designed, ride about 20 m left and 40 m right (design 20 m left, 65 m right, p. 69-70), "restricted to more advanced surfers" (p. 70-71).
- Crest higher than planned: surfable only about 1-2 h around mid-tide; exposed at low tide; too deep at high tide (p. 71).
- Wave model (MIKE21): attenuation coefficient A < 1 under all conditions tested, i.e. the reef attenuates waves in its lee (p. 143-144).
- Shoreline: no significant response in 2008-2011, no salient or tombolo, local scour at the base, -3.5 m contour 50 m closer to the beach in the lee (p. 102). The reef was designed for surfing, not coastal protection; HR Wallingford predicted a broadly neutral effect (p. 65).

Failure and lessons (p. 94-98, p. 120-123)
- After winter 2009-10 sand moved inside the bags ("pillowing"), seaward to landward (p. 94). Tears poorly repaired were found at the first inspection (Richards 2009, p. 120).
- 2011: one T4 container (A4, 5.9 x 2.3 x 65.5 m, 746.7 m3) lost its sand: 5 m gap, 2 m crest dip, cause contested (weakness, propeller strike, or vandalism by displaced fishermen, verbal account) (p. 94, 96, 121). 2012: neighbouring T4 (A5) lost, gap 8-10 m. Total 1,493.4 m3 = 12.5 % of 11,900 m3 (Table 5.3, p. 98); the text prints 1,492.4 (typo). Only 8 T4 containers exist (p. 121).
- Construction shortcuts: containers not all filled; wrong grain size from the council, mixed grain needing sieving, weather, end-of-summer deadline (p. 98).
- Thesis lessons (p. 121-122): check the seabed level against the design and stop if it differs; no bags should be missing from the final build (smaller side bags support the large ones); seal fill ports carefully; the +0.5 m higher crest added hydraulic stress; avoid geotextile in a shallow exposed surf zone; unlikely to reach the 25-year life without repairs (p. 121).
- Management: repairs not done after relations between council and ASR Ltd broke down; reef still closed at August 2013; ASR liquidated; insurance claims pending (p. 123).
- Sand: 23,000 m3 was piled on the upper beach to be pumped offshore as slurry (Fig. 5.28); the build needed 11,900 m3 "if built to design"; the council noted that not all containers of the design are in the final construction and that they are not all filled to capacity; the remaining 11,100 m3 stayed on the beach between the pier and the first groyne (p. 125-127, PDF 128-130; Fig. 5.28 photo not saved).
- Waves context: Waverider buoy at about 12 m ODN depth; mean Hs 0.53 m, maximum 3.84 m over 2003-2012 (p. 90, PDF 93); average modal direction 191 deg and modal period 3.5 s (Fig. 5.2, p. 93).
