# Cable Station Reef (Cables Reef), WA — Footprint Research

Status: verified (2026-09-25)

## Summary

Cables Reef (Cable Station, Cottesloe/Leighton, Perth, WA) is a **granite-boulder rock mound placed directly on the existing natural limestone reef/bedrock** — not a sand-filled geotextile-container ("GSC") reef. Two independent primary sources give the same headline envelope dimensions: **length (north–south, alongshore) ≈ 140 m, maximum width (cross-shore, shallow-to-deep) ≈ 70 m**, sited **≈ 275 m offshore** in **3–6 m** water depth, with the reef **crest 1–3 m below the surface on average tides** (≈1.5 m below the lowest tide per a 2009 review). The 1999 University of Western Australia (UWA) design-studies paper for this exact reef (newly located this pass, see [S1]) confirms the plan shape directly: a **"V"/chevron ("boomerang") mound**, built by extending the natural reef's 3 m depth contour offshore up a uniform 1:20 slope to the natural reef's 1 m contour, producing **two unequal peeling arms** — a shorter left-hand ride (30–40 m) and a longer right-hand ride (50–80 m). No source publishes a plan-view footprint area, crest width, or an exact compass bearing for the arms, so those are the file's weakest (low-confidence) numbers.

Gemini's `cables` entry in `reef_footprints.json` is **substantially wrong on both structure type and scale**: it labels Cables Reef a "Geotextile Sand Container (GSC) Surf Reef" "designed by Dr. Kerry Black (ASR Ltd) & ICM," 115 m × 75 m, 170 m offshore. Every one of these claims is contradicted by Gemini's own cited primary source (Raised Water Research), which we re-fetched directly this pass and which states **140 m / 70 m / 275 m / granite boulders** in nearly the same words as our card. Gemini's structure-type and designer appear to be copy-over contamination from the unrelated New Zealand/Gold Coast ASR-Ltd/Kerry-Black **sand-bag** reefs (Narrowneck, Mount Maunganui, Opunake) that sit elsewhere in the same Gemini dataset. See "Gemini comparison" below for the quantity-by-quantity breakdown.

Confidence: **high** for length (140 m), width (70 m), material (granite on limestone bedrock), and general "V/boomerang" shape typology (directly stated/depicted in two independent primary sources). **Medium** for distance offshore (two sources within ~10% of each other, 250–275 m) and crest depth (stated as a 1–3 m range, single-value estimate taken from a secondary review). **Low** for the exact polygon vertices, arm crest width, plan-view area, and the compass direction the apex/arms face (no plan-view figure with a scale bar or north arrow was recoverable at usable resolution).

## Polygon construction

- **Coordinate convention:** origin at the shoreline point nearest the reef's centroid; x = alongshore, positive toward north (the card gives the reef's length as "north–south" per [R2] but no source states which specific arm/end is the northern one — treated here as an arbitrary but stated convention, not a surveyed bearing); y = offshore, positive seaward; shoreline approximated as the line y = 0.
- **Centreline:** three points define the V — a single apex and two arm tips. Because [S1]'s Figure 7 could not be read at high enough resolution to settle which way the apex points (see "Images/figures relied on" above), we adopted **apex-offshore** (the vertex is the seaward-most point of the structure, and the two arms sweep landward from it) as the working hypothesis, for two reasons: (1) it is the standard configuration for point-break-style chevron surf reefs generally (matching, independently, the orientation Gemini also assumed for this same reef); and (2) it is consistent with [S1]'s text describing the new fill as "extending the existing 3 m depth contour offshore" (i.e., the newest/deepest part of the structure, the apex, projects out toward deeper water) while the arms "merge with the existing reef" inshore at its shallower 1 m contour.
- **Centreline coordinates** (before buffering to a solid ribbon): apex at (x=-20, y≈304 m offshore), left arm tip at (x=-70, y≈235 m offshore), right arm tip at (x=+70, y≈245 m offshore). The apex is offset from the alongshore centre (rather than centred at x=0) and the two tips are placed asymmetrically in y so that, after buffering, the resulting polygon's diagonal-tip distances are in roughly a 1.3–1.9x ratio, echoing (not reproducing exactly) the ~1.5–2x asymmetry in [S1]'s stated 30-40 m / 50-80 m ride lengths. This proportion is illustrative, not a measured fact.
- **Ribbon width:** the centreline was "thickened" into a solid, closed polygon using a straight (flat-cap) buffer of half-width 6 m (i.e., a 12 m-wide arm — see Derivation table; unsourced), via `shapely.geometry.LineString.buffer(6.0, cap_style="flat", join_style="bevel")`, then the whole polygon was translated in y so its centroid lands at y≈273.5 m offshore — matching [R2]'s stated 275 m distance-offshore figure.
- **Resulting polygon** (6 vertices + closing point, metres): `(-18.8,297.2), (66.5,236.5), (73.5,246.3), (-21.2,313.6), (-74.7,245.1), (-65.3,237.7), (-18.8,297.2)`.
- **Resulting geometry:** bounding box ≈148.2 m (alongshore) × 77.1 m (cross-shore) — about 6–10% larger than the sourced 140×70 m envelope, an expected side-effect of the flat-cap/bevel-join buffering method adding a small offset at the sharp interior apex angle, not a data disagreement. Area = 2,298.8 m² (rounds to ~2,300 m² in the JSON). Max pairwise vertex distance (tip-to-tip) = 148.2 m, close to the sourced 140 m length.
- **Validation:** checked with `shapely` (`Polygon.is_valid == True`, `exterior.is_simple == True`, `exterior.is_ring == True`) — closed and non-self-intersecting.
- **What is NOT independently verified in this polygon:** the exact apex-vs-arms orientation (offshore vs onshore-facing), the arm crest width, and therefore the plan-view area. The overall alongshore/cross-shore bounding envelope (140×70 m) and the offshore centroid position (~275 m) are the file's high/medium-confidence numbers; the polygon's internal shape is an illustrative reconstruction built to be topologically consistent with [S1]'s figures and text, not a digitized survey.

## Open issues

- The apex-vs-arms orientation (does the "V" point offshore toward the swell, or onshore toward the beach?) could not be conclusively read from the only available plan-view figures ([S1] Figures 4 and 7), both low-resolution 1999 photocopies with illegible scale bars/labels. We adopted "apex offshore" as a working assumption (see Polygon construction); flagged low confidence.
- No source publishes a crest width, a plan-view footprint area, or an exact compass bearing for the arms of Cables Reef. The 12 m crest-width assumption and the resulting ≈2,300 m² area figure in this file are our own illustrative estimate, not a published number — do not treat `area_m2` in the JSON as more than order-of-magnitude.
- The card's [R8] citation attributes "Design Studies for an Artificial Surfing Reef: Cable Station, Western Australia" to "Hutt et al." (from a blocked ResearchGate search snippet); the full paper we located and read this pass ([S1]) lists Charitha Pattiaratchi as sole author on its title page. This is likely a small attribution error in the card worth a future correction pass — not corrected here, per this task's footprint-file-only scope.
- Granite-vs-limestone material disagreement and the 3,500 m³-vs-5,000 m³ volume disagreement are both carried over unresolved from the verified card; this pass found no new source that settles either.
- The 250 m (pre-final-design, [S1]) vs 275 m (final, [R2]) offshore-distance figures may simply reflect the documented mid-construction southern-end extension noted in the card ([R5]) rather than a genuine source conflict, but no source explicitly connects the two numbers this way — flagged as an inference, not a confirmed explanation.
