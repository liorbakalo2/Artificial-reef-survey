# Palm Beach Reef — footprint provenance

Companion file: `palm-beach-gold-coast.footprint.json` — status: **verified** (2026-09-25)

## Summary

- **Shape:** Elongated, rounded rock mound — narrower/pointed at its shoreward end, broader/blunter at its seaward end (an "asymmetric mound," not a sharp-edged chevron or trapezoid).
- **Dimensions:** 160 m long (alongshore) × 80 m wide (cross-shore) "core," per the majority of sources.
- **Area:** ~9,678 m² net footprint from our estimated polygon (see Polygon construction). The commonly quoted "12,800 m²" is simply 160 × 80 — a bounding-box product, not a surveyed area figure; no source publishes an independent area number.
- **Crest depth:** 1.5 m below mean sea level at the highest point (≈0.6 m below lowest astronomical tide).
- **Position:** ~270 m offshore of 19th Avenue, Palm Beach, Gold Coast.
- **Confidence:** medium. Length, width (core), crest depth and offshore distance are directly stated in multiple independent sources including the City of Gold Coast's own explainer video. The precise polygon outline is a qualitative estimate from a non-scaled marketing schematic and a real aerial photo — no true as-built CAD or bathymetric survey was found publicly. A significant, still-unresolved source conflict exists on width (80 m vs 144 m) and offshore distance (270 m vs 330 m), both from Royal HaskoningDHV's own project page vs. every other source.

## Images/figures relied on

1. **`Palm-Beach-Artificial-Reef-Completion2.jpg`** (Cru Collective, [R7]) — a labelled schematic overlay on satellite imagery captioned by the site as *"Section view of the artificial reef showing rock sizes."* It shows a simplified plan-view icon of the reef and an arrow labelled *"Approx. 270m"* running from 19th/21st Avenue to the reef. **What was read off it:** the reef icon is a rounded, smoothly-curved shape (not a sharp polygon) that tapers to a narrow, rounded point at the end nearest the arrow (i.e. the shoreward end) and widens to a blunter, rounder mass at the far end. The 270 m arrow visibly lands on the reef's *near* edge, not its middle — evidence that "270 m offshore" more likely describes a near-edge distance than a centroid distance. **Method:** direct visual reading of a non-scaled illustrative icon — not a CAD or survey drawing, so used only for qualitative shape/taper direction, not for precise vertex coordinates. **Verified 2026-09-25:** re-downloaded and viewed at 3x zoom. Taper reading and near-edge arrow placement both confirmed. **New caveat:** the reef icon is a generic, stock-style 3D "rock pile" graphic (flat grey shading, a single highlight stripe typical of a stock render), not a purpose-drawn technical plan of this specific reef — it is weak, illustrative evidence of taper direction only. Also, this near-edge reading is in tension with the polygon construction below, which places the *centroid* (not the near edge) at y=270 — flagged as an open issue.
2. **`Palm-Beach-Artificial-Reef-Completion.jpg`** (Cru Collective, [R7]) — a wider labelled aerial overview captioned *"Aerial view of the area benefited by the artificial reef,"* showing the "Benefit Area" (pink hatched zone along the beach), a small "Artificial Reef" icon offshore, "Palm Beach Natural Reef" further offshore, the 11th/19th/21st Avenue groynes, Currumbin Creek and Burleigh Headland. **What was read off it:** the reef's position relative to the natural reef and the shoreline's overall trend (the coast runs from the groyne area up toward Burleigh Headland in this frame), and a second, independent confirmation that the reef icon is drawn as a rounded blob rather than an angular chevron. **Verified 2026-09-25:** re-viewed at 4x zoom; the icon is small and low-resolution — confirmed as a rounded blob shape, but too indistinct at this size to independently confirm a specific taper direction; treated only as weak corroboration of "rounded, not angular," not as independent taper evidence.
3. **ABC News aerial photograph** (`live-production.wcms.abc-cdn.net.au/45b719e6a29fdb237a75b4f095d83fbf`, [R9]) — a real aerial photo of the completed reef area with a dredging vessel on site. **What was read off it:** zooming into the water next to the dredger reveals an elongated, roughly lens/teardrop-shaped dark patch (submerged rock visible through the water's color change), tapering toward its near/dredger-side end and broader/rounder at its far end. This is real photographic evidence, independent of the Cru Collective schematic, and it shows the *same* taper direction — used as a cross-check, not as a scale reference (no object of known size was identifiable in the crop to calibrate absolute dimensions from this photo alone). **Verified 2026-09-25:** re-downloaded the full photo and zoomed on the patch; the elongated dark patch is confirmed. **New caveat:** the dredger is actively working at the near/broad end of the patch, so some of that end's darkening may be dredging turbidity/sediment plume rather than pure submerged-rock shadow — a possible confound not previously flagged, which modestly weakens (without invalidating) this photo as independent taper evidence.

## Polygon construction

1. **Bounding box.** Set the box to exactly 160 m (alongshore, x) × 80 m (cross-shore, y), per the near-unanimous published dimensions, centred on x = 0 with its centroid placed at y = 270 m (the stated offshore distance), since no source specifies whether "270 m" is measured to the near edge, far edge or centroid of the structure. This is a simplifying assumption, flagged in `open_issues`.
2. **Outline shape.** Rather than a faceted chevron, we drew a smooth, rounded, closed polygon (17 vertices) that: (a) comes to a narrow, rounded point at its shoreward (low-y) end, around (0, 226); (b) widens gradually on both sides moving seaward; (c) reaches its broadest, most rounded extent near its seaward (high-y) edge, around y = 305–306; (d) is mildly asymmetric about x = 0 (slightly fuller on the +x/north side), reflecting the "asymmetric mound" character mentioned in multiple sources' framing of the reef as an engineered, sculpted shape rather than a simple symmetric pile. This directly encodes what was read off the two Cru Collective schematic graphics and the ABC aerial photo (see Images section) — taper toward shore, broaden toward sea.
3. **Validation.** The polygon was checked in Python with Shapely: it is closed (first vertex = last vertex), `is_valid` is `True`, and `exterior.is_simple` is `True` (no self-intersections). Its bounding box is exactly 160.0 m × 80.0 m and its centroid sits at (0.03, 270.0), matching the design targets. Its net (shoelace) area is 9,677.6 m².
4. **Coordinate convention.** x = alongshore, positive toward north — chosen because the City of Gold Coast's own video states that "sand naturally moves north along the coast" due to the predominant southeasterly wave direction, making north the natural positive/downdrift reference direction for this stretch of coast. y = offshore, positive seaward, shoreline at y = 0.
5. **What this polygon is not:** it is not a reproduction of any as-built survey or CAD drawing — none was found publicly, despite Gemini's dataset citing one (see Gemini comparison, below). It should be read as an illustration of the reef's general shape family (elongated, rounded, asymmetric, shoreward-tapered mound) at the correct overall scale, not as a precise as-built outline.

## Open issues

- Width (80 m vs Royal HaskoningDHV's own 144 m) and distance offshore (270 m vs Royal HaskoningDHV's own 330 m) remain unresolved across every source checked, including this pass — unchanged from the card [R5].
- No source explicitly labels which of "160 m" / "80 m" is the alongshore vs cross-shore dimension; we assumed 160 m = alongshore, 80 m = cross-shore (the conventional reading, also used by Gemini), but this is an inference.
- The reference point for "270 m offshore" (near edge, far edge, or centroid) is not stated anywhere; we assumed centroid for simplicity. True near/far edges could differ by roughly ±40–45 m from that assumption.
- **New (Verification 2026-09-25):** this file's own image analysis (see Images section, item 1) concludes the "270 m" arrow on the Cru Collective schematic visually lands on the reef's near/shoreward edge, not its centroid — yet the polygon below places the *centroid* at y=270, not the near edge. This is an internal inconsistency that survived until this check. If the near-edge reading is correct, the whole polygon should sit roughly 40 m further offshore than currently drawn. Not corrected here (would require a new speculative polygon construction, outside this adversarial-check task's scope) — flagged for a future revision.
- No true as-built CAD drawing or bathymetric survey of the constructed reef was found to be publicly accessible (Gemini's citation for one does not correspond to any real document we could locate). Our polygon is a qualitative shape estimate, not a measured outline.
- Precise lat/lon of the submerged structure itself (as opposed to the 19th Avenue onshore reference point) was not found in any source.
- Net footprint area (~9,678 m²) is a consequence of our assumed polygon shape, not an independently measured or published number; treat as low-confidence even though it is reported as the headline `area_m2`.
