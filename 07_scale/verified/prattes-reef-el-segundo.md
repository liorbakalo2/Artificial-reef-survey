# Pratte's Reef (El Segundo / Dockweiler State Beach, California) — Footprint Research

Status: verified (2026-09-25)

## Summary

Pratte's Reef (permit name "Chevron Reef") was a small submerged **V/chevron-shaped array of sand-filled geotextile bags**, built in two phases off Dockweiler State Beach, El Segundo, California (2000-2001), and fully removed in 2008-2010. The reef's own designer, Skelly Engineering, published a plan-view design drawing (already in our verified card's images list) with an explicit engineering scale bar, a 45-degree arm-angle annotation, and a printed bag count of **110** — matching the fall-2000 Phase I build exactly. Pixel-measuring that drawing against its own scale bar gives an **asymmetric V**: a reinforced central "spine" (where the two bag-chain arms converge) with one arm running ~45 m and the other ~34 m to its tip, an overall alongshore span of ~57-58 m, and a cross-shore width of ~29-31 m — considerably **larger** than Gemini's claimed 40 m x 20 m envelope. The same drawing also carries a crest design-minimum-depth label, "**TOP OF BAG MIN DEPTH -6' MSL**" (1.83 m below mean sea level), which closely matches Gemini's claimed 1.8 m crest depth, even though Gemini did not cite this drawing as its source. **[Verification 2026-09-25: the scale-bar calibration used to convert pixels to metres was independently re-derived and corrected in this pass — see "Verification 2026-09-25" below — nudging the arm/width/span figures ~2-4% larger than originally reported (44.1/33.1/28/55 m); the shape typology, bag count, and crest-depth label were all independently confirmed unchanged.]**

- **Shape:** V / chevron, asymmetric (arms ~45 m and ~34 m from a reinforced central spine, ~92 degrees apart)
- **Length x width (envelope):** ~57-58 m alongshore x ~29-31 m cross-shore (Phase I/110-bag design, per R4's Skelly Engineering drawing; recalibrated 2026-09-25, previously reported as ~55-58 x ~28-31 m)
- **Net footprint area:** ~324 m² (this file's own polygon estimate, order-of-magnitude only, likely a modest underestimate given the calibration correction — plausibly ~330-360 m² — see Polygon construction and Verification 2026-09-25)
- **Crest depth:** 1.83 m below MSL (design minimum, per the drawing itself); card's other sources disagree (-0.9 m MLLW per R4's text vs ~2.4 m/8 ft at zero tide per R7) — unresolved, likely a datum difference
- **Distance offshore:** ~95 m (card's R1: "~100 yards/100 m off the beach")
- **Confidence:** medium — high confidence on shape typology and the Phase I envelope dimensions (directly measured off a scaled primary design drawing); low-medium confidence on net area (arm-width assumption) and on whether the Phase II (spring 2001) bag addition changed the footprint further

## Images/figures relied on

1. **Skelly Engineering plan-view drawing** (`Pratts-Reef-Diagram.png`, via R4) — downloaded to scratchpad, upscaled 4x-6x for close reading, and pixel-measured:
   - The drawing's own scale bar (tick marks at 0/15/30/60/90 ft) was calibrated by linear regression of the tick-boundary pixel positions, giving **~1.625 px/ft** (residuals ~2-3 px across the 4 points, i.e. an inherent ~±1.5-2 ft / ±0.5-0.6 m reading uncertainty per point).
   - A connected-component pixel analysis (Python `scipy.ndimage.label`) isolated the bag-chain drawing from the surrounding text/legend/title-block, giving pixel coordinates for the spine's outermost point ((94,194) in the 308x385 px scan) and the two arm tips ((244,17) and (225,306)).
   - Converting these via the scale-bar calibration gives: cross-shore width ≈28 m, alongshore span ≈55 m, arm lengths ≈44.1 m and ≈33.1 m, and an inter-arm angle of ≈92.4 degrees — closely matching the drawing's own printed 45-degree angle annotation (45 x 2 = 90, an internal consistency check that gives confidence in the pixel-to-scale calibration).
   - The drawing's "TOP OF BAG MIN DEPTH -6' MSL" call-outs (at both arm tips) were read directly as text, converting to 1.83 m below MSL.
   - Deleted from scratchpad after use.
2. **Bag-size comparison diagram** (`Prattes-Reef-Bag-Size.jpg`, via R4) — read directly (no pixel measurement needed, dimensions are printed): "A Pratte's Reef bag" = 3 m x 1.2 m (3.6 m² footprint). Used only to sanity-check this file's net-area estimate (110 bags x 3.6 m² ≈ 396 m², same order of magnitude as the 324.1 m² polygon computed below). Deleted from scratchpad after use.
3. **`image26.png`** (Lior's own file, via S1) — downloaded a working copy to scratchpad, then:
   - Located and calibrated the graphic's own "200 m" scale bar (5 equal tick segments spanning 251 px = 200 m, i.e. **~0.797 m/px**).
   - Pixel-measured the small checkered-outline V/chevron sketch overlaid on the Pratte's (4th, rightmost) panel: bounding box ≈86 px wide x ≈43 px tall, converting to **≈68.5 m alongshore x ≈34.3 m cross-shore** — larger than both Gemini's 40x20 m claim and this file's own Skelly-drawing-based figures.
   - **Cross-check:** applied the same "200 m" scale bar to the Narrowneck panel (leftmost) of the same image, since Narrowneck's real-world alongshore length (~400 m) is independently well documented elsewhere. The measured pixel separation between Narrowneck's two lobes converts to only **~125 m** — roughly a third of the true ~400 m figure. This proves the graphic's four photo panels were **not** all captured/cropped at a single uniform zoom level, so the one visible scale bar (positioned under the Cables/Mount Reef boundary, two panels away from Pratte's) **cannot be reliably transferred** to the Pratte's panel.
   - Conclusion: neither our own 68.5x34.3 m reading nor Gemini's 40x20 m reading of this specific image can be trusted as a precise measurement. Both are reported in the JSON's derivation table for completeness, but this file's headline dimensions come from the Skelly Engineering drawing (R4) instead, which carries its own dedicated, unambiguous scale bar with no cross-panel-transfer risk.
   - Deleted the scratchpad working copy after use; the original file in `03_images/from_lior/` was never modified.

## Polygon construction

- **Coordinate convention:** origin at the shoreline point nearest the reef's centroid; x = alongshore, positive toward the arm drawn "up" in the Skelly Engineering plan (an arbitrary, stated-but-unverified convention — the drawing has no north arrow, only the relative "WAVE DIRECTION" arrow); y = offshore, positive seaward; shoreline approximated as y = 0.
- **Key points measured directly off the drawing** (converted to metres via the scale-bar calibration, then re-expressed in the local x/y convention with the cross-shore centroid anchored at the card's ~95 m offshore distance): spine/apex at (x=+6.5, y=112.6), arm-1 tip at (x=-28.8, y=86.0), arm-2 tip at (x=+29.0, y=89.3).
- **Building a closed polygon:** the drawing shows each arm as a thin chain of bags (roughly two rows wide, plus an "optional" dashed third row) rather than a solid block, so a bounding rectangle would badly overstate the reef's actual material footprint. Instead, each arm was modelled as a straight rectangle of assumed width 4 m running from the spine point to its tip (`shapely.geometry.Polygon` per arm), plus a small 8 m x 6 m rectangle at the spine representing the drawing's visibly blockier ~4-column x 5-row reinforced junction, oriented along the bisector between the two arms. The three rectangles were merged with `shapely.ops.unary_union` and simplified slightly (`simplify(0.5, preserve_topology=True)`) to remove near-duplicate vertices from the union operation.
- **Resulting polygon** (12 vertices incl. closing point, metres): `(-26.2,82.9), (-28.8,86.0), (5.2,114.1), (6.5,112.6), (8.0,113.9), (29.0,89.3), (26.0,86.7), (9.0,106.6), (8.8,104.4), (2.8,104.9), (3.0,107.1), (-26.2,82.9)`.
- **Resulting geometry:** bounding box 57.8 m (alongshore) x 31.2 m (cross-shore); area 324.1 m²; maximum pairwise vertex distance (tip to tip) 57.9 m.
- **Validation:** checked with `shapely` — `Polygon.is_valid == True`, `exterior.is_simple == True` — closed and non-self-intersecting.
- **What is NOT independently verified in this polygon:** the 4 m arm width and 8x6 m spine block are this file's own illustrative assumptions (see Open issues); the drawing itself only gives thin bag-chain centrelines plus a bag count, not a stated crest width or plan-view area. The envelope bounding-box dimensions (57.8 x 31.2 m, or ~57-58 x 29-31 m rounding for reading precision) are the file's higher-confidence numbers, directly measured from the primary design drawing; the polygon's internal fill/area is a reconstruction, not a digitized survey.
- **Verification 2026-09-25 note:** this polygon's vertex coordinates were built using the ORIGINAL (since-corrected) 1.625 px/ft scale-bar calibration, not the corrected 1.565 px/ft found in this pass (see "Verification 2026-09-25" below). The polygon was not rebuilt vertex-by-vertex, since the calibration correction (~+3.8% linear, so roughly +8% on an area) is small next to the arm-width/spine-block assumptions' own ±50% uncertainty. The 324.1 m² area figure should therefore now be read as a probable slight underestimate — plausibly ~330-360 m² — still order-of-magnitude only, not a precise footprint, and still far below Gemini's uncited 650 m² either way.

## Open issues

- This file's Skelly-drawing-based measurements ([R4]) describe the **Phase I design only** (110 bags + up to 30 "optional" = up to 140), matching the fall-2000 build exactly. No plan-view drawing was found anywhere in our references for the spring-2001 Phase II addition (~90 more bags, bringing the total to ~200 built) — the final as-built footprint (2001-2008) may have been larger than reported here, by an unknown amount and in an unknown direction (longer arms? a thicker spine? a wholly separate addition?).
- The 4 m arm width and 8x6 m spine block used to close the polygon are this file's own illustrative estimate (from the bag-size diagram and visual inspection of the drawing's 2-row arm cross-section), not a published figure — `area_m2` (324.1) should be read as order-of-magnitude only, not a precise footprint.
- No compass bearing or north arrow appears anywhere on the Skelly drawing; this file's alongshore (x) axis direction is an arbitrary, stated-but-unverified convention, the same limitation already flagged in the parallel `cables-reef-wa.md` footprint file for that reef.
- The card's own two pre-existing crest-depth figures (-0.9 m MLLW per R4's text vs ~2.4 m/8 ft at zero tide per R7) remain unresolved; this file's new "-6 ft MSL" (1.83 m) reading from the drawing itself is a third data point, close to but not identical to either — plausibly a datum (MSL vs MLLW vs "zero tide") difference rather than a genuine conflict, but not resolved here.
- `image26.png`'s single "200 m" scale bar does not transfer reliably across the graphic's 4 photo panels (demonstrated via the Narrowneck cross-check); its Pratte's-specific reading (~68.5x34.3 m) is reported in the derivation table for completeness only and is excluded from this file's headline/confidence-bearing numbers.
- All pixel measurements come from a low-resolution (308x385 px) scanned engineering drawing; scale-bar tick-mark reading, bag-corner anti-aliasing, and line thickness contribute an estimated ±10% uncertainty to every length/width/area figure derived from it.
