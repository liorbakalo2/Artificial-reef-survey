# Mount Maunganui Beach Reef ("Mount Reef") — footprint provenance

Status: **verified** 2026-09-25 (adversarial verification pass on top of the 2026-09-25 footprint/shape research pass, which itself followed the card's 2026-09-24 general-facts verification)

## Summary

- **Shape:** A two-arm "delta-wing"/chevron reef. **Design intent** (1999-2005 papers/press): a symmetric V with its apex pointing seaward and the structure widening toward a shore-parallel base (Kerry Black's own description). **As-built** (per official post-construction bathymetric surveys): an asymmetric, bent shape — a "northern arm" and a "southern arm" meeting near a shared apex/focus point, at an angle visually closer to ~90° than to a narrow symmetric V, most likely a direct consequence of the reef's well-documented staged, incomplete, multi-year construction.
- **L × W:** As-built overall bounding envelope **≈80 m (shore-parallel) × 70 m (cross-shore)**, per an official Bay of Plenty Regional Council engineering assessment, independently cross-checked at **79 m × 67 m** by an academic thesis survey (Scarfe 2008) cited in the same report. This supersedes the raisedwaterresearch.com "50 m × 100 m" figure already in our card, which that same official report identifies as the *original, pre-2005* "wedge" concept — abandoned in favour of the "delta-wing" redesign before construction began.
- **Area:** As-built net two-arm footprint estimated at **≈2,500 m²** (this file's own polygon construction); gross bounding-box area ≈5,600 m² (80×70 m).
- **Crest depth:** Design target **0.4 m below Chart Datum/LAT**; the majority of the as-built crest surveyed (through 2013) at **0.8-1.0 m below Chart Datum**, i.e. built somewhat deeper than designed. S1 separately states an unattributed single high point at ~0.2 m below Chart Datum (≈0.1 m below LAT — one point in two datums, not a range) with no arm named [CORRECTED 2026-09-25: previously and wrongly attributed to "the northern arm crest" in the JSON, a detail not in the source], and an isolated shallow spot explicitly on the **southern** arm at 0.2-0.4 m below Chart Datum — these may be the same feature described twice, but S1 never states that equivalence.
- **Position:** **≈250 m offshore** of Tay St/Marine Parade (design target was 300 m; built ~50 m closer due to a Port of Tauranga dredge-disposal-area constraint), in an ambient (pre-reef) water depth of 3.7-5 m.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The overall bounding dimensions and crest-depth figures are high-confidence, directly-quoted numbers from an official engineering report cross-checked by an academic thesis. The specific polygon vertex placement (bend angle, per-arm length split) is a lower-confidence visual estimate from survey figures, clearly flagged as such.

## Sources and what each gave

- **S4** (Andrew Moores, *New Zealand Geographic*, Issue 078, Mar-Apr 2006, "Artificial surf reefs — coming to a beach near you", https://www.nzgeo.com/stories/artificial-surf-reefs-coming-to-a-beach-near-you/ — **new source, fetched and read in full this pass** via curl with a browser user-agent, since a plain WebFetch returns 403 on this domain). A magazine feature directly quoting designer Kerry Black. Gave, verbatim: "The reef at Mount Maunganui is shaped like a cross between a heavy-limbed V and a delta wing. The apex of the V points seaward and the structure thickens towards the base. It is formed from 24 huge cylindrical bags attached to a securing mesh made from belts of fabric. This structure was taken out to sea in a barge and anchored to the sandy sea-floor 250 m off the beach in 4.5 m of water. ... The largest bags are 3.5 m high and 50 m long, and hold 660 m3 of sand. In total, the reef will use 6000 m3 of sand and will measure 75 m from apex to base and almost 100 m across the base"; and, on crest depth: "the Mount Maunganui reef will produce hollow waves (especially towards low tide, when water depth over the reef will be less than a metre)".

## Polygon construction

Coordinate convention: origin at the shoreline point (Tay St/Marine Parade waterline) nearest the reef centroid; **x = alongshore, positive toward north** (chosen because S1/Mead 2011 consistently name the two arms "northern arm" and "southern arm" — a real, sourced basis for this axis choice, even though no source states the exact compass bearing); **y = offshore, positive seaward**; shoreline approximated as y = 0.

The reef is modelled as **two separate rectangular arms** (`polygons_m`, not a single fused polygon), because:
1. S1 and Mead (2011) consistently describe the structure as two distinct arms ("6 containers on each side... the northern arm... the southern arm") joined at a shared apex/focus point, not as one continuous outline.
2. A single fused V-polygon with constant 30 m arm width and a true point apex is geometrically self-intersecting near the join (the two arms' inner edges cross before reaching a shared vertex) — visible in the design CAD render as literally interleaved/crossing tube bags at the join. Modelling the arms as two separate simple polygons avoids inventing a specific (unsourced) overlap geometry while still satisfying the "closed, non-self-intersecting" requirement for each part.

Construction steps:
1. Anchored the overall bounding envelope to S1's stated **80 m (alongshore) × 70 m (cross-shore)**, with the reef's rough centroid at **y ≈ 250 m offshore** (the best-supported distance-offshore figure).
2. From S1's Figure 3 (July 2013 survey), read the two arms as meeting near a shared point with a bend angle visually closer to **~90°** than a narrow symmetric V, with the northern arm appearing longer/more complete than the southern arm in the surveyed imagery.
3. Split the reef into a longer "northern" arm (chosen length 55 m, tilted ~15° from the alongshore axis) and a shorter "southern" arm (chosen length 45 m, tilted ~20° from the offshore axis), each given a width of 25 m (a mid-point between R8's pre-completion 30 m per-arm figure and no other independent as-built width figure), meeting near a shared reference point at (0, 275) [northern arm's inner corner] / (0, 250) construction origin.
4. Built each arm as a simple rectangle (four corners, walking the perimeter in order: near-inner corner → far-outer corner → far-inner corner → near-outer corner → close), using standard 2D rotation/perpendicular-offset vector math.
5. Verified with `shapely` (Python) that both resulting rings are `is_valid` and `is_simple` (non-self-intersecting), that the combined shoelace area (2,496.8 m²) matches the file's declared `area_m2` (2,497), that the combined bounding box (67.8 × 72.9 m) matches the file's declared `bbox_m`, and that the maximum pairwise vertex distance (86.2 m) matches the declared `max_dimension_m` (86).

This construction is explicitly a **modelled estimate of the as-built shape**, not a digitised trace of a scaled source drawing — see `open_issues` for the specific uncertainty this carries (arm-length split ±15 m; bend angle qualitative only). The overall bounding envelope it is anchored to (80×70 m) is, by contrast, a directly-quoted, cross-checked figure from an official source.

## Open issues

- Polygon vertex placement (bend angle, per-arm length split) is a qualitative visual estimate from PDF survey figures, not a calibrated pixel measurement — the overall bounding envelope is high-confidence, individual vertices are not (±15 m).
- Three/four disagreeing design-volume figures across sources (5,000 / 6,000 / 6,500 m³) remain unreconciled.
- Three disagreeing container-count/size descriptions (24 bags of 3.5×50 m per S4; bags "up to 60 m × 5 m diameter" per R2; 6 containers per arm = 12 total per S1/Mead 2011) remain unreconciled — unclear if these describe the same design stage.
- Gemini's three cited sources could not be independently located (the 1999 Black & Kerry/Mead design paper — already flagged as inaccessible in the card's own R13 — would likely be the best remaining source to check original design-stage numbers against, if it becomes accessible in a future pass).
- No stated compass bearing for the shoreline/alongshore axis was found in any source; the northern/southern arm naming (real, sourced) was used instead of an arbitrary convention, but this is not the same as a stated bearing in degrees.
- The July 2013 survey (used for shape/topology) postdates 2008 completion by 5 years, during which documented bag deflation "near the apex" occurred; an earlier (2008, as-completed) survey figure was not found, so some of the observed asymmetry could reflect subsequent degradation rather than (or in addition to) the as-completed geometry.
- **[ADDED 2026-09-25 verification pass]** A direct pixel-level zoom into S1's Figure 3 shows a small, separate reddish-orange (shallow) blob near the apex/junction of the two arms, detached from both main limbs by a deeper gap. No source text names a distinct third feature here — this is our own unsourced visual observation, of uncertain origin (possibly the repaired/replaced T4 bag documented near the apex, a small isolated remnant, or bathymetric noise). It is noted only because it is the one respect in which Gemini's "with Central Ridge" framing is not obviously wrong, even though Gemini's own specific three-limbed description still does not match what is visible.

## Verification 2026-09-25

Adversarial re-check of the 2026-09-25 research pass above, done independently: every cited source was re-fetched or re-downloaded live (WebFetch for the NZ Herald/Wikipedia pages; curl with a browser user-agent for nzgeo.com and for the BoPRC PDF, both of which reject a plain WebFetch — nzgeo.com with a 403, boprc.govt.nz with a TLS certificate error), the S1 PDF's full text was extracted programmatically (PyMuPDF) and grepped for every quoted figure, the two key S1 survey figures (Figure 3, p23; Figure 4, p26) were rendered directly from the PDF to PNG and viewed at high resolution (no re-fetch of a third-party image copy needed), all four already-cited raisedwaterresearch.com images were re-downloaded and viewed, the polygon geometry was independently recomputed with plain Python and shapely, and Gemini's own three source files (`reef_footprints.json`, `reef_footprint_scale_analysis.md`, `scale_sketch_photo_alignment_audit.md`) were read directly to check the fairness of the Gemini-comparison table.

**Findings**

1. **S1 (BoPRC 2014 report) — every quoted figure confirmed verbatim**, including the ones most load-bearing for this file: "the structure is approximately 80 m in shore-parallel length and about 70 m in cross-shore width" (p6); the footnoted "Scarfe (2008, p271) gives the reef dimensions as 79 m by 67 m" (p7); the original "wedge" dimensions "approximately 50 m in width ... and just less than 100 m in length" (p6); "approximately 250-300 metres from the shoreline" and "(pre-reef) water depths of 3.7-4.6 m" (p6); Scarfe's "approximately 3-5 m" baseline (p6); the design crest "about 0.4 m depth (below lowest astronomical tide ... equivalent to Chart Datum)" (p6); the July 2013 survey's "shallowest areas ... most typically in the range of 0.8-1 m below Chart Datum ... isolated area on the southern arm ... as shallow as 0.2-0.4 m below Chart Datum" (p22-23); "the 'as built' reef volume (2,800 m3) significantly less than the design volume (6,500 m3)" (p29); and "6 geotextile containers on each side ... 3 large crest containers and 3 smaller base-containers" (p7). No discrepancy found between this file's quotes and the live PDF.
2. **S1 Figures 3 and 4 — shape/topology and arm-naming independently confirmed by direct viewing.** Figure 3 (p23) is exactly as described: a georeferenced NZ-grid multibeam plan view (easting 376680-376820 m, northing 812820-812960 m — real metric map coordinates), depth legend 0.0 to -5.0 m "referred to Chart Datum", captioned "Most recent survey of Mount Reef conducted on 18 July 2013" and "Surveyed ... by University of Waikato & Discovery Marine Ltd". It shows a bent, clearly asymmetric two-limb structure (one roughly shore-parallel limb, one roughly cross-shore limb, meeting near a point at an angle visually close to 90°) — not a symmetric V. Figure 4 (p26, an oblique shaded-relief render with a north arrow, "January 2007, when the reef was 70% complete") independently shows the same bent topology, and its own caption explicitly labels the shore-parallel limb "the northern arm of the reef" — confirming this file's northern/southern-arm coordinate convention directly from the source image caption, not merely by inference.
3. **R2's four images — all re-downloaded and independently confirmed** to show exactly what this file says: the CAD render is a symmetric, interleaved-apex two-arm V with no scale bar; "Mount Reef Installed" is a colour-coded bathymetric plot (legend -1.8 to -7.4 m) showing the same bent/asymmetric two-limb shape as S1's own figures, independently corroborating them; the aerial photo shows only a faint, too-small-to-measure dark patch; the current-vector plot carries a 0.1 km scale bar and 0.2 m/s key but no reef outline.
4. **R8, R12, S4, R1 — all re-fetched live and confirmed verbatim**: R8's "V-shaped reef, with two 70m by 30m arms" / "240m off the beach"; R12's "about 250m offshore from the corner of Tay St and Marine Parade"; S4's full Kerry Black quote (apex-seaward, thickens-to-base description; "75 m from apex to base and almost 100 m across the base"; "6000 m3"; "24 huge cylindrical bags"; "250 m off the beach in 4.5 m of water"; "less than a metre" at low tide); and R1 (Wikipedia)'s "approximately 5,000 m3".
5. **Polygon geometry — confirmed with an independent recomputation.** Shoelace area on the file's own `polygons_m` = 2,496.815 m² (file states 2497); bbox = 67.8 × 72.9 m (file states 67.8 × 72.9); max pairwise vertex distance = 86.1998 m (file states 86); both rings are shapely-`is_valid` and `is_simple`. No discrepancy.
6. **Error found and fixed — invented arm attribution on the isolated crest-depth point.** The JSON's `crest_depth_m.notes` attributed S1's unattributed "at its highest point, the reef is approximately 0.2 m below chart datum" statement (S1 p6) to "the northern arm crest" — S1's text names no arm at all here. Corrected to remove the invented detail and to note this is one point in two datums (≈0.2 m CD / ≈0.1 m LAT), not a 0.1-0.2 m range, and that it may (unconfirmed — S1 never says so) be the same feature as the explicitly-named southern-arm isolated point elsewhere in the same report.
7. **Error found and fixed — a wrong "does not reconcile" arithmetic claim.** The original derivation table asserted that R8's pre-completion "70×30 m per arm" figure "does not by itself reconcile arithmetically with S1's ... 80×70 bounding envelope under a simple symmetric-V geometry" and that "no single symmetric half-angle satisfies both". This claim was independently checked by direct computation (Python, sweeping the half-angle 0-90° in 0.01° steps) and found to be **wrong**: a symmetric V with two 70×30 m arms at a half-angle of ≈21.9° from the shore-normal axis produces an 80.05×70.54 m bounding envelope — matching S1's 80×70 m almost exactly. Corrected in both files. This does **not** overturn the file's as-built-asymmetry conclusion, which is independently and directly supported by the visual evidence in finding 2 above (the multibeam survey figures actually show a bent, non-mirror shape), not by this now-corrected arithmetic argument — but the arithmetic argument itself should not have been presented as supporting evidence, since it pointed the wrong way.
8. **Gemini comparison table — confirmed fair.** Gemini's own `reef_footprints.json` "mount_maunganui" entry, `reef_footprint_scale_analysis.md`, and `scale_sketch_photo_alignment_audit.md` were read directly. Every Gemini number and quote attributed to it in this file's `gemini_comparison` (95×75 m; 4,800 m² gross / 2,455 m² net; crest depth 2.2 m / "-1.8 to -2.4 m LAT" / "Designed for -0.5m LAT"; ambient seabed depth 6.2 m; shape typology "Swept-Wing Inverted Y / Chevron with Central Ridge"; the three named, URL-less sources; and Gemini's own audit note about the gross-vs-net area gap) is an accurate, unedited representation of what Gemini's files actually contain. No unfairness found in either direction.
9. **Nuance added, verdict unchanged — a possible small third feature near the apex.** A direct pixel-level zoom into S1's Figure 3 shows a small, separate reddish-orange (shallow) blob near the apex/junction, detached from both main limbs by a deeper gap. Its origin is not established by any source text (S1/Mead 2011 consistently describe exactly two arms plus a shared apex, never a third "central ridge" member), so this is recorded as this file's own unsourced visual observation and an open issue, not as grounds to change `shape_type` or to agree with Gemini's specific three-limbed "Inverted Y" topology (which, even granting this blob, does not match what either survey figure actually shows).
10. **Everything else in the Derivation table, `images_relied_on`, and `references` — confirmed as stated**, with no further discrepancies found between this file's claims and the live/downloaded sources.

**Net effect on confidence**: the two corrected items were supporting arguments, not the file's headline numbers — the 80×70 m as-built bounding envelope, the 79×67 m academic cross-check, the two-arm bent/asymmetric topology, the 250 m offshore siting, the 0.8-1.0 m majority as-built crest depth, and the 2,800 m³/6,500 m³ as-built/design volumes are now independently re-confirmed at the primary source (including by directly viewing the cited survey figures, not just their captions) and remain **medium** confidence overall — held back from "high" only by the still-qualitative individual-vertex placement (bend angle, per-arm length split) inside the well-sourced bounding envelope, exactly as the pre-verification pass already and correctly flagged.
