# Narrowneck Reef (Gold Coast, Australia) — Footprint Provenance

Status: **verified 2026-09-25** (adversarial re-check of the footprint/scale pass; see "Verification 2026-09-25" section at the end for the check log and the two corrections it produced)

## Summary

Narrowneck Reef is a submerged, sand-filled geotextile-container ("mega sandbag") reef built 1999–2000 off the Narrowneck isthmus, Gold Coast, Queensland. Its plan-view shape is a **"split V" / dual-arm chevron**: two separate wishbone-shaped reef units — a **north arm** and a **south arm** — set side by side alongshore, joined by a **narrow central channel** across which a small submerged **weir** (two containers) was added in 2004. This is confirmed directly, with figures, by the reef's own primary monitoring paper (Jackson, Tomlinson, Corbett & Strauss, 2012 — already our card's [R4]), which this pass obtained and read in full for the first time (previously the card only had the abstract/journal page, not the PDF itself).

- **Shape:** dual-arm split-V / chevron, two polygons (north arm + south arm) either side of a central linking channel with a small weir.
- **Overall footprint (bounding box):** magnitudes **350 m and 600 m**, per Wikipedia [R9] — a second source [R8] gives a conflicting **450 m × 250 m**; both are retained per the project's reference rule. **Verification correction:** R9's exact sentence is *"The 350 × 600 m V-shaped artificial reef is located 200 m offshore..."* — it does **not** say which of the two numbers is alongshore vs cross-shore. This report assigns 600 m = alongshore / 350 m = cross-shore as its own inference (analogous to the L×W convention and to the wider-than-deep plan shape a splayed chevron implies), not because R9 states it. The two magnitudes themselves are solid (exact quote, re-fetched and re-confirmed this pass); only the axis assignment is inferred — see confidence note below and the Derivation table.
- **Area:** gross bounding-box area ≈ 210,000 m² (600×350); this report's own schematic net-polygon estimate ≈ **115,000 m²** (two arms, ~57,500 m² each) — both figures are **not published anywhere**; they are geometric consequences of the bounding-box numbers and this report's own schematic vertex placement (see Polygon construction below), not independently surveyed quantities.
- **Crest depth:** **1.0–1.5 m below low tide**, stated directly in the primary monitoring paper [R4, p.13] and consistent with the card's existing [R2][R3] figures; by 2017 (pre-2018-renewal) the crest had settled to **2.5–2.6 m** below low tide [R2] — the reef's crest depth has therefore genuinely varied ±1.5 m over its life and no single "current" number is safe without a date attached.
- **Distance offshore:** **≈200 m**, per Wikipedia [R9] (treated as the reef's shoreward/inner edge, since no source specifies exactly what point "200 m offshore" is measured to).
- **Confidence: medium** for the overall bounding-box *magnitudes* and crest depth (multiple, if partly conflicting, cited numbers); **medium-low** for the length/width *axis assignment* (600=alongshore vs 350=cross-shore is this report's inference, not stated by R9 — corrected/flagged this verification pass); **low** for the specific polygon vertices (schematic reconstruction, no legible scale bar in the source figures — see below).

## Sources and what each gave

- **[R4]** Jackson, A., Tomlinson, R., Corbett, B. & Strauss, D. (2012). *Long Term Performance of a Submerged Coastal Control Structure: A Case Study of the Narrowneck Multi-Functional Artificial Reef.* Coastal Engineering Proceedings, ICCE 2012. Already in the card as the citation-only journal-page reference; **this pass fetched the actual PDF** (journal page → WebFetch found the PDF download link → `curl` to scratchpad → read with the PDF-reading tool, all 13 pages). This is the single most important new source for this footprint pass. It gave:
  - The explicit statement that the 2004 design revision added **"a submerged weir between the two arms of the reef as well [as] flared wings... to mitigate against wave penetration through the channel"** (p.3) — direct textual proof of the **two-arm-plus-channel** topology.
  - Four plan-view figures showing this topology graphically: **Fig. 2** ("Reef Levels – Original and Revised Design," depth-contour plan views), **Fig. 4** ("Placement of Containers for Reef Maintenance," three time-slices 2002/2003, 2004, 2006, with the 2004 panel explicitly labeled "Flair [sic] wings" and "Establish weir"), **Fig. 5** ("Reef Bathymetry 9/6/11" — a black-outline plan trace of the whole structure), and **Fig. 7** ("Overlay of July 2011 Aerial Photo with containers placed during maintenance campaigns"). R9 separately states "more than 400" for the original build (a rounded, independently-sourced figure, consistent with ~408 but not an exact match). Total containers: ~450 pre-2018, ~534 after the 2018 renewal (+84, [R9]/[R14]).
  - No plan-view figure in this paper carries a legible metric scale bar or a north arrow in the copy obtained; all figures are depth-contour or container-placement sketches without a printed distance scale, so **no new precise metre dimension could be read off them** — see Polygon construction below for how this pass nonetheless built a schematic polygon.

## Polygon construction

**Coordinate frame:** origin = shoreline point nearest the reef's overall centroid; x = alongshore, positive toward north (the Narrowneck isthmus/Gold Coast shoreline here trends roughly N–S, per general regional geography — no source in this pass gives an exact shoreline azimuth in degrees, so "north" is a reasonable but not independently pinned convention); y = offshore, positive seaward; shoreline approximated as y=0.

**What is well-evidenced (high confidence):** the reef is **two separate elongated units side-by-side along the shore** (a "north arm" and a "south arm" in this report's own labelling — the primary paper just calls them "the two arms of the reef"), each roughly wishbone/chevron-shaped, joined by a **narrow central channel** that carries a small submerged weir (two containers, added 2004). This directly matches the card's pre-existing "split 'V' shape... paddling channel between the arms" language [R3][R9] and is visible in all four Jackson et al. 2012 plan-view figures.

**What is NOT resolvable from the sources obtained (low confidence, flagged explicitly):** none of the four plan-view figures in the primary paper carry a legible printed scale bar or a compass/north arrow in the copy this pass obtained, so (a) the exact vertex-by-vertex outline of each arm's "wishbone" shape, and (b) which axis (alongshore vs. cross-shore) each arm's two "horns" splay along, cannot be measured or confirmed with certainty. This report's polygon below is a **schematic reconstruction**, built as follows:
1. Took the bounding-box dimensions from [R9] (600 m alongshore × 350 m cross-shore) as the outer envelope.
2. Split the 600 m alongshore span into two ~250 m arms plus an estimated ~100 m central channel gap (channel width is this report's own assumption — not sourced; see Derivation table).
3. Within each arm's own local bounding box, drew a simple 13-vertex wishbone/chevron outline (narrow "elbow" near the channel, flaring to two wider "horns" toward the alongshore-outer end), by inspection of the general proportions visible in Fig. 5's plan trace (lobe-width : waist-width ratio roughly 3:1) — **not** a vertex-by-vertex digitization, since the source image resolution and lack of scale bar do not support that level of precision.
4. Mirrored the north arm's vertices in x to produce the south arm (assumes north/south symmetry, which is a simplification — the paper's Fig. 4 shows the two arms are similar but not perfectly identical, e.g. only one arm's containers are shown in some maintenance-campaign panels).
5. Did **not** draw a solid polygon for the central channel itself, since the paper describes it as an open "paddling channel" with only two containers (a minor weir) placed across it in 2004 — representing the whole channel zone as solid reef material would misrepresent it as a load-bearing structure it is not.

**Net effect:** the **topology** (two arms + open channel) and the **overall envelope** (600×350 m, from R9) are documented and sourced; the **specific vertex coordinates** of each arm are an illustrative schematic only, explicitly not a precise as-built trace, and the resulting net polygon area (≈115,000 m²) should be read as "the two arms occupy roughly half of their combined bounding box" rather than as a surveyed footprint figure.

## Gemini comparison

Gemini's candidate (`reef_footprints.json`, `id: narrowneck`): shape "Dual-Arm Elongated Lobed Chevron" split into an **"Inshore Lobe"** (y = 155–225 m) and an **"Offshore Lobe"** (y = 250–375 m) — i.e., two lobes stacked in the **cross-shore** direction, one closer to shore and one further out, at the same alongshore position. Dimensions given: 400 m × 220 m, gross area 75,000 m² (Gemini's own audit separately computes a net polygon area of 42,650 m² for this same shape — a ~1.8× gross/net gap Gemini's own audit flags but never resolves), crest depth 2.2 m, distance offshore 180 m (Gemini's own audit further flags this as "the inshore lobe... the outer ridge crest sits at y≈300m, and the outer toe extends to y=380m" — i.e. even Gemini's own internal audit does not treat 180 m as a clean, unambiguous figure), orientation "Lobes angled to ESE swell (090°–120°)."

- **Disagree — topology (the central finding of this report):** Gemini's inshore-lobe/offshore-lobe arrangement (two lobes at different offshore distances, same alongshore position) does not match the primary source. Jackson et al. 2012 explicitly and repeatedly (text + 4 figures) describes and draws **two arms side-by-side alongshore** (a north reef and a south reef), joined by a **central channel with a weir between them** — not a near-shore ring and a further-offshore ring. This report's polygon uses the alongshore-split arrangement instead. Gemini's own audit already senses something is off here (it flags the "180 m offshore" label as ambiguous), but it never revisits the underlying inshore/offshore-lobe topology itself, which is the actual root issue, not just the offshore-distance label.
- **Disagree — length/width:** Gemini's clean 400 m × 220 m matches neither of the two sourced figures we have (350×600 [R9] or 450×250 [R8]), and no citation ties this specific number to a specific page in Gemini's three listed sources (Black & Mead 2001, Jackson et al. 2012, ICM webinar) — this report could not find "400" or "220" stated anywhere in the primary paper obtained, nor on the ICM webpage (re-checked via WebFetch, no plan-view dimensions present). We keep [R9]'s 600×350 as the scaffold (with [R8]'s 450×250 as the disagreeing alternate), consistent with the card's existing, unreconciled-but-cited pair of numbers.
- **Disagree — area:** Gemini's own audit already flags its 75,000 m² (gross) vs. 42,650 m² (net) internal inconsistency without resolving it. This report does not adopt either number; our own schematic net-polygon area (≈115,000 m², from a *different* — alongshore-split — shape) is not directly comparable to Gemini's, since the underlying shapes differ.
- **Disagree — crest depth (2.2 m):** no source found supports exactly 2.2 m. The primary paper states 1.0–1.5 m as of 2012 [R4, p.13]; the card's [R2] separately gives 2.5–2.6 m as the *settled, pre-2018-renewal* state (2017). 2.2 m sits between these but is not itself derivable from either figure without an unstated averaging assumption, and Gemini's audit note ("originally designed at -1.0m LAT; settled to -2.5m; refurbished in 2018 to -1.5m") states three different numbers, none of which is 2.2 m either — so 2.2 m appears to be Gemini's own unexplained blend of its own three internally-stated values.
- **Partially agree — orientation:** the qualitative claim that the reef faces incoming ESE swell is consistent with the Gold Coast's general open-east-facing coastal geography and is not contested by anything in our sources; however, neither Gemini nor this report found a source stating the specific "090°–120°" figure, so the precise degrees remain unverified by either party.
- **Disagree — distance offshore framing:** Gemini's 180 m (inshore lobe) / ~300 m (offshore ridge) / 380 m (outer toe) tiered framing is a direct consequence of the rejected inshore/offshore-lobe topology and is not adopted; this report uses [R9]'s single 200 m figure (treated as the shoreward edge of the whole two-arm structure) instead.

## Open issues

- No plan-view figure with a legible printed scale bar was found for this reef in any source consulted (primary paper, ICM webpage, or the card's own image list), so the specific polygon vertices in this file are a documented schematic, not a survey-grade digitization — see Polygon construction above.
- The card's own pre-existing footprint disagreement (350×600 m [R9] vs. 450×250 m [R8]) remains unreconciled; this report did not find a third, tie-breaking source.
- This report assumes north/south mirror symmetry between the two arms for the schematic polygon; Jackson et al. 2012's Fig. 4 suggests the two arms may not be perfectly identical (only some maintenance-campaign containers are shown per arm per year), but the source resolution does not support characterizing the actual asymmetry.
- The exact shoreline azimuth at Narrowneck (needed to convert "alongshore = positive north" into a true compass bearing) was not found in any source consulted in this pass; the isthmus's roughly N–S trend is inferred from general regional geography, not a stated figure.
- Crest depth is genuinely time-varying (≈1.0–1.5 m as designed/2012 → ≈2.5–2.6 m by 2017 (settled) → an unstated but presumably shallower figure after the 2018 renewal); this file reports the 1.0–1.5 m figure as the representative "as-designed / primary-source-dated" value, but any use of a single crest-depth number for this reef should specify which date/state it refers to.
- The central channel's width (~100 m, used to space the two arms in the schematic polygon) is this report's own unsourced assumption, not a published figure.
- **(Added 2026-09-25 verification pass)** R9's "350 × 600 m" is an unordered pair in the source text; this file's assignment of 600 m to the alongshore axis and 350 m to the cross-shore axis is this report's own inference, not something Wikipedia states explicitly. If a future source clarifies the axis explicitly, the polygon's bounding-box orientation may need revisiting.
