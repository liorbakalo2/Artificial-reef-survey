# Southern Ocean Surf Reef ("Midds Reef") — footprint provenance

Companion file: `southern-ocean-surf-reef-albany.footprint.json` (status: verified, verified_on: 2026-09-25)

## Summary

- **Shape:** A curved, elongated rock-berm ridge — a crescent/"boomerang" that tapers to a narrow point at its shoreward end and bulges to its maximum width at the more offshore "take-off" end, per the Bluecoast/ASR design table's own description of a curved crest built "to maximise breaking and create waves that peel evenly along the reef."
- **Dimensions:** 165 m long (alongshore) × up to 110 m wide (cross-shore) — both figures are direct quotes from the City of Albany's own project page.
- **Area:** ~9,720 m² from our own estimated polygon (see Polygon construction). No source publishes an independent, surveyed footprint-area figure.
- **Crest depth:** −1.0 m AHD (≈0.74 m below mean low water, ≈1.34 m below mean high water).
- **Position:** ~140 m offshore, measured to the reef's landward toe, ~150 m north of the "Surfer's Beach" car park (final page text) / "350 m north of the Big4 Middleton Beach Caravan Park" (a different landmark used in the earlier Bluecoast/ASR concept table).
- **Confidence:** medium. Length, width, crest depth and offshore distance are directly quoted from the City of Albany's own page and corroborated by the card's existing 18-reference dossier. The precise polygon outline and area are low-confidence estimates: no as-built CAD drawing or bathymetric survey was found, and the one available plan-view figure is a pre-final concept sketch whose own printed dimension arrows do not share a consistent scale.

## Images/figures relied on

1. **`design.jpg`** (Bluecoast Consulting Engineers "Final Reef Design" table + plan diagram, legend "ASR Design"; hosted on and embedded in [R3]). **What was read off it:** all the design-table values quoted above, verbatim; the plan-view shape of the rock mound itself (a curved, elongated ridge, narrow at its shoreward tip); the location of the "Take-off area" (wider, more offshore) versus "Surfing area" (extends shoreward from it) functional-zone overlays. **Important correction made during analysis:** the figure also shows a fan of thin curved lines sweeping across both zones from a point near the shoreward tip. On first look these could be mistaken for the reef's own contour/outline; closer inspection (zoomed 3-6x with a pixel grid overlaid) shows they are a **wave-refraction/peel-line fan** — successive positions of the breaking wave crest as it peels from the take-off point — separate from the physical rock structure, which is only the narrower, darker band at the top of the figure. **Method, and a correction made during adversarial re-verification (2026-09-25):** the original pass pixel-measured the figure's own two printed dimension arrows and reported a ~37% mismatch between them (2.73 px/m vs 1.99 px/m, tips at (920,385)-(1195,505) and (555,375)-(825,445) in a 3×-zoomed working image) — that specific measurement could not be reproduced and appears to have been a pixel-identification error. Re-measuring directly on the original 1026×521 design.jpg at 8-20x zoom with a labelled pixel grid, and this time cross-validating against the figure's own printed scale bar (0 to 0.1 km, bottom-right corner — independently measured across both its 0-0.05 km and 0.05-0.1 km segments and found self-consistent at 0.815 px/m), gives: "140 m" arrow, endpoints at pixel (660,127)-(753,172), length ≈103.3 px ⇒ 0.738 px/m; "110 m" arrow, endpoints at pixel (793,125)-(863,163), length ≈79.6 px ⇒ 0.724 px/m. **Corrected finding:** the two arrows are mutually consistent with each other (only ~2% apart, not 37%), though both read a consistent ~10-12% shorter than the scale bar predicts for their labelled distances — more consistent with hand-placed/eyeballed dimension-line graphics than a genuine internal contradiction. The figure is still used only for qualitative shape/taper topology, never to derive scaled polygon vertices (out of caution, given that residual ~10% offset and the schematic nature of the annotations) — a softer, better-supported reason than the withdrawn "37% mismatch" claim. This correction does not change any reported dimension, since the 165×110 m envelope comes from the sourced text quotes, not from pixel tracing.
2. **RaisedWaterResearch construction-overview aerial photo** (May 2025, per its own EXIF caption; already in the card's images list, [R10]). **What was read off it:** a dark, elongated, diagonally-oriented submerged rock-berm shadow visible through the shallow water, running from a narrow point near the shoreline out to a wider zone offshore where a dredge/crane barge is working — independent, real-photo corroboration of the design figure's shoreward-taper / offshore-bulge topology. **Limitation:** the photo is captioned as showing construction *in progress* in May 2025, so it may capture a partially-built extent rather than the finished footprint, and no object of known size was identifiable to calibrate an absolute scale from the photo alone.
3. **ABC News aerial/drone photo** ("Specialised vessels came from around the world to build the Albany surf reef"; already in the card's images list, [R2]). **What was read off it:** a similarly oriented dark, turbid patch of disturbed water around the working barge, generally consistent with the RaisedWaterResearch photo, but too occluded by vessels and sediment plume to extract further shape detail.

## Polygon construction

1. **Bounding envelope.** Set to exactly 165 m (alongshore, x, from −82.5 to +82.5) × 110 m (cross-shore, y, from 140 to 250 m offshore), matching the sourced dimensions and the sourced 140 m landward-toe distance as the polygon's nearest edge.
2. **Outline shape.** Rather than the earlier misread of the wave-peel-line fan as the reef's edge, we drew a smooth, closed, 10-vertex crescent: it tapers to a point at each alongshore extreme (x = −82.5 and x = +82.5, both near y = 140-145-160-168, i.e. close to the shore-side baseline) and bulges outward on its offshore (convex/outer) edge to its widest point (y = 250) roughly a third of the way along its length from the +x end — the end we associate with the wider, more offshore "Take-off area" shown in the design figure — while its shoreward (concave/inner) edge stays relatively close to the 140-172 m band throughout. This directly encodes: (a) the design table's explicit "Curved" crest description; (b) the figure's placement of a wider "Take-off area" toward the more offshore end and a "Surfing area" extending from it toward the narrower, more shoreward end; (c) the tapering topology independently visible in both aerial construction photos.
3. **Validation.** Checked in Python with Shapely: the polygon is closed (first vertex = last vertex), `is_valid` is `True`, and `exterior.is_simple` is `True` (no self-intersections). Its bounding box is exactly 165.0 m × 110.0 m, its centroid sits at (5.08, 189.19), and its shoelace area is 9,720.0 m². Maximum pairwise vertex distance is 165.7 m (between the two tapered tips), consistent with "165 m long" being the dominant dimension.
4. **Coordinate convention.** x = alongshore, positive toward north — inferred from the design figure's apparent north-up orientation (dunes/land to the west, open water to the east, coastline trending roughly north-south), which is consistent with Middleton Beach's real-world shoreline trend, but the figure carries no printed north arrow, so this is an assumption (flagged in open_issues). y = offshore, positive seaward, shoreline at y = 0.
5. **What this polygon is not:** it is not a reproduction of any as-built survey, bathymetry, or CAD drawing — none was found publicly. The only available plan-view figure (design.jpg) is a pre-final "ASR Design" concept sketch whose own two dimension arrows scale inconsistently by ~37% when pixel-measured, so it could not be used for scaled vertex extraction even if we had wanted to trace it directly. The polygon should be read as illustrating the reef's general shape family (elongated, curved, crescent/boomerang, tapered at both alongshore extremes, bulging offshore near its "take-off" end) at the correct overall 165×110 m scale — not as a precise as-built outline.

## Open issues

- No true as-built plan-view CAD drawing or bathymetric survey was found publicly. The only plan-view figure available is a pre-final "ASR Design" concept sketch (per its own legend); CORRECTED 2026-09-25 — its two printed dimension arrows are actually mutually consistent (~2% apart) but both read ~10-12% short of the figure's own scale bar (see Verification), not the "~37% mismatch" an earlier draft claimed.
- The design figure mixes the physical reef outline with a separate wave-refraction/peel-line fan; some residual uncertainty remains about exactly where the rock structure's own edge lies.
- Both real aerial construction photos used are dated/captioned May 2025 (mid-construction), so may show a partially-built extent rather than the completed footprint.
- Exact polygon vertices are a low-confidence synthetic estimate constrained only by the sourced 165×110 m envelope and the qualitative crescent curvature; treat as illustrative of shape family, not a precise outline.
- area_m2 (9,720) is not a published figure and should be treated as low-confidence, on par with Gemini's own unsourced area figures.
- The x-axis "positive = north" convention is inferred from the design figure's apparent north-up orientation, not from an explicit stated bearing or printed north arrow.
- Distance offshore has a documented range across sources (120 m / 140 m / ~145 m) reflecting design evolution over time; 140 m (the current page's figure) was used as primary, consistent with the card.
- The 34,000 m³ reef-volume figure is from an earlier 2017-bathymetry concept-stage table on the same figure as the shape sketch and may not equal the final as-built volume; only the final tonnage (62,800 t) is well corroborated across sources.
- R15 (City of Albany, May 2023 FAQ PDF), read in full on 2026-09-25, gives an even earlier interim design of "up to 100 metres in width" (vs the final 110 m) and an interim footprint of "approximately 16,000m2" (vs our ~9,720 m2 estimate and Gemini's unsourced 13,500/12,363 m2) — consistent with the card's existing "design evolution over time" framing, now shown to extend to width and area as well as offshore distance. The current R3 page's 165/110/140 figures remain primary.
- No individual designer name is confirmed for this project as of the 2026-09-25 verification pass (see Verification section). Only the firm, Bluecoast Consulting Engineers, is sourced (R3; R15's "Consultant Project Team" list, which separately also names Muller Partnership as quantity surveyor, Keston Technologies for the business case, and UWA's Wave Energy Research Centre for peer review).
