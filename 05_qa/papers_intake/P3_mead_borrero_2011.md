# P3 intake - Mead & Borrero (2011), Multi-Purpose Reefs - A Decade of Applications
Intake 2026-10-10 (papers intake agent). Source: papers/Multi-Purpose Reefs - A Decade of Applications 2011.pdf (6 pages, text layer OK, no OCR needed).

**Full citation:** Mead, S.T. and Borrero, J.C. (2011). Multi-Purpose Reefs - A Decade of Applications. Proceedings of the 20th Australasian Coastal and Ocean Engineering Conference (Coasts and Ports 2011), ASR Ltd, Raglan NZ. https://search.informit.org/doi/10.3316/informit.715072832934166 (informit footer: downloaded 7 Oct 2026). Licence: not stated (private research copy).
**Nature of source:** a short review by the designers (ASR). It is an interested party for Cables-era design claims, Mount Maunganui, Opunake, Boscombe, Kovalam. It quotes volumes and results, but gives no depths, datums or crest levels. Page numbers below are the PDF/paper pages (1-6; the paper carries its own page numbers 1-6, identical).
**Paper-internal errors worth knowing:** p.3 says the 6,500 m3 Mount Reef "was never fully completed to specification (Figure 2)" - it means Figure 3. P3 spells "Pattiarachi" (= Pattiaratchi). P3 calls the first reef "Cable Station" and says it was built in 1998.

Card status legend: HAS = card already has it; LACKS = card does not have it; CONTRADICTS = card says something different (value in card given).

## Borth (borth-coastal-defence-reef) - references only
- Reference [2] Borrero and Mead (2010), "Borth Reef: Surfing Assessment Study", report for Ceredigion Council, September 2010 (p.6). Cited for two lessons: small structures reduce amenity (varying wave period and tide) and construction tolerances can be relaxed by orienting units perpendicular to the wave crest (p.6 lessons 2 and 3). Card: LACKS (no Borrero/Mead reference). That report is a candidate paper request (not requested yet).

## Lessons list (p.6) - general, useful for the Haifa section
1 Build fast (protracted construction changes seabed, displaces units). 2 Small structures: reduced amenity because of wave period and tide variation [Borrero & Mead 2010]. 3 Orient units perpendicular to the incoming wave crest to relax tolerances. 4 Consider other materials. Plus: scale (larger structures for consistent breaks, Shand 2011), manage public perception from the start, ensure funds including contingencies. Only 2 of 7 are "true" MPRs: Narrowneck and Kovalam (p.1). 4 of 7 have coastal-protection monitoring (p.4).
