Abstract
Sigandu is a coastal tourism area, which is located in the coastal zone of Batang District. Central Java Province.
This coastal area faces severe coastal erosion problem. It is indicated that erosion processes are occurred due to the
jetty construction of Batang Commercial Port. This construction cause disturbance of longshore transport
equilibrium. The objective of jetty construction is to trap the sediment, however, the classical problem of erosion
and accration take place in a short time, soon after the jetty construction finished. The local government of Batang
District becomes aware because of this erosion problem. Commenced on September 2014, the Marine and
Fisheries Affair initiates the solution by applying low-crested breakwaters structures using geotextile tube. One
month after the tube installation, rapid sedimentation occurred by the formation of salients and tombolos behind
the structures. Beach profile calculation have been carried out through the series of monitoring and cross section
measurement as well as visual observation. This paper presents results of monitoring beach activities. . The case
study is expected to be an example and applied for other coastal areas having similar coastal problems.

2. Theoretical Framework

2.1. LCB Concept

LCB is to be developed by cutting the structure height of conventional non-overtopping breakwaters, which is
directly proportional to the volume and cost reduction. Its protection capacity is as effective as conventional
emerging breakwaters. The LCB crest close to the still water level or slightly higher, and the main objective of
reducing their energy in the coastal area behind it (Buccino and Calabrese, 2007). Therefore, as the name implies,
LCB is a coastal protection structures that are always overtopped by the wave. Moreover, the structure may have
positive or negative freeboard and the armor layer is attacked by the breaking waves (Pascual et al., 2007). The
LCB structures are constructed in such a way that the waves breaking and overtopping of the structure and causing
wave energy dissipated. When the wave breaks over the structure, a certain amount of water is likely to be
pumped over it. The net discharge across the structure creates an accumulates mass in the lee side, behind the
structure which establishes a higher mean water level, referred to as piling-up. In a serial or discontinuous low-
crested breakwater, i.e. serial breakwaters that are interrupted by gaps, piling-up may generate strong currents
through the gaps. These currents are called as rip currents, they are responsible of scouring at the toe of the
structures, resulting in severe erosion along the gap, and may be dangerous to swimmers (Bellotti, 2004; Lamberti
et.al, 2007).

The LCB structure will be functioning properly if its existence is not only able to restore eroded beaches behind
the structure, but also able to widen the shore seaward with the formation of salient and tombolos. Similarly, the
waves energy that pass through over the structure will encourage sediment to accumulated on the beach. LCB
constructed using a gap in between, in terms of the pattern of the dynamics of the mass of water to give a physical
impact as follows: 1) reducing the piling-up behind the structure. Water mass accumulated behind the LCB, will
flow back towards offshore through the gap, therefore reducing the piling-up; 2) decreasing wave reflection
effects. Wave reflection due to the existance of LCB causes instability of the structure by scouring at the toe of the
structure seaward. However, the LCB structure installed in between, at the same time, generate strong currents in
the gap which called as rip currents. This strong water mass flow serves to reduce the piling-up behind the
structures. The flow velocity of the strong rip currents, potentially eroding and transporting coastal sediments
behind the LCB’s. In addition to causing coastal erosion behind the structure, strong currents can also cause scour
at the end of the LCBs in the gap, which can threaten the stability of the structure.
The flow velocity in the gap decreases exponentially with increasing gap width (Sulaiman et. al., 2014). Therefore,
for practical planning purposes and to get more effective low-crested breakwaters, it requires an optimal gap width,
which is able to minimize the piling-up, but also do not cause strong currents which can cause unwanted effects.

structure, d = depth of water, and Rc = h-d is freeboard, which is the difference between the crown level of
structures and still water level. One of the important parameters in determining the effectiveness of the breakwater
is submergence degree of the LCB, which can be explained by three parameters, namely: (1) the degree of
submergence or freeboard, Rc = h - d; (2) relative height of the structure = h/d; and (3) the ratio between freeboard
and water depth= Rc/d. Submergence degree is the ratio between the water depth of the structure height of the
LCB. For emerging breakwater, with crown level beyond the depth of water, the ratio is less than one. Relatively
high structure, which is the ratio between the heights of the structure to a depth of water (h/d) can also be used as a
non-dimensional parameter to describe the degree of submergence and the degree of exposure.

2.3 Use of Geotextile
Various construction materials may be used for coastal protection structures ranging from the conventional
(natural rocks and concrete blocks) until the leading edge of innovation outcomes such as geotextiles and gabion.
Among the materials the results of these innovations, geotextile is the most popular material as material protection
structure. An alternative material for coastal protection which have softer and cheaper such as sandbags geotextile
(geotextile sand containers, GSC) or in the market known as Geotube, widely used as a substitute for conventional
materials are expensive and rigid (Oumeraci and RECIO, 2010).

Geotextile tube is a big sack (width and height of approximately 2 m and 1.5 m) geotextiles made from woven
or non-woven, shaped like a bolster pillow filled with sand with a length of 20 m, and is used for coastal protection
and river (Fig. 5). Geotextile is now a substitute material for natural rockws has increasingly known in protecting
coastal erosion. This method of Coastal protection use geotextile tube material for offshore breakwaters, as
substitute material of natural rocks. The use of geotextile provides several advantages, namely: 1) The geotextile
material such as Geotube or Geotextile Sand Containers are available in the market with various sizes; 2) relatively
lightweight geotextile material which allows freight to a location relatively inexpensive; 3) a filler material such as
sand obtained at the project site; 4) a flexible response to the wave; and 5) can be installed by using a local power
without the need for special expertise.

pressure during the filling process and after Geotube fully charged. In addition, this material also should have the

does not come out. Geosynthetic ultimate tensile strength required for 200kn/m. Geotextile tube filler materials
such as sand and are assumed to have weight (γ) of 18kN/m3. Geotextile material is geosynthetic fibers produced

4. Results and Discussions

4.1. Beach Profile Changing

Changes of Sigandu coastline can be seen visually with increasing accreted sediment behind Geotube LCBs. The
advance of the coastline and the widening of the coast seaward approach the LCB structure, occurs along the beach
installed by the LCBs. The accumulation of sediment that make up a new coastline in the form of salient, visible
along the beach behind the LCB. In some spots and points of measurement, has formed some tombolos, a new
coastline due to sediment accretion that almost approach the LCB structures. Photographs of shoreline change
before and after the LCB installation are presented in Figure 8. To get more information regarding how much
changes after the installation of the tube, beach profile measurement has been conducted along the coast from the
jetty eastward. Beach profile measurement performed every 25 m.

4.2. Estimation of Accreted Sediment on the Beach

From the analysis of beach profile measurement results on 7th November, 2014, it is identified that
approximately 1 month after the LCBs installed in Sigandu Beach, the amount of sediment accreted behind the
LCBs is in average of 17,627 m3. Which the beach length of 280 m and its width of 75 m, so that the thickness of
accreted sediment is around of 84 cm.

The source of the sediment that accumulated on the beach behind geotube LCB comes from offshore-
onshore sediment transport and longshore sediment transport. In addition, the advantage of the LCB and serial
LCB’s compared to conventional emerging breakwater is that sediment transport towards the coast takes place
continuously all the time, either during high tides and low tide. At the time of a lower sea level, sediment may
able to enter the beach through a gap in between the LCB’s.

Figure 8. Sigandu Beach condition (a) before and (b) after LCB installation.

The presence of Sigandu beach situated on the upstream of Batang Commercial Port, during the east
monsoon the direction of the dominant sediment transport is coming from East to West. The jetty of Batang
Niaga Port at the East monsoon plays an important role in blocking of longshore sediment transport that coming
from the east side and the entire sediment transport westward accrated and halted by the jetty. Therefore, the
existence of the Niaga Batang Port is the most probably expected to become a major cause rapid accumulation of
sediment after the LCB Geotube installation.

Another source of sediments that accelerate the development of the coastline Sigandu is an abundance of
sediment in front of geotube LCBs, most likely derived from the results of dredging the harbor to the west coast of
Sigandu beach. Sediment that is transported by waves and currents over the LCB structure. The estimate is quite

4.3. Performance and condition of LCB with Geotube

Geotube LCBs installed since October 2014 as a whole showed a good condition. Geotube position at some point
experienced settlement, it appears at low tide with the surface of the tubes identify partially emerging and
submerging (See Fig. 9). Oblique position of some tubes are identified at the gap between the LCBs, both edges of
the left and right ends. This slanting is predicted due to the occurrence of scour at the toe end of tubes as a result of
strong currents in the gap that referred to rip currents. The strong currents arising in the gap stems from
unproporsional gap width compared to the length of the tubes. Reverse flow toward the sea comes from the
accumulation of water mass behind submerged structure, known as the piling-up. Heaps of water masses are then
turned toward the sea through the gap and generate a strong return flow. The flow velocity depends on the gap
width. A narrower gap generate stronger currents. Therefore optimizing the width of the gap needs to be
determined at the planning stage. Other toe scouring occurs in the toes of the tubes shoreward, this condition is
indicated by deepening of water depth behind the structures.

5. Conclusions and Recommendation
5.1. Conclusions

The pressence of the Batang Commercial Port plays an important role in seasonal changes of Sigandu
beach. The port act as a dam that traps longshore sediment transport.

Counter-measuring coastal erosion along Sigandu beach by installing geotube LCBs has shown
remarkable results. Previously eroded shoreline, in a relatively short time has established a new
coastline by formation salient and tombolos.

Placement of the LCB’s position, the length of structure, and the gap width between the LCBs
determine the successful of the LCB’s application.

Serial geotube LCBs play a major role in the process of sedimentation, transport of sediment into the
beach will continue through the gap even during low tide.

Geotube LCBs are designed as coastal protection efforts that can generate beach profile balance. Using
alternative materials such as geosynthetic has contributed to the coastal areas which lack of the
resources such as an adequate natural rocks.

The growing demand for tourist beaches and beaches aesthetic situation contributes greatly to the
application of LCBs as environmentally friendly, due to reduce visual effects.

5.2
Recommendations

•
Some improvements need to be made to the structure geotube LCBs include widening of the gap, in order
that the resulting flow is not too large impact on the local scour at the toe.
•
Due to material geotextile tube is fairly susceptible and attract the attention of tourists, special care must be
made such as warning signs and security assigned to protect it.
•
Rehabilitation of Sigandu beach through the installation of geotube LCBs, will be more effective when
combined with the planting of coastal vegetation, such as mangroves that will act as a binder of sediments
and natural protector against extreme conditions.
•
Protection of Sigandu beach on the east side should be carried out sooner in order to establish a single
system of Sigandu beach which will tend to be more stable and avoid the influence of reflection waves and
currents from adjacent reclamation land.
•
The successful effort in countermeasuring erosion problems of Sigandu beach, needs to be disseminated for
public references.
