## Numbers (page = PDF page)

Reef dimensions and bag (container) sizes
- p. 1 (abstract): reef built from geotextile containers "up to 60 m long by 7.2 m wide and 3.2 m high (container volumes of up to 900 m3)". OCR shows "900 m*"; read as m3, check on image.
- p. 1 (abstract): finished reef "peel fast and hollow for over 50 m in each direction".
- p. 2: breaking distance "a minimum of 50 m on each side" (Figure 2 design).
- p. 3: at 1:20 model, breaking ~30 m when scaled to full size (short crest, wave fell off the end). Scale 1:20 (p. 2), final model 1:21 (p. 3-4).
- p. 2: horseshoe breaking zone gradient 1:20 to 1:10 (cites Black and Mead, 2005).
- p. 4: built in 2 halves, 12 mega-containers per half; sizes range from "50 m long x 7.2 m wide x 3.2 m high (when filled) to 30 x 1.5 m wide x 1.0 m high (when filled)". OCR "30 x 1.5 m wide" is ambiguous (length of the smallest unit); check on image.
- p. 5: each half-reef (approx. 80 x 30 m) winched to the seabed on webbing and manta-anchors.
- p. 5: in 2006 the reef was "some 70% complete"; the unfilled gap is "some 60 m long by 6 m wide" through the middle.
- p. 5: pump: 8/6 Warman submersible; OCR "pumping some 70-80 m into the co..." is truncated; check on image (may be a volume or distance).

Design wave and tide data
- p. 2: design waves "wave height of 1-3 m; periods of 5-13 s with optimal period of 11 s; optimal wave approach angle of 50 deg +/- 20 deg True". Design basis from "4 years of offshore wave data" transformed to Tay Street (Figure 1).
- p. 1 (abstract): target surf waves "1-1.5 m straight-crested waves with 8-10 sec period".
- p. 4: best waves at mid tide on 1.5-2 m swells; clean swells 1.5-2.5 m at high tide; best waves near low tide when waves are below 1.5 m. Wave quality ranked 1-10 (Moores et al., 2005).
- p. 2: physical basin 8.5 m x 4.5 m, maximum water depth 0.6 m, wave heights up to 0.20 m (lab, not field).

- p. 4: Figure 7 axis label "MSL Water Level and Wave Height (m)". Mean sea level is the only datum named on the OCR text.
- No crest level (m), chart datum (CD) or other vertical datum is stated in the text. Crest level and datum must come from another source (for example the Mount Reef Trust or Mead 2005 report) or from the figures.

Volumes
