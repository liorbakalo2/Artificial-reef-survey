**Figure 1.** Plan shapes (upper part of each row) and cross-shore depth profiles (lower part) of the nine surf reefs whose outline is known, in order of construction; Opunake, Kovalam, Burkitts and Xala (Mexico) are not drawn because no outline could be traced. Colour identifies the reef (the same in all figures) and the symbol before each name its surf outcome (legend at the bottom: works, works rarely, mixed or disputed, does not work, unknown). All plans share one metric scale in x and y (1 cm = 66 m at print width; 100 m bar at lower right), are viewed looking seaward (unmirrored), and each outline is set at its distance from the MSL shoreline (thick line, y = 0) with arbitrary alongshore slots; the hatched shape is Borth's southern oval, a shore-parallel breakwater rather than a surf reef, and Bunbury is the 12 m Airwave bladder, torn on installation. Each profile is the section of the 3D reconstruction marked by the dashed line: a cross-shore cut through the part of the reef where the wave breaks and is ridden, perpendicular to the local shoreline (Albany: middle of the long -2 m AHD ridge along which the left-hander is ridden, not the -1 m take-off plateau; Cables: through the middle of the reef, turned 17.5° from the design frame to the shore normal; Borth: middle of the main ridge of the northern reef), and for the other six reefs the section whose crest depth best matches the master table; it is drawn at the plan's horizontal scale (window shifted per reef) with a vertical exaggeration of about 21:1; elevations are relative to MSL, the blue band is the tide range (spring range MLWS-MHWS; great diurnal range MLLW-MHHW at Pratte's; predicted annual range, about LAT-HAT, of the 2026 WA DoT/BoM predictions at Cables 1.14 m, Bunbury 1.20 m and Albany 1.38 m, centred on MSL; see the tide-range table in Appendix C), and C, T and G are the crest depth, the seabed depth at the seaward toe and the depth gap (T - C) read from the plotted section. They agree with the master table within 0.35 m except where the cut deliberately avoids the shallowest point: Albany (C 1.97 m on the ridge against the 1.0 m take-off crest; G 5.4 against 6.3 m) and Cables (C 1.92 against 1.55 m).
