**Figure 5.** Peel angle at breaking and the surfer skill that can use it. Upper panel: the surfer-skill table of Hutt, Black and Mead (2001, Table 3, as reproduced by Lewis et al., 2015, Table 3, and Barlow, 2013, Table 1.1): a surfer of rating r can ride waves peeling at an angle of at least 90, 70, 60, 55, 50, 40, 29 and 27 degrees (ratings 1 to 8; ratings 9 and 10 have no limit), within the breaking-wave height range of the rating printed beside each step (units not printed in the reproduction, taken as metres). Lower panel: for each reef the bar spans the peel angle at the site period for incident heights H0 = 0.5 to 3 m (dots; open dots: the wave does not break on the reef), obtained from the angle φ0 between the incident crest and the reef arm and refracted to the break depth by Snell's law without diffraction (vertical ticks: φ0, unrefracted); the large symbol is the site-wave value, its shape the outcome (circle works, triangle works rarely, diamond mixed or disputed, cross does not work, hollow square unknown) and its colour and the label colour the reef; + marks a case whose breaking height lies above the Hutt height range of its rating (Borth at H0 = 3 m reaches 62° but breaks at 3.2 m, above the 2.5 m limit of rating 3). The angle φ0 is assumed for five of the eight reefs (*; dashed: the alternative of 41° for Narrowneck); the grey zone below 25° approaches close-out (Mead and Black, 2001, as cited by Lewis et al., 2015); the red dashed line is Walker's (1975, p. 5) 30° minimum peel angle for a 6-ft plunging wave, a limit that rises with wave height, close to the rating-7 limit (29°) of Hutt et al.
