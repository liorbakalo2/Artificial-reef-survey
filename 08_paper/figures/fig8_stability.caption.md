**Figure 11.** Estimated wave loads on sand-filled geotextile tubes and on rock in the breaking zone (linear wave pressure on a rigid section; tearing itself is not modelled). $U$ is the largest upward wave force in a wave cycle divided by the submerged weight of the tube, with no wave pressure under the tube (sealed base, upper bound); it measures how strongly the vertical load on the fabric and seams alternates, not whether the tube lifts off, which has not been observed. $U_c$ is the same ratio when the wave pressure also acts under the tube (lower bound). $SI_c$ is the friction coefficient a tube needs in order not to slide [@bar2023]; sliding is expected where it exceeds the tube-on-sand friction of about 0.5. Each tube keeps its fabric circumference and takes the partly filled shape described by @bar2023 (flat base, rounded sides, elliptical top); the filling ratio FA is the sand area relative to a full circle. (a) Under depth-limited breakers ($H_b = 0.78\,h$, largest value over $T$ = 6, 9 and 12 s): $U$ per tube at FA 0.8 (solid, labelled with the filled width x height in m) and at FA 0.6 (thin dashed); full circles (FA 1.0) stay at $U \leq 0.11$ because they have almost no flat base. The grey band and the hatched band give the range of $U_c$ and of $SI_c$ over all tubes and periods at FA 0.8. The bars above show the depth to which the rock of the Albany, Cables and Borth reefs stays stable under depth-limited breakers (Hudson formula, $K_D$ = 2; † extrapolated beyond a 1:5 face). The grey strip marks the depths of surf-reef crest tubes; the blue strip marks the depth at the seaward foot (toe) of a hypothetical Haifa reef on the synthetic 1:50 profile. (b) $U$ for the Israeli tubes (theoretical diameter 5 and 3 m) in three storms, with the wave at the tube $H_{max} = \min(1.8\,H_s K_s, 0.78\,h)$; the shaded band reaches up to the value at FA 0.6. (c) The local $H_s$ (limited to $0.55\,h$) against the single-tube sliding limit of @vansteeg2011 at FA 0.8. The forces are calibrated against values read from @bar2023 (x 1.28 on $SI$ in the linear range; x 1.06 on $SI$ and x 2.25 on $SI_c$ near breaking), not computed with their numerical model; the real tube lies between the sealed and the open base, and single tubes are less stable than interlocking mounds.
