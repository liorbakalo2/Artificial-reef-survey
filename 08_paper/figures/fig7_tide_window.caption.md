**Figure 7.** Fraction of the lunar month during which a reef gives a surfable break, given that the swell is present, as a function of the tide range (spring range; great diurnal range at Pratte's; predicted annual range of 2026 at the Western Australian reefs Cables, Bunbury and Albany). Lines: a synthetic Haifa-type reef (1:50 bed, crest 10 m wide; ramps from 1:12 to 1:30 give identical fractions), mean over incident heights H0 = 0.6 to 1.5 m, for a crest 1.0 m below MSL (black) and for the crest depth that is best at each range (0.6 m, blue), at T = 7 s (solid) and 10 s (dashed); a break counts as surfable when it occurs on the ramp or crest with ξ_b < 2 and Y ≥ 1.6 (Battjes, 1974; Mead and Black, 2001), on a synthetic semidiurnal tide modulated between neap and spring. Symbols: the nine modelled reefs, each at its own tide range (ticks on the lower axis) and surfable fraction (mean over H0 = 0.5 to 2 m, best default section; colour and label: reef; shape: outcome, circle works, triangle works rarely, diamond mixed or disputed, cross does not work, hollow square unknown), joined by an arrow to the same geometry and swell under the Israeli tide (spring 0.40 m, neap 0.15 m; Rosen, 2001; shaded band). The Israeli tide leaves 0.87 (T = 7 s) to 0.98 (T = 10 s) of the no-tide value for the synthetic reef but does not raise the mean surfable time of the built reefs (mean change +0.01); it makes the break a function of wave height alone. The fractions are shares of the time with suitable swell, not days per year, because no wave climate is included.
