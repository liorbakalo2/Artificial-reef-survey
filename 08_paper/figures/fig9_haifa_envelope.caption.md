**Figure 12.** Design envelope for a surf reef at Haifa on a synthetic 1:50 bed (1:70 gives the same result; no Haifa bathymetry is available), as a function of crest depth below MSL and seaward ramp slope (crest 10 m wide, back slope 1:3). Cell colour is the fraction of the time the break is surfable under the Israeli tide (spring range 0.40 m; the same for ramps from 1:12 to 1:30 and falling with crest depth), the bar in each cell the share of waves ($H_0$ = 0.6 to 1.5 m, $T$ = 6 to 8 s, at MSL) that are too hollow, advanced or expert, spilling or gentle, or do not break on the reef (classes from $\xi_b$ and $Y$ after `\cite{battjes1974}`{=latex}, and `\cite{mead2001b}`{=latex}), and the contours the reef volume per 100 m of length (10,000, 15,000, 25,000 and 40,000 m³). The heavy box is the recommended envelope (crest 0.6 to 1.0 m, ramp 1:16 to 1:20, 15,000 to 25,000 m³ per 100 m, rock armour $D_{n50}$ of about 0.7 to 0.85 m); the dashed lines the water depths at which waves of $H_s$ 0.45 and 0.60 m ($H_{max}$ 0.6 and 0.8 m) were observed to break at Haifa (1.30 and 1.50 m), the bracket the observed depths at the peak from the smallest surfable shoulder to the largest sets (1.15 to 1.70 m) and the dotted line the take-off depth in borderline seas (about 1 m; field observations by the author, Appendix G; Section 7.1); a seasonal rise of the surrounding bed is not shown, because it does not change the break while the crest stays above the sand (Section 7.4). The symbols show the built reefs by crest depth and mean seaward slope on their surf cut (Palm Beach, Cables, Albany on its −2 m ridge, Narrowneck second crest line; Boscombe, 1:3.1, is off the scale), coloured and labelled per reef, with the outcome as symbol shape. The model is one-dimensional linear shoaling with $\gamma$ = 0.78 and no refraction or focusing, so the envelope is a first screening and needs a Haifa wave climate and a two-dimensional wave model or flume test.
