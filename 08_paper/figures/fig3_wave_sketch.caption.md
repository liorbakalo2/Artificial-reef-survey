**Figure 3.** Shoaling and breaking over a long gradual ramp (a, Palm Beach, Gold Coast) and over an abrupt rise (b, Boscombe), both along the crest line of the reef's 3D model, drawn at one horizontal scale and with the same incident wave (H0 = 1.0 m, T = 10 s, normal incidence, mean sea level). The overview panels are vertically exaggerated 5:1 (sinusoids are linear shoaling, H = H0 Ks, Dean and Dalrymple, 1991; the wave breaks where H = γ d with γ = 0.78, McCowan, 1894, as given by Weggel, 1972), and the dashed boxes are enlarged 1:1 to show the breaker at two instants, onset (t0, filled) and about 1 s later (t0 + 1 s, dark dashed; the crest has moved c_b × 1 s = √(g h_b) × 1 s ≈ 4.2 m shoreward), with the breaking depth h_b = H_b/γ = 1.78 m (H_b = 1.39 m in both panels) and a 1.8 m standing surfer. In (a) the crest sharpens and the front turns near-vertical at t0, and 1 s later a thin lip (about 0.15 H_b) has been thrown onto the trough ahead, enclosing an air pocket (plunging; Galvin, 1968; Peregrine, 1983); in (b) the steep front does not overturn and 1 s later it collapses from its base, which surges ahead over the crest as a thin run-up sheet with little foam (collapsing/surging; Galvin, 1968; Battjes, 1974). Landward of the break the surface drops to a trough about 0.38 H_b below the local mean level and then rises over the back of the previous, already broken wave to its bore front one wavelength (L_b = T √(g h_b) ≈ 42 m) ahead; the mean water level (dash-dot) dips by about 0.05 H_b at the break (set-down) and rises toward the shore (set-up, about 0.25 H_b at the shoreline, an order-of-magnitude value) after Longuet-Higgins and Stewart (1964) and Bowen et al. (1968); the trough shape follows Dean and Dalrymple (1991). Lip, foam and bore shapes are schematic; heights and positions of the break are model values. A comparable depth gap from the crest to the seabed at the seaward toe (5.49 m and 4.51 m) spread over an 89 m ramp (mean slope 1:17) gives a plunging breaker with surf similarity ξ_b = 0.56 (Battjes, 1974) and a "very high" breaking intensity, Y = 2.05 (Mead and Black, 2001), whereas the 13 m ramp (1:3.1) gives a collapsing front with ξ_b = 3.00 and Y = 1.05†; under Boscombe's own typical sea (0.5 m, 8 s) ξ_b = 1.60 and Y = 1.30†. † Outside the 1:9 to 1:40 slope range of the Mead and Black (2001) fit, i.e. extrapolated; the blue band is the local spring tidal range (MLWS to MHWS) and the dashed red lines the Israeli spring range of 0.40 m (Rosen, 2001).
