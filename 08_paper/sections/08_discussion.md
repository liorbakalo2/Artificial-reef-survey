Two cases deserve comment. The Pratte's testimony separates the two failures that (a) and (c) run together: the witnesses describe a wave that almost never broke, and one says the bags were intact when removed (Dan Harmon, 2026; grade D), whereas the removal report found fabric that "could be torn by hand" (Blacka et al., 2013, pp. 116-117). Either way the reef failed first as a wave, through its size and its site in the swell shadow of Catalina Island, which supports (a) more directly than (c). And Albany, the best wave in the sample, has a crest line rising at about 1:4 (Table 2) and is "intermediate-to-advanced on most days" (Mercer, 2025): a steep face can give a good but demanding wave when the reef is large and the swell long, so (f) describes the hollowness, not the value, of the wave.

## 8.2 Limitations and what would change the conclusions

The sample is small (8 to 13 reefs), and the outcome classes are this review's readings of sources that are mostly by the designers; every association is descriptive and confounded, because the large reefs are also the recent, better-funded rock reefs. No reef has a published wave climate at the structure, so the surfable fractions are shares of the time with a suitable swell, not days per year, and the reported surfable days are not comparable (Section 3.6). The geometry is idealised for six of the nine models, the incidence angle is assumed for five of eight reefs, and the wave model is one-dimensional linear shoaling with $\gamma$ = 0.78 and no focusing; the Mead and Black (2001b) fit is extrapolated beyond 1:9 at Boscombe, Bunbury and Albany. Three key sources (Hutt et al., 2001; Mead & Black, 2001a; Black & Andrews, 2001) were used only as reproduced by others. The Haifa analysis rests on a synthetic profile and on unpublished observations of the author.

The conclusions would change with (1) measured surfable-day counts tied to a wave record at any reef; (2) a small reef that works, or a large rock reef whose wave fails, which would weaken (a); (3) a controlled before/after survey showing a reef-made salient at a site without nourishment, against (b); (4) a geotextile reef that survives a decade in the breaking zone without renewal, against (c); and (5) Haifa breaking depths that confirm the early breaking observed by the author ($H_s/d$ of about 0.35 to 0.40), which would move the recommended crest from 0.6-1.0 m to 1.2-1.5 m below MSL.

## 8.3 Overall rating of the built reefs

To answer the question that motivates the review, which reefs have delivered what they promised, this review rated each reef on three criteria: (A) surf quality, the quality and consistency of the wave reported or computed; (B) safety and longevity, the structural record of the material and any hazard to users; and (C) sediment and storm protection, any measured effect on the beach or on storm damage. Each criterion is scored from 1 (very poor) to 5 (very good), with 3 for mixed or neutral evidence, including "no measurable effect"; ? means that no usable evidence exists, and * that a signal exists but cannot be attributed to the reef. The reefs are ranked by A, with B as tie-break; C does not affect the rank, because it is mostly unknown. The scores are this review's assessment, based on the evidence cited in Sections 3 to 6 and Appendix C, graded A (measured), B (engineering report or thesis), C (journalism or surfer media) and D (forum or single anecdote) (Table 7).

Albany is the only reef that clearly delivers a high-quality wave, but with safety problems: reported injuries led to the repositioning of 18 rocks within a year of opening (Sheil, 2026). Hard rock reefs (Cables, Burkitts, Palm Beach, Borth) score best on B, with no structural failure; geotextile and inflatable reefs score worst on B, with six of the seven at the minimum. Criterion C is unknown or confounded for every reef: no score above 3 survives attribution, and the only negative evidence (Mount Maunganui) comes from a press report. The rating thus repeats the central result of this review: a surf reef that works is large, built of rock and given a long ramp, and its benefit is surfing rather than beach protection.
