---
title: "Artificial surf reefs: a review of built projects, surf quality, sediment transport and durability, with implications for the Israeli coast"
author: "Lior Bakalo"
date: "October 2026"
subtitle: "Course paper, SM-52080001 Introduction to Ocean Engineering (Prof. Nitai Drimer)"
abstract: |
  Artificial surf reefs are promoted as structures that create surfable waves, protect beaches and enhance habitat. This review compiles the 13 surf reefs built or reshaped since 1997 with traced plan shapes, nine three-dimensional reconstructions and traceable sources. Coastal-engineering theory (shoaling, breaker index, Iribarren number, breaking intensity, peel angle) applied to profiles cut through the surfed part of each reconstructed reef shows that the crest depth sets which waves break and the ramp how: ramp length and mean seaward slope rank with the surf outcome (Spearman $\rho$ = 0.93 and 0.77, $p$ < 0.05), crest depth does not ($\rho$ = 0.39, $p$ = 0.36), and over similar depth gaps a 1:17 ramp gives a plunging, surfable wave and a 1:3 rise a collapsing one. Surfer testimony indicates that the reef must also stay shallow just shoreward of the break for the wave to hold its shape. Reefs that work are large (25,000 m³ or more), because volume buys ramp length; the three that do not work are 4,900 m³ or less. No reef offers a beginner's wave. Where surveys can test it, the shoreline response is 2 to 6 % of standard salient predictions; on the Gold Coast it cannot be separated from nourishment. Six of seven geotextile or inflatable reefs suffered major damage within a few years, none of four rock reefs. In a three-criterion rating (surf quality; safety and longevity; sediment and storm protection), only Albany clearly delivers a high-quality wave, with safety problems; the other two reefs that work (Palm Beach, Burkitts) do so only in a narrow range of conditions; rock reefs rate best on durability, and the sediment benefit is unknown or confounded everywhere. At Haifa, Israel, the 0.4 m spring tide would make the wave depend on wave height, not on the tide; a rock reef with a crest 0.6 to 1.0 m below mean sea level, a 1:16 to 1:20 ramp, a crest that stays shallow shoreward of the break and 15,000 to 25,000 m³ per 100 m is proposed for testing.

  **Keywords:** artificial surf reef; multi-purpose reef; surf quality; breaking intensity; peel angle; submerged breakwater; shoreline response; geotextile sand containers; Israel; Haifa
