---
title: "Artificial surf reefs: a review of built projects, surf quality, sediment transport and durability, with implications for the Israeli coast"
author: "Lior Bakalo"
date: "October 2026"
subtitle: "Course paper, SM-52080001 Introduction to Ocean Engineering (Prof. Nitai Drimer)"
abstract: |
  This review compiles the 13 artificial surf reefs built or reshaped since 1997 and reconstructs nine of them in three dimensions from traceable sources. Profiles through the surfed part of each reef show that the ramp, not the crest depth, decides the surf: ramp length and mean seaward slope rank with the outcome (Spearman $\rho$ = 0.93 and 0.77, $p$ < 0.05), crest depth does not ($\rho$ = 0.39). Reefs that work hold 25,000 m³ or more; those that fail hold 4,900 m³ or less. The shoreline response is 2 to 6 % of standard salient predictions, and six of seven geotextile or inflatable reefs were badly damaged within a few years, against none of four rock reefs. For Haifa, Israel (spring tide 0.4 m), a rock reef with a crest 1.0 to 1.2 m below mean sea level, a 1:16 to 1:20 ramp and about 14,000 to 20,000 m³ per 100 m is proposed for testing.

  **Keywords:** artificial surf reef; multi-purpose reef; surf quality; breaking intensity; peel angle; submerged breakwater; shoreline response; geotextile sand containers; Israel; Haifa
