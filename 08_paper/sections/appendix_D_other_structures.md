# Appendix D. Other reefs and geotextile structures named by Bar and Drimer (2023) and Israeli geotube projects

This appendix lists the structures that are not among the 13 surf reefs of the survey (Section 3, Appendix C) but are named in the survey paragraph of Bar and Drimer (2023, p. 2) or are relevant to the Israeli coast. None of them was built as a surf reef. Of the eleven projects and studies named in Bar and Drimer (2023), three (Narrowneck, Pratte's Reef and Mount Maunganui) are among our 13 reefs; the other eight are D.1 to D.8: four (D.1 to D.4) are geotextile-tube (Geotube) shore-protection breakwaters, one (D.8) is a cliff-foot geotube scheme, one (D.5) is a nature-based breakwater, and two (D.6, D.7) are design studies; D.9 to D.11 are further Israeli geotube projects. Each entry gives type, year, place, purpose, material and size, outcome, and the verification level of this review. The levels are: READ (the cited text was opened and the number checked), ABSTRACT (only an abstract or catalogue record was reached), and VENDOR (a manufacturer page, treated as promotional). Nothing is stated here that is not in the cited source; where the source is silent the entry says "not found". All sources were read or checked on 15 October 2026. Page numbers of conference papers and of the Israeli audit reports are PDF pages.

Bar and Drimer (2023) is not a survey. Their review of built projects is one paragraph; the remainder of the paper develops and validates a two-dimensional boundary-element wave-flume model for geotextile tubes as submerged impermeable structures. They borrow the statement that "most of the projects have failed" from the Raised Water Research website, without a list or a definition of failure (Bar & Drimer, 2023, p. 2). Of the eight built projects they name, only three (Narrowneck, Pratte's Reef and Mount Maunganui) are among our 13 reefs; ten of our reefs are not mentioned by them.

## D.1 Young-Jin beach, Gangneung, Gangwon-do, east coast of Korea

*Type:* submerged double-lined geotextile-tube detached breakwaters for shore protection. *Year:* not stated; the paper (2006) already reports 12 months of monitoring, so construction was about 2004-2005 (derived; assumption). *Purpose:* severe erosion threatened scenery and public property; a tetrapod breakwater would have needed heavy equipment, so geotubes were chosen as a cheaper and faster alternative (Oh & Shin, 2006a, pp. 1-2). *Material and size:* woven geotextile of at least 700 g/m^2^; sand fill with D~10~ = 0.50 mm; tubes filled by cutter-suction dredge (about 500 m^3^/h). Double-lined tubes in about 3.0 m of water, 90-100 m from the shoreline, each tube 50 m long and 1.8 m in diameter, four twin units at 20.0 m spacing covering 240 m of shoreline; model tests led to a design with the crest at the water surface (Oh & Shin, 2006a, pp. 2-4). *Outcome:* the shoreline advanced about 2.4-7.6 m seaward within 12 months and sand accumulated around the tubes; at one benchmark the shoreline advanced 5.3 m in three months and then re-eroded, which the authors attribute to changed tidal flow; seaweed colonised the tubes after one year. The authors state that long-term effectiveness cannot be judged yet because of severe storms (Oh & Shin, 2006a, p. 4). *Cost and contractor:* not stated. *Relevance:* a zero-freeboard soft structure at about 3 m depth with local re-erosion and no durability record beyond 12 months. *Citations:* Oh and Shin (2006a), read; the journal version (Oh & Shin, 2006b) is paywalled and was not read. *Level:* READ (conference paper).

## D.2 Yucatan coast (Progreso area), Mexico

*Type:* geotextile tubes as low-crested or semi-submerged breakwaters for beach restoration. *Year:* field installation 2005 (4 km of tubes by September 2005), further sites 2005-2007 (Alvarez et al., 2006; González Leija et al., 2014). The paper that Bar and Drimer (2023) cite is a laboratory flume study, not the field project. *Purpose:* shoreline retreat of about 1 m/yr and more over 15 years; federal beach rehabilitation programme from 2001 (Alvarez et al., 2006). *Material and design inputs:* woven polypropylene tubes with a scour apron; breaking wave height below 1.0 m (93 % of the time), T~z~ 4-8 s, bed slope 0.1-0.5 %, tide range 0.90 m, littoral drift 60,000 m^3^/yr, D~50~ = 0.30 mm; the designers computed the Iribarren number, found spilling breakers, and set the crest so that high-tide freeboard avoids reflection (Alvarez et al., 2006). *Outcome:* mixed. "In some cases shore protection and beach stabilization was achieved ... but in other places the results have been far from the expected", with a downdrift shadow effect; tubes were set at about -0.3 m relative to MSL (González Leija et al., 2014). The laboratory study found that the transmission coefficient depends on the ratio of submergence depth to incident wave height, and that tubes crowned well below mean water level make waves "steepen and break more violently". *Cost and contractor:* not found. *Citations:* Alvarez et al. (2006), González Leija et al. (2014) read; Alvarez et al. (2007) not read. *Level:* READ (magazine article and one-page poster).

## D.3 Al Aqah Beach, Fujairah, UAE

*Type:* geotextile-tube groins plus a seaward submerged breakwater, for beach nourishment and erosion control; not a surf reef. *Year:* after cyclone Gonu (2007); manufacturer's case-study timeline 2010-2014. *Purpose:* erosion over "several kilometers"; rip-rap groins had sunk under repeated wave action (ACE Geosynthetics, n.d.). *Material and size:* geotextile tubes of 8.6-17.2 m circumference and 52-77 m length, two arms of 200 m each, filled with sand in situ. Crest depth, fill height and volume: not found. *Outcome:* the manufacturer states that erosion is "well-controlled"; no independent monitoring found. *Cost:* not found. *Citations:* ACE Geosynthetics (n.d.), read; Chien et al. (2013, 2014) were not accessible (abstract level only). *Level:* VENDOR.

## D.4 Sigandu Beach, Batang Regency, Central Java, Indonesia

*Type:* low-crested geotextile-tube breakwaters for shore protection. *Year:* installation from September 2014; paper 2015. *Purpose:* an eroding tourist beach, attributed to the jetty of the Batang commercial port; the beach receded about 5 m in 8 years (Solmax, 2023). *Material and size (vendor):* tubes of 9.5 m circumference, design filled height 1.5 m, length 25 m, on a scour apron with two pilot tubes; number of tubes, crest depth and offshore distance not found. *Outcome:* about one month after installation, rapid sedimentation formed salients and tombolos behind the structures (Sulaiman et al., 2015, abstract). A later paper by the same group (Sulaiman et al., 2017) may report the longer-term outcome; it was not read. *Citations:* Sulaiman et al. (2015) abstract only (full text returned HTTP 403 and was not bypassed); Solmax (2023) read. *Level:* ABSTRACT and VENDOR.

## D.5 Living Breakwaters, Tottenville, Staten Island, New York

*Type:* eight partially submerged breakwaters of armour stone and ecologically enhanced concrete units, with "reef ridges" and "reef streets" for habitat, plus sand replenishment; coastal risk reduction and ecology, no surfing. *Year:* construction from 2021; completion announced in September 2024. *Size and cost:* 2,400 linear feet of breakwaters, USD 111 million, designed to reduce the waves that reach buildings and roads to below three feet (about 0.9 m) (Hochul, 2024). *Outcome:* too recent for performance data. *Design papers:* Baker et al. (2018), abstract only; the conference paper that Bar and Drimer (2023) cite for this project was not accessed. *Relevance:* a rock-plus-eco-concrete submerged breakwater, comparable to the ECOncrete applications in Israel. *Level:* READ (press release).

## D.6 Portuguese multifunctional artificial reef (design study)

A preliminary design of a multifunctional reef (coastal protection and surfing) for the Portuguese coast (ten Voorde et al., 2009), with a follow-up on the influence of the parameters in the vertical plane (Antunes do Carmo et al., 2011). Neither paper was read and no evidence that the reef was built was found. *Level:* bibliographic only.

## D.7 Babilonia beach, Guardamar del Segura, Alicante, Spain (design study)

A design method for multifunctional artificial reefs applied to the eroding Babilonia beach on the Spanish Mediterranean coast (López et al., 2016). The abstract, as relayed by the University of Alicante record, states that the reef would give a surfable wave "60 % of the time" for surfers of low to intermediate skill. This figure was not confirmed in the full text (the open-access download returned HTTP 401 and was not bypassed) and must not be quoted before it is. The paper is the closest published analogue to a microtidal Mediterranean surf reef with a surfer-skill statement. *Level:* ABSTRACT.

## D.8 Ashkelon North geotubes, Israel

*Type:* submerged geotextile-tube structure of 48 tubes in five clusters at the foot of the eroding Ashkelon cliff; urgent, temporary marine protection until permanent breakwaters are approved. It was the first geotube project in Israel (Bar & Drimer, 2023, p. 2, citing a 2016 design report by Gouaud that could not be located online). *Dates:* the State Comptroller of Israel (2020, PDF pp. 71, 83) gives July 2017 as the contractor's start and September 2019 as completion; the Government Company for the Protection of Mediterranean Sea Cliffs (MCCP, 2026) lists April 2018 to April 2019; Rinat (2019) says the tubes were laid in summer 2018. *Size:* 48 tubes (State Comptroller of Israel, 2020); 50 according to Rinat (2019); tube dimensions are not stated in any source read. The figures of 10 dunam and 4 m height in the author's notes were not found in any source. *Cost:* about NIS 11 million (State Comptroller of Israel, 2020); NIS 10 million (Rinat, 2019); NIS 10.5 million in the author's notes. *Outcome:* "several of the geotubes were damaged and torn shortly after being laid in the sea, after the winter storms, and had to be repaired" (State Comptroller of Israel, 2020, p. 71). In early February 2019 the contractor reported severe damage of unknown cause and halted laying; the last storm "was not so severe" (Rinat, 2019). The number of destroyed tubes is not stated in any source read; the count "15 of 48" in the author's notes and the count "17 by storms, 2 by boats" in an earlier repository dossier are unsupported. During maintenance in late 2021, 12 geotubes were replaced (State Comptroller of Israel, 2024, p. 48). The 2024 report states that the geotube marine protection in Ashkelon North "collapsed" (p. 29) and that geotubes were considered "less effective" there, with five rock breakwaters planned (p. 53). The same report lists general geotube threats: poor design assumptions, poor filling, and damage by propeller blades, anchors and divers' knives (p. 9). *Surfers and ecology:* the local surfers' organisation opposed the tubes (Rinat, 2019). No monitoring results were found. *Netanya:* the author recalled torn tubes at Netanya in winter 2019; no source shows a geotube built or contracted at Netanya, whose cliff protection is rock breakwaters (State Comptroller of Israel, 2020, p. 71; 2024, p. 48; MCCP, 2026). The timing the author remembers (first winter after laying) matches Ashkelon North, so Ashkelon is the site used in this review. *Level:* READ (two official reports and one press article).

## D.9 Ashkelon South (area 39), Israel

Geotubes were not built. A December 2017 committee decision limited stage 1 to sand feeding; from September 2023 the company has piloted an "armoured geotube" (fabric with metal conductors for low-voltage mineral precipitation), designed in response to "the low durability and survivability of the fabric sleeves used at Ashkelon North" (State Comptroller of Israel, 2024, pp. 54, 55, 57). *Level:* READ.

## D.10 Hof HaTzuk ("Cliff Beach"), Tel Aviv

A submerged geotube breakwater was planned (tender 2019), opposed by surfers and an environmental group, and stopped at tender stage in 2022 on the Ministry's recommendation; it was not built (State Comptroller of Israel, 2024, p. 11; the 2019 press reports were not re-opened). *Level:* READ for the 2024 report, secondary for the rest.

## D.11 Ashkelon geotube trial, 2005-2006

An earlier trial by the Ashkelon Economic Company (about NIS 1.7 million, tubes of about 20 m) suffered storm damage and vandalism according to a Haaretz report of 6 April 2006 as recorded in an earlier dossier of this review; the report was not re-opened and the entry is not verified. *Level:* secondary.

## D.12 Types named without a site

Bar and Drimer (2023, p. 1) mention floating breakwaters, Bragg breakwaters, submerged reef balls and an artificial mangrove-root system as alternatives that "may present potential advantages in cost, hydrodynamics and environmental features". No project data are given and none of the underlying papers was read.
