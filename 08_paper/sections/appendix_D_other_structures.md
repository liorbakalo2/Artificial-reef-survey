# Appendix D. Other reefs and geotextile structures named by @bar2023 and Israeli geotube projects

This appendix lists the structures that are not among the 13 surf reefs of the survey (Section 3, Appendix C) but are named in the survey paragraph of @bar2023 [p. 2] or are relevant to the Israeli coast. None of them was built as a surf reef. Of the eleven projects and studies named in @bar2023, three (Narrowneck, Pratte's Reef and Mount Maunganui) are among our 13 reefs; the other eight are D.1 to D.8: four (D.1 to D.4) are geotextile-tube (Geotube) shore-protection breakwaters, one (D.8) is a cliff-foot geotube scheme, one (D.5) is a nature-based breakwater, and two (D.6, D.7) are design studies; D.9 to D.11 are further Israeli geotube projects. Each entry gives type, year, place, purpose, material and size, outcome, and the verification level of this review. The levels are: READ (the cited text was opened and the number checked), ABSTRACT (only an abstract or catalogue record was reached), and VENDOR (a manufacturer page, treated as promotional). Nothing is stated here that is not in the cited source; where the source is silent the entry says "not found". All sources were read or checked on 15 October 2026. Page numbers of conference papers and of the Israeli audit reports are PDF pages.

@bar2023 is not a survey. Their review of built projects is one paragraph; the remainder of the paper develops and validates a two-dimensional boundary-element wave-flume model for geotextile tubes as submerged impermeable structures. They borrow the statement that "most of the projects have failed" from the Raised Water Research website, without a list or a definition of failure [@bar2023, p. 2]. Of the eight built projects they name, only three (Narrowneck, Pratte's Reef and Mount Maunganui) are among our 13 reefs; ten of our reefs are not mentioned by them.

## D.1 Young-Jin beach, Gangneung, Gangwon-do, east coast of Korea

*Type:* submerged double-lined geotextile-tube detached breakwaters for shore protection; an example of submerged geotube shore protection, not a surf reef [@oh2006b, p. 879]. *Year:* not stated; the paper was received in March 2005 and reports 12 months of monitoring, so construction was about 2004-2005 (derived; assumption). *Purpose:* severe erosion threatened scenery and public property; a tetrapod breakwater would have needed heavy equipment, so geotubes were chosen as a cheaper and faster alternative [@oh2006a, pp. 1-2]. *Material and size:* woven geotextile; sand fill; tubes filled by cutter-suction dredge. Two lines of tubes as detached breakwaters in about 3.0 m of water, 90-100 m from the shoreline; each tube 9.5 m in circumference (3.5 m diameter), 50 m long and 1.8 m effective height (85 % fill), eight tubes in four twin units covering 240 m of shoreline, with a scour apron. Model tests favoured zero water depth above the crest, and the tubes were installed 1.0 m below the water surface, as deep as possible to spare the scenery and reduce navigational risk [@oh2006b, pp. 892-893]. *Outcome:* the shoreline advanced about 2.4-7.6 m seaward within 12 months and sand accumulated around the tubes (up to 10.4 cm at the monitored point); at benchmark BM1 the shoreline advanced 5.3 m in three months and then re-eroded, which the authors attribute to changed tidal flow; seaweed colonised the tubes after one year; scour developed on the seaward side and between the tubes [@oh2006b, pp. 879, 893-894]. The model tests found the two-line zero-freeboard layout stable up to 5.0 m significant wave height [@oh2006b, p. 894]. *Cost and contractor:* not stated. *Relevance:* a zero-freeboard soft structure at about 3 m depth with local re-erosion and no durability record beyond 12 months. *Citations:* @oh2006b (journal version, read in full; journal pages) and @oh2006a (conference version). *Level:* READ.

## D.2 Yucatan coast (Progreso area), Mexico

*Type:* geotextile tubes as low-crested or semi-submerged breakwaters for beach restoration. *Year:* field installation 2005 (4 km of tubes by September 2005), further sites 2005-2007 [@alvarez2006; @gonzalezleija2014]. The paper that @bar2023 cite is a laboratory flume study, not the field project. *Purpose:* shoreline retreat of about 1 m/yr and more over 15 years; federal beach rehabilitation programme from 2001 [@alvarez2006]. *Material and design inputs:* woven polypropylene tubes with a scour apron; breaking wave height below 1.0 m (93 % of the time), T~z~ 4-8 s, bed slope 0.1-0.5 %, tide range 0.90 m, littoral drift 60,000 m^3^/yr, D~50~ = 0.30 mm; the designers computed the Iribarren number, found spilling breakers, and set the crest so that high-tide freeboard avoids reflection [@alvarez2006]. *Outcome:* mixed. "In some cases shore protection and beach stabilization was achieved ... but in other places the results have been far from the expected", with a downdrift shadow effect; tubes were set at about -0.3 m relative to MSL [@gonzalezleija2014]. The laboratory study found that the transmission coefficient depends on the ratio of submergence depth to incident wave height, and that tubes crowned well below mean water level make waves "steepen and break more violently". *Cost and contractor:* not found. *Citations:* @alvarez2006, @gonzalezleija2014 read; @alvarez2007 not read. *Level:* READ (magazine article and one-page poster).

## D.3 Al Aqah Beach, Fujairah, UAE

*Type:* geotextile-tube groins plus a seaward submerged breakwater, for beach nourishment and erosion control; not a surf reef. *Year:* after cyclone Gonu (2007); manufacturer's case-study timeline 2010-2014. *Purpose:* erosion over "several kilometers"; rip-rap groins had sunk under repeated wave action [@acegeosyntheticsnd]. *Material and size:* geotextile tubes of 8.6-17.2 m circumference and 52-77 m length, two arms of 200 m each, filled with sand in situ. Crest depth, fill height and volume: not found. *Outcome:* the manufacturer states that erosion is "well-controlled"; no independent monitoring found. *Cost:* not found. *Citations:* @acegeosyntheticsnd, read; @chien2013 was not accessible (abstract level only); @chien2014 as reported by @bar2023, full text not accessed. *Level:* VENDOR.

## D.4 Sigandu Beach, Batang Regency, Central Java, Indonesia

*Type:* low-crested geotextile-tube breakwaters (LCB) for shore protection, not a surf reef. *Year:* installation from September 2014 (October 2014 in the performance section); paper 2015 [@sulaiman2015, pp. 510, 518]. *Purpose:* an eroding tourist beach, attributed to the jetty of the Batang commercial port that blocks the westward longshore transport [@sulaiman2015, pp. 511, 518]. *Material and size:* geotextile tubes of about 2 m width, 1.5 m height and 20 m length as a generic type; 14 units installed (the text lists 5 + 5 + 5 tubes in three groups, which is 15), three gaps of 20 m, about 75 m from the coast, set at about mean sea level (no other crest depth is given) [@sulaiman2015, pp. 514-516]. *Outcome:* about one month after installation, salients and tombolos had formed behind the tubes, with on average 17,627 m^3^ of sediment accreted over 280 m of beach 75 m wide (about 84 cm thick); the authors attribute the speed of the accretion largely to the port jetty and to dredged sediment, so the amount cannot be credited to the structure alone. Some tubes had settled and tilted at the gap ends from toe scour by gap currents, but the structures were in good condition overall [@sulaiman2015, pp. 517-518]. A later paper by the same group [@sulaiman2017] was not read. *Cost:* not stated. *Citations:* @sulaiman2015 (read in full; journal pages); the vendor page @solmax2023 gives tube dimensions of 9.5 m circumference, 1.5 m filled height and 25 m length. *Level:* READ.

## D.5 Living Breakwaters, Tottenville, Staten Island, New York

*Type:* partially submerged breakwaters of armour stone and ecologically enhanced concrete units (ECOncrete armour units and tide pools), with "reef ridges", "reef streets" and crenulated crests for habitat, plus a shoreline restoration; coastal risk reduction and ecology, no surfing, in Raritan Bay at Tottenville [@marrone2019, pp. 1044-1045]. *Year:* design-modelling paper 2019; construction from 2021, completion announced in September 2024 [@hochul2024]. *Design and modelling:* the paper describes the modelling programme only (wave transformation, GENESIS shoreline change, FUNWAVE wave attenuation, Delft sediment transport, FLOW-3D, and 1:20 and 1:80 physical models); it gives no breakwater dimensions, crest levels or costs. The design target was wave heights below 1 m (3 ft) in the lee under 100-year conditions with 0.8 m of sea-level rise, which the final design achieved in the model; the shoreline model showed erosion mitigated, with accretion near the most exposed neighbourhood, and the sediment model showed lower bed shear stress at the shoreline and negligible effects on the navigation channel [@marrone2019, pp. 1048, 1053-1054]. These are model results, not measurements. *Size and cost:* 2,400 linear feet of breakwaters, USD 111 million [@hochul2024]. *Outcome:* too recent for performance data. *Other design paper:* @baker2018, abstract only. *Relevance:* a rock-plus-eco-concrete submerged breakwater, comparable to the ECOncrete applications in Israel; it shows how a balance between longshore transport and storm protection is modelled. *Level:* READ (paper); size and cost come from a press release.

## D.6 Portuguese multifunctional artificial reef (design study)

*Type:* preliminary design of a multifunctional artificial reef (MFAR; coastal protection and surfing) for the northwest coast of Portugal, a delta-shaped submerged breakwater of geotextile sand containers on a platform; a design study with numerical and physical tests, and no evidence that it was built [@tenvoorde2009, pp. 69, 78]. *Year:* paper 2009; a follow-up on the parameters in the vertical plane is @antunesdocarmo2011 (not read). *Size and crest depth:* delta angle 66 degrees, side slope 1:10 with a side length of at least one-fourth of the local wavelength, valid only for a sea bed flatter than 1:50; crest submergence 1.5 m for a design breaking wave of 2.0 m, with a minimum reef height of 2.0 to 2.5 m and a platform depth of 4.0 m; in the worked example, 175 m from the delta base and 250 m from the apex to the shore, the ride is about 82 m [@tenvoorde2009, pp. 69, 75-76, 78]. *Position:* the distance from the apex to the undisturbed shoreline should exceed 1.5 times the natural surf-zone width, after @ranasinghe2006 (Figure 8a) [@tenvoorde2009, pp. 77-78]. *Outcome and cost:* none (not built); no cost is stated. *Relevance:* the main design guideline in the literature for a submerged surfing reef that must also protect the coast. *Level:* READ.

## D.7 Babilonia beach, Guardamar del Segura, Alicante, Spain (design study)

*Type:* design method for a multifunctional artificial reef, applied as a design study to the eroding Babilonia beach, Guardamar del Segura (Alicante), on the Spanish Mediterranean coast; not built as far as the paper states [@lopez2016, p. 910]. *Year:* paper 2016. *Design:* a V-shaped reef of geotextile containers, 60 m in the cross-shore direction, 63.4 m wide at the top, internal angle 24 degrees, offshore slope 1:10, in 4 m of water with the crest at 0.7 m depth, breaking zone about 100 m from the coast and a maximum ride of about 65 m; it is combined with a 240 m dike at the -5 m contour, 329 m from the shoreline, to form a hemitombolo at the marina mouth [@lopez2016, pp. 916-919]. *Predicted outcome:* the authors' model predicts a maximum beach accretion of 33.2 m over 270 m of beach, and a wave for beginner to intermediate surfers; they state that the reef "could be used 60 % of the time" by surfers of low to intermediate skill, the 60 % being the occurrence of the surfing wave conditions in the study area [@lopez2016, pp. 910, 921-922]. These are modelled predictions, not measurements. *Cost:* volume about 1,920 m^3^; the cost is estimated from the unit costs of other reefs (43 to 332 EUR/m^3^) at 82,560 to 637,440 EUR, most probably about 403,200 EUR [@lopez2016, p. 921]. *Relevance:* the closest published analogue to a microtidal Mediterranean surf reef; the authors note that a microtidal site keeps the reef operable and rarely hidden [@lopez2016, p. 921]. *Level:* READ.

## D.8 Ashkelon North geotubes, Israel

*Type:* submerged geotextile-tube structure of 48 tubes in five clusters at the foot of the eroding Ashkelon cliff; urgent, temporary marine protection until permanent breakwaters are approved. It was the first geotube project in Israel (`\cite[p. 2]{bar2023}`{=latex}, citing a 2016 design report by Gouaud that could not be located online; not verified). *Dates:* the State Comptroller of Israel (`\citeyear[PDF pp. 71, 83]{statecomptroller2020}`{=latex}) gives July 2017 as the contractor's start and September 2019 as completion; the Government Company for the Protection of Mediterranean Sea Cliffs [@mccp2026] lists April 2018 to April 2019; @rinat2019 says the tubes were laid in summer 2018. *Size:* 48 tubes [@statecomptroller2020]; 50 according to @rinat2019; tube dimensions are not stated in any source read. The figures of 10 dunam and 4 m height in the author's notes were not found in any source. *Cost:* about NIS 11 million [@statecomptroller2020]; NIS 10 million [@rinat2019]; NIS 10.5 million in the author's notes. *Outcome:* "several of the geotubes were damaged and torn shortly after being laid in the sea, after the winter storms, and had to be repaired" [@statecomptroller2020, p. 71]. In early February 2019 the contractor reported severe damage of unknown cause and halted laying; the last storm "was not so severe" [@rinat2019]. The number of destroyed tubes is not stated in any source read; the count "15 of 48" in the author's notes and the count "17 by storms, 2 by boats" in an earlier repository dossier are unsupported. During maintenance in late 2021, 12 geotubes were replaced [@statecomptroller2024, p. 48]. The 2024 report states that the geotube marine protection in Ashkelon North "collapsed" (p. 29) and that geotubes were considered "less effective" there, with five rock breakwaters planned (p. 53). The same report lists general geotube threats: poor design assumptions, poor filling, and damage by propeller blades, anchors and divers' knives (p. 9). *Surfers and ecology:* the local surfers' organisation opposed the tubes [@rinat2019]. No monitoring results were found. *Netanya:* the author recalled torn tubes at Netanya in winter 2019; no source shows a geotube built or contracted at Netanya, whose cliff protection is rock breakwaters (`\cite[p. 71]{statecomptroller2020}`{=latex}; `\citeyear[p. 48]{statecomptroller2024}`{=latex}; `\cite{mccp2026}`{=latex}). The timing the author remembers (first winter after laying) matches Ashkelon North, so Ashkelon is the site used in this review. *Level:* READ (two official reports and one press article).

## D.9 Ashkelon South (area 39), Israel

Geotubes were not built. A December 2017 committee decision limited stage 1 to sand feeding; from September 2023 the company has piloted an "armoured geotube" (fabric with metal conductors for low-voltage mineral precipitation), designed in response to "the low durability and survivability of the fabric sleeves used at Ashkelon North" [@statecomptroller2024, pp. 54, 55, 57]. *Level:* READ.

## D.10 Hof HaTzuk ("Cliff Beach"), Tel Aviv

A submerged geotube breakwater was planned (tender 2019), opposed by surfers and an environmental group, and stopped at tender stage in 2022 on the Ministry's recommendation; it was not built (`\cite[p. 11]{statecomptroller2024}`{=latex}; the 2019 press reports were not re-opened). *Level:* READ for the 2024 report, secondary for the rest.

## D.11 Ashkelon geotube trial, 2005-2006

An earlier trial by the Ashkelon Economic Company (about NIS 1.7 million, tubes of about 20 m) suffered storm damage and vandalism according to a Haaretz report of 6 April 2006 as recorded in an earlier dossier of this review; the report was not re-opened and the entry is not verified. *Level:* secondary.

## D.12 Types named without a site

@bar2023 [p. 1] mention floating breakwaters, Bragg breakwaters, submerged reef balls and an artificial mangrove-root system as alternatives that "may present potential advantages in cost, hydrodynamics and environmental features". No project data are given and none of the underlying papers was read.
