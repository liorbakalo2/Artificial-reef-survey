# Appendix F. Author's leads and their status

Before the analysis, the author listed eight leads from personal experience of built reefs and of the Haifa shore, labelled (a) to (h) in Section 1.3. They were used only as questions to investigate; the Discussion (Section 8) rests on the computations and the evidence, not on the leads. This appendix keeps track of each lead: what the evidence of Sections 3 to 7 says about it, and whether it is consistent with published work or new to this review (verdicts by this review). Lead (h) is split into two parts, the small tide (h-A) and mobile sand at Haifa (h-B). Six leads are supported, at least in direction; two are partly supported; and (h-B) is split, with the burial risk supported and the sand trap not supported as a sand source. None is contradicted as a whole.

Two cases deserve comment. The Pratte's testimony separates the two failures that (a) and (c) run together: the witnesses describe a wave that almost never broke, and one says the bags were intact when removed (`\cite{danharmon2026}`{=latex}; grade D), whereas the removal report found fabric that "could be torn by hand" [@blacka2013, pp. 116-117]. Either way the reef failed first as a wave, through its size and its site in the swell shadow of Catalina Island, which supports (a) more directly than (c). Albany, the best wave in the sample, first appeared to contradict (f): on the automatic profile through its −1 m take-off plateau it rose at about 1:4 and fell in the collapsing band. On the cut through the long −2 m ridge where the wave is ridden (Figure E1, Appendix E) it has a 99 m ramp at 1:19.8 and plunges like Palm Beach, so it supports (f); it is still "intermediate-to-advanced on most days" [@mercer2025], consistent with its median vortex ratio of 1.75 ("extreme").
