# 2 Methods

## 2.1 Sources and the traceability standard

For each reef, the published design and monitoring papers, official reports, survey data sets, georeferenced aerial and satellite images, press articles and reef-specific web pages were collected. Peer-reviewed and agency reports were preferred; designers' papers [for example @mead2011] are treated as the account of an interested party; press, surfer media and web databases are used only for facts that nothing else gives, and are named as such. Every number in the data set carries the value and unit, a full citation, the place where it was read (page, table, figure or file), the method, and a status: *sourced* (read in a cited source), *derived* (computed in this review from sourced inputs, a traced plan shape or a reconstructed three-dimensional model), *assumed* (flagged and numbered in Appendix A) or *unknown* (searched for and not found; no value given). Confidence is rated high, medium or low by a fixed rubric (Section 2.3; Appendix A.0). Where a statement rests on a second-hand source, for example @hutt2001 as reproduced by @lewis2015, the text says so. Values from secondary compilations whose cited sources could not be fetched and confirmed were not used. Costs were converted to US dollars at the World Bank annual-average exchange rate of the price year and to 2024 prices with the US consumer price index [@worldbank2026]; both steps are derived and inherit the uncertainty of the price year.

## 2.2 The reef sample

The sample is the 13 surf reefs built (for Burkitts, reshaped) with surfing as a primary or joint purpose, including those that failed or were removed and the unverified Xala reef (Mexico, 2025-26). It was compiled from @mead2011, @blacka2013, the web database of Raised Water Research and press reports, and is not claimed to be exhaustive. Borth is included because its northern mound was shaped as a surf reef; its southern oval is a breakwater. Of the eleven projects named by @bar2023, three are in the sample; the other eight, and the Israeli geotube projects, were not built as surf reefs and are described in Appendix D.

## 2.3 Plan shape, three-dimensional reconstruction and datums

Each outline was traced on the best available evidence, in a fixed order of preference: a measured survey of the structure, then a drawing of the built version, then a photograph. Satellite and aerial images (Esri World Imagery and dated Wayback releases; `\cite{esrind}`{=latex}) were georeferenced from their tile bounds, drawings were scaled from a scale bar or a known dimension, and each outline was checked on a second image and against the dimensions in the text. Confidence is high if the structure is clearly visible on a georeferenced image or an as-built survey and the dimensions agree with the text to about 10 %, medium for a scaled design drawing, a partly visible structure or a 10-25 % disagreement, and low if the shape is inferred from text or a sketch. Outlines were obtained for nine reefs (Figure 1).

A three-dimensional model was built only where the plan, the crest level and the seabed depth were each sourced. The seabed is taken from the best measured data (soundings, lidar, multibeam read from a published figure, a coastal elevation model), otherwise from a chart (Garmin Navionics SonarChart, datum tested; `\cite{garminnavionicsnd}`{=latex}) or from design contours. The reef surface is the surveyed surface or a loft from the crest level over the traced toe outline down to the seabed; its volume is a mound envelope with voids included, not the fill volume. All heights are relative to mean sea level (MSL) through an explicit datum chain per reef (Appendix C, note 1), and only the tidal planes of the nearest standard port are used, for example those of @maritimesafety2026 for the Gold Coast. Nine reefs were modelled (Appendix A, Table A1; Appendix B).

## 2.4 Depth profiles, depth gap and ramp length

The depth profile is the shore-normal section of the model through the shallowest crest cell. For Albany, Cables and Borth this automatic line did not cross the part of the reef where the wave breaks and is ridden, or was not shore-normal; for these reefs the profile is taken along a surf cut through the surfed part of the reef, perpendicular to the local shoreline (Albany: the middle of the long −2 m AHD ridge rather than the −1 m take-off plateau; Cables: through the footprint centroid, normal to the fitted shoreline; Borth: 10 m south of the centreline, a judgement because no source locates the break). The cuts are drawn in Figure E1 (Appendix E) and their reasons recorded in Appendix A; volumes and footprints do not depend on the cut. The crest depth $C$ is the depth of the shallowest crest point below MSL (negative if the crest is above MSL); $T$ is the seabed depth at the seaward toe of the traced outline; the depth gap is $G = T - C$. The ramp length $L$ is the horizontal distance from the toe to the crest edge (the seaward-most cell within 0.25 m of the column maximum), and the mean slope $G/L$ is quoted as 1:$n$. Six reefs (Narrowneck, Cables, Albany, Palm Beach, Borth, Boscombe) have a ramp from audited geometry; the Pratte's ramp is assumed, and Mount Maunganui and Bunbury rest on hand estimates or a stand-in surface (Appendix C, note 2). Sourced crest depths are used where they exist (Cables, Palm Beach, Albany).

## 2.5 Computations

Sections 4 to 7 apply standard coastal-engineering relations to the profiles; formulas, thresholds and the status of every input are given where they are used. In outline: linear shoaling and Snell refraction for deep-water heights of 0.5 to 3 m and periods of 6 to 14 s at three tide levels, with breaking at $H = \gamma d$ ($\gamma$ = 0.78), the breaker type from the surf-similarity (Iribarren) number [@battjes1974], the breaking intensity from the seabed gradient [@mead2001b] and the peel angle against surfer skill (`\cite{hutt2001}`{=latex}, as tabulated by `\cite{lewis2015}`{=latex}) (Section 4); shoreline predictors [@black2001; @ranasinghe2006] and wave transmission, compared with the surveyed seabed and shoreline change in the lee and at control sections, and longshore transport by the CERC formula [@usace2002] as an order of magnitude (Section 5); single-tube sliding and uplift after @bar2023 with a calibrated estimate, and rock stability by the Hudson formula (Section 6); and the same calculations with the Israeli tide (Section 7). Associations with outcome are Spearman rank correlations with permutation $p$ values.

## 2.6 Limitations of the method

Every value is traced to a cited source, and the design choices made in this review are listed in Appendix A. Readings from figures are by eye or by calibrated scales and carry the stated reading error. The original texts of @hutt2001, @mead2001a and @black2001 were not read; their content is used as reproduced by later sources, and this is stated where it matters. No new field data were collected; the author's field observations at Haifa (Appendix G) are used only in Section 7 and are labelled there. The consequences of the small sample, the unknown wave climates and the idealised seabeds are discussed in Section 8.2.

## 2.7 Authorship and use of AI tools

The author selected and supplied the primary sources (papers, reports, survey data, imagery and surfer testimony collected by the author, for example viewer comments on online videos), defined the research questions and the hypotheses to be tested, and directed the analysis. AI tools assisted with source extraction, data compilation, computation and drafting. Every source added by the AI tools was validated by the author, who also reviewed and confirmed all text, data, calculations and figures, including by comparing the three-dimensional reconstructions with the published figures and imagery (a worked example is given in Appendix A, Section A.14).
