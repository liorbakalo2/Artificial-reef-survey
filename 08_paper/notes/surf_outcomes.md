# Surf-quality evidence, reef by reef (13 reefs)

Prepared 2026-10-16 for the review paper (Lior Bakalo). Scope: does each reef make a surfable wave, how often, for whom, what shape,
with quotes and full citations (section 7). Only this file was written. Scratch: /tmp/claude-0/paper/surf/ (text extracts of Rendle 2016,
Mead et al. 2010, Mead & Borrero 2011, Blacka et al. 2013, Borrero & Nelsen 2003, Prenzler et al. 2022; fetched web pages).

**Conventions.** Source status: [READ] = text read this session (PDF or web page); [CARD] = taken from the reef card
(02_research/reefs/<slug>.md|json, fetched 2026-09-24 by earlier agents; page not re-read now); [SEC] = quoted inside another source.
Pages: Rendle (2016) is given as printed page (= PDF page - 3); NSW review as printed page (= PDF page - 12). DERIVED = computed
here or by the analysis agent from sourced inputs. INFERRED = our reading, not a source statement. "Author's observation (L. Bakalo)" =
Lior's own notes, not a citable source. Evidence grade: **A** measured/monitored data; **B** engineering report or thesis (designers are an
interested party, marked "designers"); **C** journalism or surfer media; **D** forum comment or single anecdote. Gemini material was not used.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **The England reef is Boscombe (Poole Bay, Dorset), confirmed** by several lines: an independent evaluator (Davidson 2010 via Rendle
   2016), the designers (Mead et al. 2010), a survey of 721 UK surfers (Rendle 2016) and surfer media (section 3). It is the only purpose-built
   surf reef in England that we found; Borth is in Wales. Lior's memory is right in substance, with one correction: the sources say the wave suited
   bodyboarders *and advanced stand-up surfers*, not bodyboarders only (Davidson 2010 via Rendle 2016; Mead et al. 2010 designers).
2. Reefs that are reported to work for stand-up surfers are the rock reefs: Palm Beach (2019), Albany (2025), Burkitts (1997), Cables
   (partly). The sand-container reefs are rated minor or partial (Narrowneck, Boscombe) or failed (Mount Maunganui, Opunake, Kovalam later).
   Small n and confounded by construction faults, so this is a pattern, not a test.
3. Surfable days are always a minority of days and are bounded by a swell window and a tide window. Reported counts range from 1-5 a year
   (Pratte's) to about 140 (Cables, Albany) and are not comparable (section 5).
4. No surveyed reef was built or reported as a beginner wave. Skill demand is intermediate at best (Albany "intermediate-to-advanced",
   Boscombe "intermediate to expert", Boscombe design levels 3-6). This supports Lior's insight (g).
5. Steep, hollow or plunging waves are reported at Boscombe, Mount Maunganui, Kovalam (barrels) and Narrowneck before settlement. Steepness
   alone does not decide failure: Albany has a similar face (about 1:3) and works, but is a hard wave with injuries (insight (f)).

## 3. The England reef: Boscombe, confirmed (Lior's insight (d))

Lior's recollection (author's observation, L. Bakalo, 01_source_notes/lior_notes_geotubes_and_boscombe.md): an English reef broke very heavily
and was ridden mainly by bodyboarders. Checked against sources:

| Element | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Which reef | **Boscombe** | The only purpose-built surf reef in England that we found ("Europe's first", Surf Europe 2009). Lior's note cites the Raised Water Research page for it (RWR-BOS). |
| Ridden mainly by bodyboarders | **Partly confirmed** | RWR-BOS: "occasionally produced fast, slabby waves that were much more attractive to bodyboarders than surfers. The left was shorter and quicker, and occasionally offered barrels to bodyboarders." Rendle p.204-205: stand-up surfers "refer to it as a bodyboarders wave"; "Bodyboarders were more positive about Boscombe ASR". 115 of 721 UK respondents mention it as a gain for bodyboarding (p.180). Wikipedia: "a group of dedicated local bodyboarders continued to use it regularly before and after its closure". Video "Boscombe Surf Reef in action November 2009" (Gardiner): "being used by body boarders one day after the official press launch" (title and description only). |
| Not bodyboarders *only* | Correction | Davidson (2010) via Rendle p.70: "restricted to more advanced level surfers"; Rendle p.273: "an intermediate to expert break". Designers (MEAD10 p.5): in the design range (H10 = 1 +/- 0.5 m, T = 7 +/- 2 s, from 191 deg) "high-quality breaking waves suitable for stand up surfing"; outside it waves are "frequently more suitable to body-boarding due to the intense plunging nature of the wave breaking". |
| Too hollow / steep | **Confirmed** | Rendle p.70-71 (Davidson): "the wave is steep and frequently plunging"; "novice surfers and intermediates would not be able to surf the Boscombe Reef". Rendle p.180: "Bodyboarder prefer steep, heavy waves and refer to them as wedge shaped waves"; survey quote "not suitable for many stand-up surfers due it being steep and hollow". Rendle p.205: needed Hutt level 7 or 8 against a design of 5 or 6. Surf Europe 2009 (Paul Clarke, Bournemouth Surfing Centre): bodyboarders "surfing the hollow, powerful barrels that it was intended to produce". | Reef rose very shallow very fast | **Consistent, with a nuance on cause** | DERIVED (08_paper/data, profiles): crest 0.9 m below MSL, toe about 5 m, so a drop of 4.5 m in a ramp of 13-14 m (mean 1:3.1, max 1:2.3). Other Boscombe facts: tide window "surfable waves at mid-tide for a brief period (1 to 2 hours)" (Rendle p.71); sample 721 UK waveriders (493
shortboard, 126 longboard, 72 bodyboard; p.172); stand-up surfers "less satisfied ... compared to the bodyboarders" (p.178). A survey respondent:
"It doesn't offer anything for learners as hoped" (p.181). Damage: propeller strike and container loss from March 2011, closed 31 March 2011
[CARD, Wikipedia]. Lior's "broke apart after a year and a half" (author's observation) matches the 2009 opening to the 2011 closure.
The reef is made of geotextile sand containers (54-55 units, 15-70 m long), not small tubes.

## 4. Reef notes (short, with quotes)

**Narrowneck.** EcoShape: before settlement the reef "created some hazardous waves which were very hollow and sucked dry at breakpoint";
after settling conditions improved "especially when weather conditions are suitable for surfing. However, the reef has not gained a good
reputation as a surf spot and is rarely surfed", because it lies between world-class breaks that work in similar conditions. Swellnet (Nettle,
2017): "Under three feet of clean south-east swell, a negative tide, and light winds ... the reef itself was unsurfable" [CARD]; a 2007
monitoring report (via Swellnet) says the reef needs "long period, clean swell" [CARD]. Mead & Borrero p.3: non-uniform container placement
"detracted from the surfing amenity", yet "surfing rides of up to 60 s long have been recorded ... starting on the reef and connecting to
adjacent sand bars". The renewal physical model gave peel angles "within the surfable range for all tests" (Corbett et al. 2023, p.4; model, not field).
The RWR figures (~50 % of the time, 0.7-2.0 m, 150-200 m rides) come from the card; the RWR page returned HTTP 406 on 2026-10-16 and was not re-read.

**Cables.** Two incompatible readings. Pattiaratchi's video study counted a day "surfable" if "surfers were visibly present" and found the reef
"performing as well, if not better than" predicted; Mead & Borrero p.2 give 5-7 surfable days before and 142 after. Surfers said "the wave
quality is good during big swells ... and that the number of good surfable days per year is small" (Blacka et al. 2013, p.93). Pitt (2009,
via RWR [CARD]): "few surfable days per month"; crest about 1.5 m below the lowest tide, swell shadow of Rottnest Island. The 142 figure
counts presence, not quality. Still surfed in 2025 (video title, Ben Scott, 2025-08-16). Mead & Borrero p.2: "a fickle surfing break that does
not consistently produce good quality waves but may produce occasional periods of good waves".

**Pratte's.** The only reef with a counted surf record (grade A). Borrero & Nelsen (2003, p.13): "the total number of people that surfed at
Pratte's reef is certainly less than 25 over two years, hardly making it a legitimate surf spot"; "somewhere on the order of 10 surf sessions";
"in an academic sense, Pratte's reef was a success ... rideable waves were produced by a man-made structure" but "basically no one surfed at the
reef". Cause: "small size", 30 m cross-shore against about 150 m at Narrowneck, "70 times" less volume (p.13-14). This supports insight (a).
Fontaine (Surfline 2008): "it never produced a consistently surfable wave" [CARD]. Rendle p.39 (after Borrero & Nelsen): wave breaking "only
1-5 times per year" (Rendle's wording).

**Mount Maunganui.** Mead & Borrero p.3: in late October 2006 the right-hander "produced very good surfing waves which broke as designed in a
hollow, tubing manner"; after slumping (crest ~1 m lower, 2,800 of 6,500 m3 left) it "breaks during only a very confined set of conditions ...
a failure in terms of surfing". Derek Winter (NZ Herald): "three or four times since the surf reef was completed ... pretty pathetic" [CARD].
Scoop (Dec 2005, first days on the half-built reef): short sharp waves, more suited to bodyboarders than stand-up surfers [CARD]. RWR [CARD]:
"quick barreling waves ... like the Box but smaller", rides 20-40 m. Removed 2014.

**Opunake.** Moores (2006): "At present, there are surfable waves in the Opunake district on about one day in eight. The artificial reefs should
produce good surf one day in three" (target, not outcome; 1/8 = ~46 and 1/3 = ~122 days/yr, DERIVED). Designed waves 1.8-2.5 m face, 100 m
ride. Mead & Borrero p.4: "despite the confidence provided by the quality of the waves on the inside section ... it is widely considered a
failure". No post-construction surf counts exist. Cory Scott (NZ Surfing Magazine, via Newsroom 2021): "not one artificial reef has created a
wave of any quality" (his opinion, contested by the designer's email).

**Kovalam.** Good first season: 7-10 s lefts "fun and open faced" (RWR [CARD]); Collins (2010-04-29): "could very well be the best wave an
artificial reef has ever created". A Kovalam Surf Club member (Surfer.com, 2010-05-06, read by earlier agent from Wayback [CARD]): "less
surfable days because the reef only starts to work when the swell is above 3 feet, but when it works it throws up a sucking barrel ... the level
of the kids grows gradually with every surf session". Then Jelle Rigole (RWR [CARD]): "after the first monsoon season ... most of the waves just
roll over the reef now without even breaking". Mead & Borrero p.4 credit an existing "almost" break and clean non-monsoon swell. The
April-2010 praise and the "less than two weeks" tearing report contradict each other (unresolved in the card).

**Borth.** No independent surf evidence. Shaw Mead's email (Newsroom 2021): "while the structures have failed (except for Cable Station
(limestone), Borth (rock), Palm Beach (rock) and Narrowneck (sandbags maintained)), all have created surfing waves that have replicated the
designs" (designer, interested party). Blacka rates the surfing objective "Unknown" (insufficient monitoring). Stormrider still lists it only as
a proposed site [CARD]. Treat as unknown.

**Palm Beach.** Prenzler et al. (2022), 3,500+ h of shore-camera "wave peel tracking" Oct 2020-Oct 2021: "surfing conditions over the reef become
desirable when easterly or southerly swells with significant wave heights over 0.8 m and peak period over 7 s, with average ride lengths that are
significantly greater than in the natural surf zone" (extended abstract; the figure data are not in the text). Swellnet first test, July 2019
[CARD]: 4-5 ft, a surfer's remark "none of the reef's real estate is wasted in a closeout section. The takeoff area is small but it has a
surprisingly long wall"; a commenter says it "goes from too shallow to too deep really quickly" [CARD] (tide window). Cory Scott (Newsroom 2021)
disputes it: "Not one top surfer I know in Australia surfs those locations." Still surfed in 2022 and 2024 (video titles).

**Albany.** Peter Bolt (ABC, 2025-07-16, [READ]): "We were aiming for an intermediate wave, which it is when the swell is smaller. But as it gets
bigger, it breaks out there and it's fairly shallow. So it's intermediate-to-advanced on most days." Cameron Warburton: "it's a really high-performance
wave". Swellnet forum [CARD, grade D]: on everyday swells under 1 m "No, but it has created reliable banks either side ... The reef needs a bit of swell
to get it working"; injuries ("broken bones, gashes, concussion") led to a detuning proposal (about 18 rocks moved, Feb 2026 [CARD]). The "40 % of the year" is an RWR figure
with no basis stated.

**Burkitts.** Redgard (ABC, 2024-01-28, [READ]): before the works "you'd take off, go for about 2 or 3 metres and you would just run into rocks".
Pitt via RWR/Swellnet [CARD]: "a handful" of surfable days rose to about 30 a year, about 4 of them overhead. The count is a researcher's
estimate, not a measurement.

**Bunbury Airwave.** Never operated: the bladder tore at a seam near completion in December 2019 [CARD, ABC 2019]. The only wave datum is the
developer's account: "a wave did break on it ... peeled nearly all the way to the beach", 0.75 m (Bunbury Mail, 2022-03-22 [CARD]).

**Xala.** No surf report, wave measurement or footage description found; RWR (2026-03-31) only announced an unveiling. A web search on
2026-10-16 found no first-waves report.

## 5. Cross-reef patterns used by the paper

**Frequency is defined four different ways.** (a) Surfers visibly present (Cables, 142 d/yr); (b) wave-climate window (Boscombe: "appropriate
conditions ... on 20 different occasions between September 2009 and March 2010", designers, MEAD10 p.6); (c) share of year the reef breaks
(Albany ~40 %, Narrowneck ~50 %, both RWR, basis unstated); (d) anecdote or researcher estimate (Burkitts ~30 d, Boscombe ~15 d, Pratte's 1-5, Mount
Maunganui 3-4 in ~2 years). Only Pratte's (BN03) and Palm Beach (PRE22 camera) are counted records. The Boscombe pre-reef baseline is the best
benchmark: the council study found 77 "good" surfing days of 153 surfed days a year (Wikipedia and RWR-BOS; The Breaker 2018 [CARD]); Davidson (2009) found acceptable
conditions 20 % of the time on the beach and 0 % at the reef site (MEAD10 p.5). Design target: double the good days (about 154).

**Swell windows reported.** Palm Beach Hs > 0.8 m and Tp > 7 s (E/S); Kovalam swell above ~3 ft in the non-monsoon season; Boscombe design
H10 = 1 +/- 0.5 m, T = 7 +/- 2 s, 191 deg (MEAD10); Narrowneck long-period clean SE swell at low tide; Albany "needs a bit of swell";
Cables large swells only (Rottnest lee). Designers (MB11 p.2, after Shand) explain narrow windows by small structure volume.

**Tide windows.** Boscombe mid-tide 1-2 h (crest awash at low, too deep at high); Narrowneck low tide; Palm Beach "too shallow to too deep"
(comment); Cables crest 1.5 m below lowest tide; Borth crest above MSL. Tide-limited windows are the rule on reefs with 1.3-1.8 m spring range.

**Geometry vs outcome (DERIVED, reefs.json; ramp is toe-to-crest on the seabed, small n).** Boscombe 4.6 m over 14 m (1:3.1); Albany 6.3 m
over 20 m (1:3.2, LOW-confidence seabed); Mount Maunganui 2.7 m over 5.1 m (hand estimate; 1:9 over 20 m in profiles/SUMMARY.md); gentle ramps:
Palm Beach 5.2 m over 94 m (1:18), Narrowneck 3.8 m over 51 m (1:13), Cables 5.0 m over 41.5 m (1:8), Borth 4.6 m over 38 m (1:8). The two steepest
faces with a reliable record (Boscombe, Albany) both demand intermediate-to-expert skill; only Albany is rated a success, because it breaks on
larger swells and on a larger, deeper-crest structure. No regression is justified.

**Insights tested.** (a) volume: supported by Pratte's (BN03) and by M&B/Shand scale argument; (d) few days and narrow windows: supported
everywhere with data; "Australia does it well" holds for the rock reefs (Palm Beach, Albany, Burkitts), not for Narrowneck; (f) shallow crest
for small waves gives plunging faces: supported at Boscombe (design intent for 0.5 m waves); (g) beginners: no evidence of a beginner reef wave.
Whether each point is already in the literature is in notes/insights_in_literature.md.

## 6. Relevance for Haifa (INFERRED; to be tested in section 5 of the paper)

Lior's own notes (author's observation, L. Bakalo; 01_source_notes): natural surf in Israel on "less than 1 day/week"; a minimal surfable wave at
Shikmona of SWH 0.45-0.6 m, max height 0.6-0.8 m, direction 285-294 deg (field definition, Sept 2025 buoy comparison). That climate is below the
Palm Beach window (Hs > 0.8 m, Tp > 7 s) and close to Boscombe's design range, so Boscombe is the nearest analogue, including its trap: a crest
shallow enough to break 0.5 m waves turns into a steep plunging face and an awash crest as soon as the tide or swell changes. A small tide
range (Haifa) would remove the tide-window loss seen at Boscombe, Narrowneck and Palm Beach.
