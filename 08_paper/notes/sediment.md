## 0. Bottom line (for the paper's section 6)

1. **Independent, non-nourished test cases show no measurable shoreline response.** Boscombe (Rendle thesis, three years of surveys) found "no significant shoreline response"; Mount Maunganui (Weppe et al. 2007, via Blacka 2013) found effects "subtle and within the natural variation", "no noticeable salient"; Pratte's (monitoring summarised by Blacka 2013) found "no discernible impact" on bathymetry or beach. Lior's view (little measurable effect where there is no nourishment) is supported by these three.
2. **Where a salient was claimed, an alternative explanation was available.** At Boscombe the designer's 60 m "salient" (Mead & Borrero 2011) was measured 6 months after construction, while 11,100 m3 of surplus fill sand sat on the beach between the pier and the first groyne (Rendle, p.125-127); Rendle found the feature gone after the first winter. At Narrowneck the 20-50 m widening claimed in the literature was re-read by Blacka et al. (2013) as an artefact of an arbitrary reference line, and the 1.3 Mm3 nourishment of 1999-2000 is the main beach-widening event. At Palm Beach the reef was part of an AU$18 m nourishment project; the City's "550,000 m3 retained" is the same order as the ~470,000 m3 placed (DERIVED comparison, section 3.11).
3. **What the reefs do change is the bed within about 50 m of the structure**: toe scour on the lee side, steepening, local bar re-alignment. Scour at the toe is reported by Blacka (p.72) as general. Burial of the seaward part of the reef by sand is reported at Narrowneck (Jackson et al. 2012; Corbett et al. 2023). Pratte's bags were scoured to about 4 m depth, crest dropped 1.5 m in a year, and many bags were buried at removal.
4. **Theory agrees.** Ranasinghe & Turner (2006, abstract): "a majority of the submerged structures constructed to date have resulted in shoreline erosion in their lee", and the controlling parameters "are yet to be identified". A reef 200 m or more offshore in 3-5 m of water is in the regime where shoreline effects are weak (Rendle p.123, using Ranasinghe's approach).
5. **Only one non-nourished reef has a reported positive shoreline effect: Kovalam** (designer's weekly photographs, beach 10-15 m wider in monsoon, up to 30 m wider otherwise). It is designer-reported, under 3 years, and the structure later deteriorated (Blacka p.70). Treat as unverified.
6. **Australia is a separate, confounded case** (section 4): Gold Coast nourishment is continuous (strategy asked for at least 60,000 m3/yr after 1.3-1.5 Mm3 initial; longshore transport about 500,000 m3/yr). Lior's description (claimed protection is offshore, at depth, and cannot be attributed) matches what the City itself now says (sand-volume gains in offshore sandbars, section 3.10).
7. **Not available:** no source gives measured shoreline data for Cables, Burkitts, Opunake, Borth, Albany (opened Jul 2025), Bunbury Airwave (never completed) or the Mexico reef. The reasons are in the per-reef blocks.

## 3. Evidence by reef

### 3.1 Boscombe (UK), non-nourished by the reef project but surrounded by other works

Primary source: **[REND]** Rendle, E.J. (2016; the title page is dated August 2015). *The Environmental, Social and Economic Impacts of an Artificial Surf Reef - The UK Experience.* PhD thesis, University of Plymouth. https://pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/bms-theses/412/ (PEARL publication date 2016). Local copy: `papers/Rendle, E.J., PhD thesis, ...pdf` (343 PDF pages, text layer). Printed pages below. Not the "2016 thesis" as the brief says: use "Rendle (2016)" in the reference list with a note, or check Plymouth's own citation.
Independent: Rendle was not the designer. ASR Ltd and Bournemouth Council surveys were supplied to her by the council; the 2012 survey was withheld (p.95-96).

- Shoreline: "Over the three years investigated there has been no significant shoreline response to the Boscombe ASR; the small fluctuations (<0.2 cm) [sic, as printed; probably metres] that are observed are not different from elsewhere along the coastline, or out of line with normal seasonal fluctuations." "No salient (or tombolo) formation can be observed." [P, REND p.102, PDF 105]
- Bed near the reef: "Offshore, at the -3.5 m contour is where the ASR has made more significant changes to the bathymetry in a 50 m radius of the structure. There is some localised scouring at the base of the structure creating a steepening of the bathymetry towards the beach in the leeward area ... This contour has migrated 50 m from the ASR towards the shoreline in the leeward area." [P, REND p.102]. Scour in the lee 0.5-0.6 m (standard deviation of bed level) [P, p.107].
- Reef-adjacent bed between surveys 1 and 2 (2009 to 2010): about 0.5 m erosion to the west and north-west, 0.25-0.5 m accretion to the east and south-east; "indication of erosion at the toe of the structure towards the northern (landward) extent" [P, p.96].
- Volume of the beach in the lee fluctuates about the post-nourishment mean; "the fluctuation in the profile volume post-construction is not consistent with permanent salient formation" [P, p.113-114]. RMS variability of the profile decreased from about 4 m to 3.5 m after construction, but "it cannot be stated that the stability will continue to increase due to the ASR or that it is simply a reflection of the stabilising beach" [P, p.114]. Depth of closure about 300 m offshore [P, p.111].
- Rendle's reading of the theory: Ranasinghe's relation (Y/B against Sa/SZW) predicts +17.5 m (Hs 1 m), +27.5 m (Hs 1.5 m) and -5 m (Hs 2 m) at the shoreline for Boscombe's dimensions (her Table 5.4, p.118, her calculation; I did not repeat it). She states the observed non-response agrees with the theory because the reef is more than 200 m offshore [P, p.118, 123].
- The designer's salient claim and the nourishment confound: Mead (2010) showed surveys of 09/10/09, 31/01/10 and 22/03/10 "indicating the development of the salient in response to Boscombe Reef" [P, REND Fig. 5.27, p.126, PDF 129]. Rendle: the beach was surveyed "during a period where the beach is full post-renourishment" and the extra width appears only in the profile between the pier and the reef (p.114). Sand pile: 23,000 m3 piled on the upper beach to fill the bags; the reef needed 11,900 m3 "if built to design"; "the remainder 11,100 m3 remained on the beach, spread in the area between Boscombe Pier and the first groyne" [P, p.125-127, PDF 128-130]. Nourishment of the beach also occurred 2006-2008 (BIS4) and "increased beach width is eroded near the ASR" after 2008 [P, p.103]. Her conclusion: the temporary salient "disperses after the first winter period"; Mead's observation is not supported; the groynes and pier could equally explain retention [P, p.128, PDF 131]. The designer's own statement: "Within 6 months of construction the beach in the lee and to the west of Boscombe Reef had accreted to a maximum additional width of some 60 m along more than 500 m of beach" [P, MB11 p.5, section 4.1.3].
- Independent review: "no noticeable long-term impact on the 0 m contour" (Rendle & Davidson 2012) and accretion predicted mainly around the -1 m contour (K. Black pers. comm. 2013); inshore scour and steepening "well predicted at design" [S, via the Blacka intake note `05_qa/papers_intake/P8_blacka_2013.md`, Blacka 2013 p.70]. Blacka rating: surfing "Partially", protection "Minor" (Table 4.1, p.63) [S via intake, not re-read].
- Site context: 60 hardwood groynes, beach re-nourished every 10-12 years, net transport eastward, much of it "dragged offshore" in storms [P, MB11 p.5].
- Judgement: the thesis supports Lior's view for a non-reef-driven site; the reef was 210-225 m offshore in 3-5 m of water [card; Blacka Table 4.2: 225 m offshore, depth 3-5 m, submergence 0.5 m, nourishment Y, settlement/scour Y, p.66, S via intake].

### 3.2 Mount Maunganui (NZ), no nourishment

- No nourishment (Table 4.2 "no nourishment"), drift 60,000-80,000 m3/yr net, variable direction, tide range 2.0 m (Blacka Table 4.3) [S via intake, not re-read]. Net littoral drift 60,000 to 80,000 m3/year (Healy 1980 in Weppe et al. 2007) [P, Blacka p.101, PDF 113].
- Weppe et al. (2007) odd-even analysis, as summarised by Blacka: "30 m accretion at about 100 m to 200 m north-west of the reef through to regions of slight erosion. There was little net accretion in the region directly in the lee of the reef, indicating that there was very little salient type formation"; "a slight localised groyne effect"; "Any effects of the reef on shoreline position are subtle and within the natural variation of shoreline position; No noticeable salient developed" [P, Blacka p.103, PDF 115]. Surveys 2004-2009 with the reef 0-100% complete (Blacka Table A.1, p.102) [P].
- Blacka adds that the wider beach to the north-west is "possibly due to sheltering from the offshore islands or localised dredge spoil disposal", with beach-width variations of 20-30 m evident even before construction [P, Blacka p.103]. So Mount Maunganui is not perfectly free of other sand inputs (Port of Tauranga dredge spoil), but there was no nourishment scheme.
- Same work as read by the designer: "caused a widening of the beach of some 25 m and a positive influence along some 150 m of beach to the southeast" [P, MB11 p.5, section 4.1.2]. The two readings conflict; both kept. Weppe, S., Healy, T., Scarfe, B. & Immenga, D. (2007), "Shoreline Response to an Offshore Submerged Multifunction Reef at Mount Maunganui, New Zealand", Coasts & Ports 2007, full citation from Blacka's reference list (p.91, PDF 103); original not read.
- The reef was built "some 50 m closer to shore than design" at the limit of the Port of Tauranga dredge-disposal grounds, so "during large wave events, the reef is inside the surf-zone, which is counter-productive for coastal protection" [P, MB11 p.5].
- Hazard side effect: council statement "creating a large scour hole which affects waves and currents, increasing the frequency and intensity of rips" (Bay of Plenty Regional Council deputy chief executive Eddie Grogan) [card:mount-maunganui-reef R3, via the Geosynthetics Magazine 2023 article, [P] fetched 2026-10-15: "creating a large scour hole which affects waves and currents, increasing the frequency and intensity of rips which pose a serious risk to swimmers"]. Original council release not re-read.

### 3.3 Opunake (NZ)

- Designed purely for surfing (TRC 2009 p.3: "fast hollow wave, ... suitable for surfers and body boarders"). Reef 30 m wide, 3.5 m high, 99 m long, about 2,970 m2, about 4,900 m3, built in 3.0-1.8 m depth below chart datum on "a thin layer of sand over underlying rock strata" so that "no excavation was required" [P, TRC09 p.3-4 (PDF 9-10)]. **No shoreline or bed monitoring is in the report**: the Taranaki Regional Council programme covered intertidal and kaimoana (seafood) baselines and consent compliance only (Executive summary, PDF 3).
- Blacka Table 4.2 footprint for Opunake (30 m long x 100 m cross-shore, 1.8-3.0 m depth, ~300 m offshore, no nourishment, settlement "not reported") differs from the card (reef 90 x 20 m); unresolved [S via intake].
- Seabed change: "In 2010 seabed changes were reported to be causing uneven performance across the reef" [card:opunake-reef as_built_vs_design, from card ref R2 (a news item); original not re-read].
- Taranaki Regional Council (2009) is cited in full in section 6.

### 3.4 Pratte's Reef, El Segundo (USA), removed

All from **[BLACKA]** Appendix A.11, p.115-119 (PDF 127-131) [P, I read these pages]:
- Objective: mitigate surf loss after the Chevron groyne and the "subsequent backfilling of the adjacent Dockweiler beach" (coastal protection not an objective) (p.115).
- "Shortly following the completion of the initial reef construction, dive inspections revealed that the seafloor had scoured to a depth of approximately 4 m (at least 1 m of scour depth from initial bed level)" (p.116). Design: water depth 3 m MLLW, crest 1.8 m below MLLW; after the second phase (+90 bags, +80% volume) the apex was 0.9 m below MLLW (p.115-116).
- Monitoring: monthly beach profiles updrift, adjacent and downdrift; quarterly ADCP bathymetry and dives. "After several years of beach and bathymetry monitoring the reef was reported to have no discernible impact on either the surrounding bathymetry or the beach" (p.117).
- "In the year following the stage 2 construction, the reef crest dropped by 1.5 m, likely due to a combination of displaced bags, compaction of bag fill, and settlement from scour of the seabed" (p.117).
- Removal 2008-2010: "Many geocontainers were buried or partially buried; geocontainers were in clusters, with areas of bare seabed in between" (p.117). Of the bags originally placed, 142.5 (full-equivalent) were removed, 1 remained visible, 42.5 "were deemed to have been torn up and washed away or buried" (p.119).
- Rendle (p.39, PDF 42) says the reef broke "only 1-5 times per year", "began to sink into the sediment", with damaged bags "likely responsible for significant lowering through scouring effects" [S: Rendle summarising Borrero & Nelson 2003; original not read].
- Observation for the paper: sinking, scour and burial are documented here with an independent engineering source, in only 3 m of water.

### 3.5 Kovalam (India)

- Designer's account: weekly photographs from the lighthouse platform; beach "some 10-15 m wider in comparison to pre-reef conditions" in the monsoon and "up to 30 m wider during the non-monsoon" (Fig. 7) [P, MB11 p.5, section 4.1.4]. Completed late February 2010 (p.4). The same page says the "Lighthouse end of Kovalam Beach was an 'almost' break to begin with".
- Independent: "only one appears to have resulted in any significant positive effect on shoreline alignment to date (Kovalam). This structure has had less than three years of beach monitoring ... insufficient to enable confident judgement"; unpublished observations that "damage to the reef now hampers its protective effect (E. Rendle pers. comm. 2013)" [P for the first sentences, Blacka p.70 (PDF 82); the pers. comm. is [S] via intake note]. Blacka Table 4.2 (p.66): 65 x 85 m, depth 1.3-3.7 m, 90 m offshore, no nourishment [S via intake].
- Fate: Rendle shows the "clear up of the beach after the first monsoon post-construction ... one of the geotextile sand bags is washed ashore", September 2010 (Fig. 2.1, p.42, PDF 45) [P]. Later status (reef "non-functional", "waves just roll over the reef") [card:kovalam-reef-india R1 = Raised Water Research page, original not re-read]. A 2021 erosion-protection and walkway project (Rs 8.35 crore) was commissioned for "recurring" erosion at the same beaches [card R6, Kerala Tourism 2021; not confirmed as a repair].
- Judgement: the single positive, non-nourished claim; it needs independent verification (CESS assessment "pending" in 2013, not found).

### 3.6 Borth (Wales)

- A coastal-defence scheme, not an isolated reef: multi-purpose reef + two groynes + two breakwaters + shingle recharge; 275,000 t material moved including 84,500 t shingle replacement (contractor's figures) [card:borth-coastal-defence-reef size, ref R1]. ### 3.7 Cables Reef (Perth)

### 3.9 Southern Ocean Surf Reef (Albany), Bunbury Airwave, Mexico reef

## 4. Australia (Gold Coast): evidence together with the nourishment volumes

**Context volumes**
- Gold Coast longshore transport "about half a million cubic metres" per year, south to north [P, ICM managing director's conference talk, auto-transcript, ~00:49:24 and ~01:09:37; speaker name phonetic and not confirmed; YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch/Gi6wNNP6OKE, file `Initial info from Lior/transcript- goldcoast ... .txt`]. Same figure in Blacka A.2.1 (about 500,000 m3/yr north) [S via intake].
- Answer to an audience question: "we pump back about 80,000 cubes [of that] as needed", plus offshore and waterway sources; "a lot of most of the nourishment ... we put into this nearshore zone" [P, same talk, ~00:56:15 and ~01:09:40]. The Q&A asked about "the annual quantity of sand required to maintain these solutions".
- City's annual program: 60,000 m3 dredged from Tallebudgera and Currumbin creeks and pumped to Burleigh Heads and Palm Beach; a 7.8 km pipeline "expected to be completed this year [2022]" to pump up to 120,000 m3 from South Stradbroke Island to Surfers Paradise [P, ABC News Australia, 3 Sep 2022, "Millions spent to protect Gold Coast beaches, but climate change poses a huge challenge", https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-09-03/gold-coast-no-stranger-to-beach-erosion/101381812; author not shown in the extracted text].

**Narrowneck (reef 1999-2000; 84 containers added 2017-18)**
- Nourishment: "The large scale nourishment of 1.5Mm3 ... placed on the beaches in 1974" no longer sufficed by the 1990s; the 1997 strategy proposed "a minimum of 1.5Mm3 to be pumped ... to widen Surfers Paradise beach by 30-50m. Regular ongoing nourishment of at least 60,000m3/yr"; an allowance of 60,000-80,000 m3/yr north of the structure "has yet to be implemented"; "A total of 1.3Mm3 of nourishment was applied between February 1999 and June 2000" [P, JAC12 p.1-2]. Blacka: "approximately 1.5 million cubic metres of nourishment sand was placed south of the reef prior to construction ... Monitoring was complicated" [P, Blacka A.2.6, p.98, PDF 110].
- The strategy's own stated aim: "widen the beach by 20-30m ... large-scale beach nourishment and construction of a submerged breakwater 'reef' to act as a control point" [P, JAC12 abstract]. Reef design role: "to act as a coastal control point to stabilize the nourished beaches to the south" [P, JAC12 p.2].
- Shoreline claims, in order: (a) designer/ARGUS: beach widened 30-50 m in the lee over about 2 km after 12 years [P, MB11 p.4]. (b) Turner (2005): "general beach accretion of the order of 20 m in the vicinity of the reef, likely due to the combined salient and groyne effects" (2001-2003, 1000 m study area) [P via Blacka p.99, PDF 111]. (c) Blacka et al. (2008) as reported by Blacka 2013: shoreline receded "approximately 2.8 m per year south and north of the reef, while in the lee of the reef the recession was more like 4.3 m per year"; "by mid-2008 ... some 15 m further than sections of beach further north or south"; "only a minor planform perturbation in the lee of the reef ... likely of the order of 10 m"; "the published 20-50 m salient is a likely misreading" because widths were "relative to an arbitrary dune reference line" [P, Blacka p.98-100, PDF 110-112]. (d) Jackson et al. (2012): 2009 storms (peak Hs 6.2 m, Hmax 10.6 m) cut the six-month average shoreline by 20 m; "the erosion ... was accommodated within the wider beach created in 1999"; monthly average beach width 50-100 m; "Much of this increase may be due to onshore movement of nourishment sand placed offshore"; "a minor groyne effect"; "the absence of a long term salient ... suggests that ... sufficient long shore transport is occurring shoreward of the reef to inhibit the formation [of] a permanent salient" [P, JAC12 abstract and p.9-10]. (e) Corbett et al. (2023): seasonal beach-width variability about 40 m; "a salient feature about 100 m to the north as well as a small groyne effect"; widths 50-70 m in 2009 and 70-95 m (Sep 2018-Mar 2019, WRL 2019), "only 8 months after the renewal" and "possibly influences from massive mega nourishment of Gold Coast Beaches although placement did not extend as far north as Narrowneck" [P, COR23 p.667, PDF 5]. (f) Rendle's reading: a "circa 30 m salient (100 m wide)" is "often visible ... not always present ... exposed to the same variability as the rest of the coastline (circa 20 m)" [S for Jackson et al. 2010; P for Rendle, p.128, PDF 131]. (g) City 2019 statement: gains in "offshore sandbars rather than the upper beach"; earlier 30-50 m salient not corroborated by historical imagery [card:narrowneck-gold-coast outcome, ref R14 (Swellnet); not re-read]. This matches Lior's description.
- Seabed: "The nourishment and large storms prior to reef construction had created a very large storm bar (over 1 m high over the back half of the reef)" (Tomlinson et al. 2007, quoted in JAC12 p.2). 2011 survey: "Raising of seabed levels on outer reef since 2008 appears to have resulted in burial of seaward containers"; "a large proportion of the structure is presently buried by a large storm bar and could not be inspected" [P, JAC12 p.4-5]. 2023: "significant seabed changes which has resulted in burial of sections of the original structure as well as the damage and subsequent loss of individual GSCs ... a reduction of the effective reef crest level" [P, COR23 p.663, PDF 1]. Blacka rates protection "Minor" (Table 4.1) and notes settlement; the crest was not topped up (p.77-78) [S via intake].

**Palm Beach (rock reef, 2019)**
- Project: "The AU$18 million construction project began in May 2019 ... the city's largest offshore dredging and beach nourishment plan"; "the beaches along the Gold Coast will receive an approximate total of 3 million cubic metres of sand, with Palm Beach receiving approximately 470,000 cubic metres" [P, Royal HaskoningDHV project page, https://www.haskoning.com/en/projects/holistic-beach-nourishment-to-protect-palm-beach-gold-coast, fetched 2026-10-15; page gives the reef as 144 m wide, 330 m offshore].
- City claim: the reef "cost $18.2 million and has retained about 550,000 cubic metres worth of sand over its first two years" [P, ABC 2022-09-03, attributed there to the City of Gold Coast]. The cost figure is the whole project, not the reef alone (see Haskoning page).
- Monitoring paper: "The City ... delivered the Palm Beach Shoreline Project (PBSP) in 2019, involving beach nourishment stabilised by an artificial reef"; objective "retain beach nourishment sand along the most vulnerable central section"; monitoring "has shown an increased beach width and sand volume within the surf zone"; "increased volume of sand in the beach profile immediately south of the structure" [P, PREN23, 2-page abstract]. No before/after comparison with a nourishment-only control is given there.
- **DERIVED comparison**: retained 550,000 m3 (City, 2 years) versus 470,000 m3 placed at Palm Beach (Haskoning). Method: direct comparison of the two quoted volumes; they may use different control volumes and dates (ASSUMPTION), so only the order of magnitude is meaningful. The reef's own contribution is not separable from the nourishment, which is Lior's point.
- Engineer's economic claim: "Over a 30-year period it definitely stacks up against beach nourishment" (Evan Watterson) [P, ABC 2022]. No sand-volume baseline for nourishment alone is given.
