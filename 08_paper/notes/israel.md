## 1. Bottom line

1. Tide: about 0.4 m at spring and 0.15 m at neap on the whole Israeli Mediterranean coast (Rosen 2001, IOLR). Station benchmarks (Haifa, Jaffa, Ashdod) differ by no more than about 10 cm. Small tide, but storm and seasonal water-level variation is larger than the tide: 1-year low and high levels are -0.38 m and +0.64 m (DERIVED range 1.02 m), 100-year high +1.10 m.
2. Waves: calm summer (Hs rarely above 2 m, April to October), storm winter (Hs up to about 5 m, November to March, about 5 storms a year above Hs 3.5 m at Haifa). Most common Hs 0.5-1.0 m. Four storms above Hs 7 m since 1993. Haifa Bay waves are lower than the open coast (design wave height 3.0-3.3 m vs 5.1-5.8 m).
3. Sand: net drift northward, about 400,000 m3/yr at Ziqim falling to 80,000 +/- 20,000 m3/yr at the entrance to Haifa Bay. Sand moves seasonally (winter loss, transition-season and summer gain). Haifa Bay beach shoreline moves up to about +/-20 m seasonally. The "about 1 m seasonal bed-level change" Lior states is NOT published anywhere I could find; a DERIVED check from the +/-20 m shoreline swing gives about 0.3 to 1.3 m depending on slope, so 1 m is plausible but unconfirmed. His high-sand months (October, May) fit the "transition seasons" described by the GeoMare opinion but are not documented as months.
4. Geotubes: the torn tubes Lior remembers were at ASHKELON NORTH (laid 2017-2019), not Netanya. No geotube was built at Netanya. 48 tubes in five clusters, cost about NIS 11 M for the tubes (comptroller) or NIS 10 M (Haaretz), several torn after the first winter storms, 12 tubes replaced in 2021, protection collapsed by 2024. The company's own data say an unarmoured sleeve lasts "months to a few years".
5. Lior's "15 of 48 destroyed", "10 dunam, 4 m high" and the "step from 4-5 m to 2-3 m depth" are not in any source I could open. The comptroller's list of fragility causes (laying, filling, propeller, anchor, diver knife) is the official basis for the durability argument.
6. A senior Israeli coastal engineer (Prof. Nitai Drimer, Technion) wrote in Nov 2024 that the Ashkelon failures do not prove geotubes unsuitable, that they can be strengthened, and that Haifa Bay waves are much lower than the open coast. This is an opinion in a legal appeal, not a study.
7. Ecology: seaweed on Young-Jin tubes after a year and on Boscombe geotextile bags (monitoring 2009-2014) show colonisation is real; for Ashkelon the only sources are a surfer-group claim ("nothing develops") and the missing marine-biologist survey. Michmoret (CCell mineral accretion) and ECOncrete (bio-enhanced concrete) are alternatives with company-supplied evidence only.

## 4. Sand movement and sand shortage

### 4.1 Littoral drift (net northward)
- Nile littoral cell: since the Aswan High Dam (1964) sand comes from erosion of the Nile delta coast; longshore currents carry it eastward along Sinai and then northward along Gaza and Israel to Haifa Bay, "the northernmost final depositional sink" (Spanier & Zviely 2023, section 2.3.4, PDF p.8). READ.
- Net rate falls northward: about 400,000 m3/yr at Ziqim, about 200,000 m3 at Ashdod, about 100,000 m3 at Tel Aviv, about 80,000 m3 (+/- 20,000 m3) at the entrance to Haifa Bay (same, PDF p.8, citing Zviely & Kit and Zviely refs 61, 62). READ. CAMERI gives about 200,000 m3/yr (Ashdod) to about 80,000 m3/yr (Haifa) (search summary of cameri-eng.com, not opened). Primary sources behind these numbers: Zviely, D.; Kit, E.; Klein, M. (2007). Longshore sand transport estimates along the Mediterranean coast of Israel in the Holocene. Marine Geology 238(1-4), 61-73, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.margeo.2006.12.003; Perlin, A.; Kit, E. (1999). Longshore sediment transport on Mediterranean coast of Israel. J. Waterway, Port, Coastal, Ocean Eng. 125(2), 80-87, https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)0733-950X(1999)125:2(80). Both listed, not opened.
- Drift occurs in both directions locally; wave-induced currents in the surf zone to about 5 m depth, to about 10 m in extreme events; wind-driven shelf currents to about 30 m (Spanier & Zviely 2023 PDF p.8). READ. Net northward sand transport was observed at depths beyond 24 m off Ashdod (Zviely 2026, PDF p.48). READ (page via text search).

### 4.2 Active depth (relevant to reef burial)
Closure depth (Birkemeier 1985, h = 1.57 He): 8.0-9.1 m on the open coast, 4.7-5.2 m in Haifa Bay (Bitan & Zviely 2020 PDF p.3-4). A surf reef crest at 1-3 m depth, and its seaward toe at 3-6 m, lies inside the active zone: bed level there changes with the seasons and storms.

### 4.3 Seasonal and long-term change at Haifa Bay
- Zviely, D.; Kit, E.; Rosen, B.; Galili, E.; Klein, M. (2009). Shoreline migration and beach-nearshore sand balance over the last 200 years in Haifa Bay (SE Mediterranean). Geo-Marine Letters 29(2), 93-110, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00367-008-0126-2. READ. Abstract and Discussion: after the Haifa Port main breakwater (1929-1933), 1928-2006, "most of the bay's coast was in a steady state, with seasonal fluctuations of less than about +/-20 m, and slight erosion of about 7,000 m3 annually". Before the port (1799-1928) the shoreline grew 50-150 m (0.4-1.2 m/yr). Nearshore profiles: depths 0-5 m measured along cross sections up to 350 +/- 50 m (printed p.100).
- Sand trapped by the Haifa Port main breakwater: about 5 million m3 between 1929 and 2004, average 66,000 m3/yr; about 1.3 million m3 dredged in the early 1960s and about 2.5 million m3 in 2005-2007 from the sandbar (Zviely et al. 2009, p.95). READ. The trap held about the whole net drift at the bay entrance (80,000 +/- 20,000 m3/yr): precedent for what a large structure can intercept.
- Qualitative statement (GeoMare opinion, cover date November 2024, PDF p.6): beaches in ecological balance show seasonal sand movement: "in winter there is a natural net loss of sand due to storms, and in the transition seasons there is natural feeding of sand through dunes"; the longshore current carries sand from the water to the shore by waves, winds and local currents "mainly in the transition seasons and in summer"; the author's aerial-photo analysis shows a south-to-north current in winter and a north-to-south current in the north of the bay in transition seasons. Sarid, N. (GeoMare), Expert opinion on alternatives for protecting the Kiryat Haim beaches, prepared for Zalul, https://zalul.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/חוות-דעת-בנושא-חלופות-נעמה-שריד.pdf (Hebrew; READ). Opinion, not a measurement.
- DERIVED check of Lior's "about 1 m seasonal bed-level change": if the +/-20 m shoreline swing is a rigid translation of the profile, the vertical change at a fixed point is (shoreline excursion) x (profile slope). Haifa Bay nearshore mean slope from Zviely et al. 2009's survey geometry: 5 m depth over about 350 m = 1:70 (range 1:60-1:80). Excursion +/-20 m (40 m peak to peak) gives +/-0.29 m (0.57 m peak to peak). On a steeper profile of 1:30 (bar and beach face, ASSUMPTION) the figure is +/-0.67 m (1.3 m peak to peak). So the vertical swing is of order 0.3-1.3 m, and Lior's 1 m is inside that range but not demonstrated. Treat as "Lior's field observation, order of magnitude supported by a published +/-20 m seasonal shoreline swing".
- Not found: any published seasonal profile or bed-level time series for Haifa (searches in English and Hebrew; dead-end entry in `02_research/dead_ends/_paper_general.json`). The CAMERI report for 2001-2013 (cited in the Zalul appeal) probably holds Haifa Bay survey data (not obtained).

### 4.4 Lior's high-sand seasons (October and May)
No source gives months. Related, READ: Bitan & Zviely 2020 recommend starting nourishment at the end of the winter storm season (April-May), PDF p.13; the GeoMare opinion places natural onshore feeding in the transition seasons and summer (above). October (after summer) and May (after winter) match "transition seasons" but the sand-rich months remain Lior's observation. Sea level (not sand) is high in August-September and December (Rosen 2001, PDF p.4).

### 4.5 Sand shortage and nourishment behaviour
- Sand shortage is stated by the press as one cause of cliff collapse at Netanya and Herzliya (Haaretz 7 Feb 2019, READ) and, in the literature, as the Nile supply cut-off plus structures that interrupt the drift (Spanier & Zviely 2023, p.8).
- Ashkelon North nourishment: 136,000 tonnes in July 2015 (sand from the Rotenberg power-plant cooling basin, about NIS 3 M); "already after one winter most of the fed sand had drifted west" (State Comptroller 2020, PDF p.71, READ). North Ashkelon marina project 2015: nourishment widened the beach by 30 m within three weeks and the beach was back to its initial width six months later (Bitan & Zviely 2020, PDF p.7-8, READ).
- Ashkelon (PEI fence) nourishment: "a few days after the nourishment, the sandbar began to erode due to a typical seasonal storm, and in less than three weeks most of the sandbar had almost disappeared"; a storm of Hs 5.2 m and Hmax 10 m in early December 2016 "destroyed all evidence of the nourishment" (Bitan & Zviely 2020 PDF p.10). READ. Second Ashkelon nourishment May-June 2017: 185,000 m3.
- Design guidance: about 450 +/- 50 m3 per metre of beach for certain success on moderate-wave, low-tide coasts (Bitan & Zviely 2020, conclusion 3, PDF p.12); imported grain size 1.5-2.0 times native (conclusion 4). READ.
- Kiryat Haim (Haifa Bay): the Zalul appeal reports that the Israel Ports Company planners raised the breakwaters from 1.5 m to 2.3 m above sea level, a 2.3 m "wall" along 1.7 km (appeal PDF p.11, 16, 20, READ; the earlier dossier counts seven breakwaters, CARD), and quotes experts that fed sand was swept away after the first winter storm (GeoMare opinion PDF p.6, READ).

### 4.6 What this means for a surf reef at Haifa (for the paper, with evidence strength)
- Burial and exhumation: crest and toe lie within the 4.7-5.2 m closure depth; seasonal vertical change of 0.3-1.3 m (DERIVED, section 4.3) is of the same size as the reef height of small reefs (Pratte's 1.35 m, Bunbury 2.0 m, Narrowneck 2.2 m in the dataset). Medium evidence.
- Sand trapping in transition seasons: Haifa Port trapped about the full net drift (66,000 m3/yr vs 80,000 +/- 20,000 m3/yr) with its main breakwater (a large emergent structure, length not checked here); a submerged reef would trap far less (ASSUMPTION; no measurement found). The idea of covering trapped sand with a geotextile sheet is not documented anywhere I found. Low evidence.
- Nourished sand is lost within weeks to months at Ashkelon (above), so any reef scheme that depends on imported sand carries a maintenance cost.

### 5.3 Causes
- Official list of fragility (State Comptroller 2024, PDF p.9, footnote 25, READ): a geotube is a two-layer fabric tube up to 5 m diameter and 30 m long; "the main disadvantage ... is the fragility of the synthetic material: unprofessional laying, non-optimal filling, external damage for example from propeller blades, anchors or divers' knives - all can easily cut the fabric and cause its content to leak, leading to rapid collapse of the structure" (Hebrew: החסרון העיקרי של הגיאוטיוב הוא ה'שבריריות' של החומר הסינטטי ...).
- Company data (PDF p.57, READ): an "armoured geotube" (fabric with metal conductors; low voltage precipitates limestone on the fabric) "can survive about 30 years compared with months to a few single years" for a sack without conductors "like the one the company used at Ashkelon North". Pilot from September 2023, not yet in the sea (earlier note, CARD).
- Hydraulic cause: not stated by the sources; Lior's own hypothesis (strong breaking where depth drops from 4-5 m to 2-3 m) is unsupported. Drimer 2023 (BD23) shows the critical load just before breaking but does not model fabric tearing (see `drimer_2026_forces.md`).
- Surfers' view: Shomrei Hayam (Ashkelon, mainly surfers) opposed the tubes; founder Assi Dvir: "Nothing develops near the geotubes, and they damage the marine environment"; the group prefers breakwaters (Haaretz).

### 5.4 Expert view in favour (opinion)
Drimer, quoted in the Zalul appeal of 2 July 2025 (PDF p.23): the failures at Ashkelon led to an opinion that geotubes are not durable in Israeli conditions, "premature and unfounded"; not enough experience, and tubes can be strengthened, possibly with attached seaweed strengthening the envelope; Haifa Bay waves are much lower than the open coast. Full quotes in Hebrew and English: `drimer_2026_forces.md` section 3. Sarid (GeoMare, Nov 2024) lists geotubes as soft engineering with "low construction costs, simple installation, flexibility, possible removal", and cites Bar & Drimer 2023.

### 5.5 Other Israeli geotube projects
- Tel Aviv, Hof HaTzuk (Cliff Beach), submerged geotube breakwater: planned (2019 tender), surfer and NGO protest, stopped at tender stage in 2022 on the Ministry's recommendation. No cost estimate found. Not built (Comptroller 2024 PDF p.11 table, per the earlier note; CARD).
- Ashkelon South, area 39: geotubes not built; Dec 2017 committee decision limited stage 1 to sand; nourishment without sea protection eroded quickly (Comptroller 2024 PDF p.54-55, READ).
- Ashkelon 2005-2006 earlier trial (about NIS 1.7 M, about 20 m tubes; storm damage and vandalism): CARD only.
- Ashkelon North remains the only built geotube structure found.

### 5.6 Ecological benefit
- Evidence for colonisation: Young-Jin (Korea): seaweed on the tubes after one year (Oh & Shin 2006, 8ICG PDF p.4, READ). Boscombe (England) geotextile-bag reef: monitoring 2009-2014 found bryozoans and green algae replaced by red algae, hydroids and ascidians, and use by migratory, spawning and juvenile fish and invertebrates (Southampton ePrints record 414487, abstract via search summary; authors and title not confirmed because the page returned HTTP 403). Sarid (GeoMare) notes colonisation of tubes but "further research is needed" on local species (PDF p.4-5, READ). Drimer suggests seaweed could strengthen the envelope (above).
- Evidence against, Israel: only the surfer-group statement (Haaretz). No monitoring results in the comptroller reports.
- Marine biologist's survey that Lior mentions (one year after completion, depths, cobbles on the seabed as evidence of wave power): NOT FOUND online (dead-end entry). The earlier dossier names a marine biologist, Dr Yaakov Nirarani, who asked in Sept 2019 for a pause; spelling and role not confirmed.

### 5.7 "Geotubes are the cheapest structure"
- Supported in the literature: cheaper and faster than tetrapod or rock (Oh & Shin 2006, 8ICG PDF p.1-2, citing Pilarczyk 1996, Heibaum 2002, READ); Drimer: speed of construction, less disturbance, removable (above).
- Israeli numbers: tubes about NIS 11 M for 500 m (about NIS 22,000 per metre, DERIVED from Comptroller 2020 p.71 and 2024 p.48; the 500 m is the 2024 maintained length, ASSUMPTION it equals the 2019 length) versus the Netanya breakwater scheme about NIS 190 M for about 1,400 m of breakwater plus sand (CARD, about NIS 136,000 per metre; not like-for-like because it includes sand and rock). Lifecycle cost is the weak point: 12 tubes replaced in year 3, and the company's own survival estimate of months to a few years. No source supports "cheapest" over the life of the structure.

## 6. Michmoret ecological reef and ECOncrete (material alternatives)

All from the earlier dossiers (CARD); I could not confirm the Michmoret pilot by an independent search (search returned CCell's Isle of Man and Mexico work only).
- Michmoret (CCell Renewables, mineral accretion): pilot began 15 July 2022 at Michmoret marina; three submerged metal-mesh structures carrying low-voltage current that precipitates limestone from seawater; commissioned by MCCP; size, cost and results not published; no news after 8 Jan 2023 and the pilot is absent from the current MCCP project list. Sources: MCCP 25 Jul 2022 (https://www.mccp.co.il/page.php?type=page&id=7999&m=217&ht=), MCCP 8 Jan 2023 (id=8289), Maariv 28 Dec 2022 (https://www.maariv.co.il/news/environment/Article-968323). A UK company trial in Cancun reported rock growth of about 6 mm per month with power cut in April 2022 (search summary, company material). No independent evaluation found. The State Comptroller 2024 PDF p.57 records MCCP's interest in electrified-fabric "armoured geotube" as the durability answer.
- ECOncrete (Israeli company, Tel Aviv, founded 2012 by Perkol-Finkel and Sella): bio-enhancing precast concrete; Israeli installations: Haifa "Polinom" port breakwater antifer units (monitored 2013-2015: more than double invertebrate species richness vs standard units and about two-thirds native species), Herzliya Marina seawall panels (Nov 2014, monitored 22 months), Jaffa Port tide pools (Feb 2019); cost premium stated by the company about 2-7% over standard marine concrete. Sources: ECOncrete case studies (https://www.monmouth.edu/uci/wp-content/uploads/sites/58/2021/09/ECOncrete-Case-Studies.pdf), Perkol-Finkel & Sella (2014), Ecologically active concrete for coastal and marine infrastructure, in From Sea to Shore, ICE, pp. 1139-1149, https://doi.org/10.1680/fsts.59757.124 (listed, not opened). Company-supplied evidence; an independent caution about diversity metrics is in `econcrete-armoring-israel.md` R17. Relevance: ECOncrete addresses colonisation, not wave loading or sand burial; "ecologically enhanced concrete units" are also used in the Living Breakwaters project (New York; NY Governor release, READ), supplier not confirmed.
