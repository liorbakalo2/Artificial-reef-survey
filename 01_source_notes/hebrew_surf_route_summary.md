## Summary

This is **not** a third-party research paper — it is a short pitch document (4 pages) that **Lior himself wrote**, addressed "For the attention of: Deputy Mayor of Haifa, Mr. Avihu Han" (לכבוד: סגן ראש עיריית חיפה, מר אביהו האן), proposing to build a professional surfing wave route (תוואי גלישת גלים מקצועי) at the beaches of Haifa. It is data/briefing material for a real-world meeting, not an academic source.

The core idea: instead of building an energy-intensive artificial wave pool, construct an artificial submerged reef/structure on the seabed that reshapes how natural sea waves refract, "steering" wave energy from a wide stretch of coastline toward one converging point — mimicking the natural effect seen at headlands/points where the world's best big-wave surf breaks occur (waves refract around a shallow submerged promontory and combine into one larger wave). The document argues this is a "clean and completely green solution" requiring **no additional energy input**, only reshaping of the seabed contour (תוואי קרקעית הים).

It cites one directly comparable precedent — the Gold Coast, Australia artificial reef (built primarily for coastal/storm protection, with surfable waves as a "byproduct") — and one Israeli precedent for cost benchmarking — a submerged structure built off Ashkelon to stop coastal-cliff erosion (not a surf reef, but used here purely as an Israeli unit-cost reference). It closes with revenue/tourism projections for Haifa (domestic + international surf tourism) and a summary of environmental/economic advantages (calm swimming water behind the structure like a breakwater but without blocking the sea view/coastline, increased fish population, more efficient wave-energy harvesting, and surf tourism revenue).

This document is best read as **Lior's own concept note / motivation piece** for the wider Artificial Reef project — it supplies: (a) the core engineering logic for why a submerged headland-style reef amplifies waves, (b) Israeli cost benchmarks (Ashkelon), (c) Israeli surfer population figures, (d) global wave-pool price benchmarks used as an economic contrast, and (e) the Gold Coast, Australia reef as the explicit precedent to research further online.

**Haifa proposal itself**
- Addressed to Haifa's Deputy Mayor, Mr. Avihu Han (סגן ראש עיריית חיפה, מר אביהו האן).
- Core proposed structure: an artificial reef inside the water, built to "navigate"/steer waves from a wide stretch of coastline toward a single convergence point (ליצירת ריף מלאכותי בתוך המים כדי לנווט גלים מרצועת חוף רחבה למרכז אחד).
- Mechanism explained via a diagram (see Images, page 2, figure 1): a submerged (or partially submerged) headland/cliff (מצוק) causes shallow water near it to refract/bend incoming waves toward it from multiple angles, so several wave trains combine into one larger wave near the headland — described as the defining geography of most of the world's biggest-wave surf breaks. If the headland is fully submerged, the same refraction/convergence effect still occurs, just more gently ("עדין יותר"), and can be used to concentrate ("constructive pattern") waves from a wide beach frontage onto one point.
- Key conceptual difference vs. Gold Coast, Australia: in Australia, waves are already large and the structure only reshapes how they break for surfers. In the Haifa proposal, the structure is meant to actively **increase wave height** at one location (not just reshape existing big waves), which is why the more apt comparison is to an artificial wave pool rather than to a coastal-protection reef.

**Israeli surf demand / market data**
- Average number of days per week with naturally surfable conditions in Israel: **less than 1 day/week** (ממוצע הימים בהם ניתן לגלוש בתנאים טבעיים הוא פחות מיום בשבוע).
- Active surfers in Haifa + the North of Israel alone: **more than 5,000**. Of those, **more than 500** are "surf nomads" (נוודי גלישה) — defined in the file as people who travel more than half an hour to reach a good surf beach.
- Active surfers countrywide (all of Israel): **more than 13,000**.
- Hadera surf-simulator pricing: **1,200 NIS / 45 min / group of 10** (see reef section above).

**Haifa tourism revenue projections (Lior's own estimates)**
- Domestic tourism: projected **~100 people/week** arriving in Haifa from other Israeli cities specifically to surf there, totaling **~8,000 unique people/year** who visit Haifa at least once for surfing.
  - **Footnote 1 methodology** (page 3): based on the maximum capacity the wave/structure could hold — **40 people at a time**, with full turnover every **2 hours**, operating **1 day per week**.
- International/world tourism: the file argues Haifa's expected surf-tourism value will be "lower, but not by a lot" than Gold Coast's cited $8 million/year figure, reasoning that (a) Haifa is much easier/more convenient to reach than the world's "remote" surf destinations, and (b) the Mediterranean-region surfer population has very few other nearby options. This is supported by a world map of "popular surf destinations" (see Images, page 3) that the text says shows very few options in the Israel/Mediterranean region — used as the rationale for why a Haifa site would draw outsized international interest for its region.

**Stated advantages of the proposal (Lior's own list, repeated in the executive summary and again as a closing "Summary of advantages")**
- No energy used to create the waves (pure seabed reshaping, no wave-pool machinery).
- Massive contribution to the coastal-area economy; possibility of profit from the project itself.
- Environmental: calm, quiet water around the structure enabling safe swimming — described as achieving "the same effect as a breakwater, without harming the coastline" (i.e., without a visible/intrusive above-water breakwater structure — this is the direct conceptual link to the "keep the horizon clean, unlike a wave-breaker" requirement in the wider project brief).
- Increased fish population in the area — the file notes this also reportedly happens at other artificial reefs (מסתבר שזה קורה גם בריפים מלאכותיים) — no citation given for this claim.
- Possible added benefit: more efficient wave-energy harvesting, since waves would break at one concentrated location instead of being spread along the coast.
