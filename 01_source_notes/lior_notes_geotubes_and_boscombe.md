## Artificial reefs mentioned

### 1. Ashkelon Cliff geotube project (מצוק אשקלון) — Israel
- **Place:** Facing "Ashkelon cliff" (מול מצוק אשקלון), Ashkelon, Israel. (Exact sub-location/coordinates: not stated.)
- **Year:** Not stated in this file.
- **Type:** Geotubes (גיאוטיובים) — a sand-containment / erosion-control structure, not explicitly called a "surf reef" here; stated purpose was to prevent sand erosion.
- **Purpose:** To prevent sand erosion (סחיפת חול) at the site — a coastal-protection project, not a surf-enhancement project.
- **Size:** ~10 dunams (כ-10 דונם ≈ 1 hectare / 10,000 m²) in area; height 4 meters; total of 48 geotubes laid out. — *from Lior's notes*
- **Cost:** 10.5 million NIS (10.5 מ' ש"ח) for the ~10-dunam, 4 m-high layout. — *from Lior's notes* (no source article cited for this figure)
- **Financing:** Not stated (no financing body named).
- **Design vs. built:** Not explicitly contrasted, but Lior's own analysis (below) implies the built bathymetry differed from what the design intended, in the sense that the transition in seabed depth at the geotube line was steeper/sharper than appropriate.
- **Outcome:** Partial failure during construction — even before the full layout was completed, 15 of the 48 geotubes were destroyed (later refilled/repaired: "ומולאו לאחר מכן"). Maintenance costs ended up higher than expected.
- **Why (per the file):**
  - Stated claim: the higher-than-expected maintenance cost is attributed to the contractor not being professional/skilled enough, and specifically to not consulting an international company experienced in the field before construction. The file notes this is detailed on "page 14" of some document (not identified in this file — see Open Questions).
  - Lior's own opinion ("לדעתי" — in my opinion, i.e., his inference, not a cited fact): what actually happened is that the seabed went from a depth of 4–5 m straight to a depth of 2–3 m (where the geotubes were placed), and then in winter storms there was strong wave-breaking at that point which broke apart some of the geotubes.
  - A report by a marine biologist who surveyed the site one year after project completion is referenced as attached ("מצ"ב דוח של ביולוג ימי") — it reportedly gives good detail on locations and depths, and shows an arrangement of relatively large beach cobbles/pebbles on the seabed, which Lior notes is evidence of the wave energy/power at that site. **This report is referenced but not actually included in this text file.**
- **Citations given in file:** None (no article/report link provided for this project specifically).

### 2. Boscombe Surf Reef — Bournemouth, England
- **Place:** Boscombe, Bournemouth, United Kingdom.
- **Year:** Not stated in this file (to be confirmed via the cited article).
- **Type:** Artificial (geotube-based) surf reef.
- **Purpose:** To add surfable wave days (נועד להוסיף ימי גלישה).
- **Size / Cost / Financing:** Not stated in this file (the cited article's URL text hints at a "financing... pods" detail — see citation below — but figures are not given in Lior's own text).
- **Design vs. built:** Not explicitly detailed, but per Lior's analysis (see Outcome/Why) the failure pattern is described as very similar to the Ashkelon project — implying an unintended, overly sharp seabed height transition compared to what a smoother design would need.
- **Outcome:** Failed / broke apart about a year and a half after construction ("אחרי שנה וחצי הוא התפרק").
- **Why (per the file):**
  - Stated proximate cause: apparently (ככל הנראה) a boat's propeller hit the geotubes and damaged them. (Note: the Hebrew word used is "פלופלור," which appears to be a typo/misspelling of "פרופלור" = propeller.)
  - Wave-breaking at the reef was described as very heavy/powerful ("השבירה של הגל מאוד כבדה"), and mainly bodyboarders ("בוגיסטים"/bodyboarders) rode it, per Lior — implied to be a sign the wave was too powerful/hollow for standard surfing as originally intended.
  - A video link showing the wave is referenced ("קישור לסרטון שמראה את הגל") but **no actual video URL is given in this file**.
  - Lior's own opinion: this also likely resulted from a sharp transition between seabed height levels — and he states this sharp height transition, combined with the use of geotubes (which break down relatively easily), was in his view the main problem shared by both the Ashkelon and Boscombe projects.

  `https://raisedwaterresearch.com/spot/artificial-reef/europe/united-kingdom/boscombe-surf-reef/`
