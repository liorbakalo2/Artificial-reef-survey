# Borth coastal defence reef — footprint provenance

## Summary

The Borth "multi-purpose reef" is not one continuous berm but a **two-part ("double") submerged rock reef**: one elongated oval/racetrack rock mound plus one adjoining hook/crescent-shaped rock mound, sited off the southern end of Borth beach near the Craig y Delyn cliffs, Cardigan Bay, Wales. This two-mound structure is confirmed by (a) the contractor's own 2011 account calling it "a double reef" [R1], (b) the project's own progress-photo captions describing "the northern and southern multi-purpose reef" (already in the card's images list), and (c) a Royal HaskoningDHV/Ceredigion County Council public-exhibition location diagram found and verified this session [S1], which draws exactly two adjoining mound icons under one "Multi-Purpose Reef" callout.

Distance offshore is bracketed by two directly-conflicting, independently-sourced figures: **400 m** per a contemporaneous 2011 trade-press account [R1] and **300 m** per a heritage record and local press [R2][R3]. This file uses the midpoint, **350 m ± 50 m**, and does not resolve the conflict further.

**Alongshore length, cross-shore width, footprint area and crest depth are not published anywhere located or accessible this session.** This matches the card's own pre-existing "gaps" list. A low-confidence, explicitly-flagged schematic placeholder (≈145 m × 73 m envelope, ≈3,785 m²) is given in the JSON only to satisfy the polygon-format requirement; it is a rough order-of-magnitude bound derived by analogy and tonnage comparison, **not a measurement**, and should not be read as a confirmed figure. Crest depth is left unresolved (no value given at all) because no source, image or figure found this session offered any defensible anchor for even a rough estimate.

Overall confidence: **low** for dimensions and crest depth; **medium** for the higher-level fact that the structure is two separate/adjoining parts rather than one continuous berm (independently corroborated by text: R1's "double reef", BAM Nuttall's "northern and southern multi-purpose reef" captions); **low / unconfirmed** for the finer claim of which part is oval-shaped vs hook-shaped and which is "northern" vs "southern" — see "Verification 2026-09-25" below, this specific sub-claim was downgraded after re-examining the source figure.

## Polygon construction

Coordinate origin: the shoreline point nearest the reef's centroid (approximately the Craig y Delyn frontage, southern end of Borth beach). `x` runs alongshore, positive toward north (toward Ynys-las / Borth village centre — this matches the left-to-right reading direction of the [S1] location diagram, which runs from the reef's position near the village's southern end on the left to Ynys-las on the right, consistent with real compass geography since Ynys-las is north of Borth). `y` runs offshore, positive seaward (west, into Cardigan Bay). Shoreline is approximated as `y = 0`.

Two closed polygons are given (`polygons_m`, multi-part per spec):

- **Lobe A** (provisionally the southern mound, oval/racetrack): a 16-point discretised ellipse, semi-axes ~32 m × 11 m, centred at roughly `(-45, 335)`.
- **Lobe B** (provisionally the northern mound, hook/crescent): a 12-point bent-ribbon polygon (outer and inner edges of a hook), spanning roughly `x: -5 to 68`, `y: 295 to 368`.

Both were validated as closed (first point = last point) and non-self-intersecting using `shapely.geometry.Polygon.is_valid` (both `True`). The north/south assignment of which lobe is which is an inference from the [S1] diagram's reading order, not a labelled fact in any source — flagged explicitly in `orientation_notes` and `open_issues`. **These polygons are a schematic, order-of-magnitude placeholder, not a measured footprint** — see Open issues.

## Open issues

- The polygon in the JSON is an explicitly low-confidence, order-of-magnitude schematic placeholder (method: shape analogy + rough tonnage scaling), not a measurement. - R2 (Daily Post) redirected WebFetch to an unfamiliar third-party domain (`tollbit.dailypost.co.uk`); this was not followed, on the grounds that an unverified redirect target should not be trusted by default. The 300 m figure was instead reconfirmed via an independent web-search snippet and via R3.
- The two-mound shape finding rests on one schematic location diagram (S1) whose icons are demonstrably reused elsewhere on the same page for generic future structure locations; the "two adjoining mounds" topology is corroborated independently (R1's wording, the project's own photo captions) and is held with medium confidence, but the exact proportions and the north/south labelling of each mound are not independently confirmed.
- Live aerial/satellite inspection (Bing Maps) did not visually resolve a distinct offshore structure at the expected site; recorded as inconclusive, not as counter-evidence.
- Gemini's three cited sources for this reef could not be located anywhere online this session; all of Gemini's Borth-specific numeric claims are therefore treated as unverified at the source and not adopted, per project rule.

1. **`max_dimension_m` was wrong: 162 m → corrected to 145.1 m.** Recomputing the maximum pairwise distance between all vertices of both polygons gives 145.09 m, not 162 m. The 162 m figure in the draft was actually the bounding-box *diagonal* (√(145² + 73²)), which is a different, larger quantity than the polygon's true maximum extent — no vertex pair actually spans that diagonal, since the two lobes sit offset rather than at opposite bbox corners. This is a genuine computation bug, now fixed in the JSON and flagged in `open_issues`/`verification`.
2. **Shape-topology confidence was overstated for the specific "oval mound + hook mound" sub-shape and its north/south assignment.** Direct visual inspection of the re-rendered S1 page-3 diagram found: (a) the diagram's small legend icon next to the "Multi-Purpose Reef" label does show one oval/racetrack shape plus one separate hook/L-shaped mound — the source of the draft's description — but (b) the icon actually **stamped at the reef's own location** on the frontage line is a *different*, symmetric double-hook/"bowtie" icon that does not match the legend; (c) that stamped icon recurs identically at 6+ other points on the same diagram, including a **second cluster still within "Completed Phase 1 Works"** (a detail the draft's own text did not mention — it only flagged reuse "in the Phase 2 span"), plus four more within "Phase 2 Works" marking generic future Beach Rock Breakwater/groyne locations. This confirms the icon is a reused generic location-marker symbol, not a scaled or literal footprint drawing of the reef. **Fixed:** the higher-level "two separate, adjoining structures, not one continuous berm" fact remains at medium confidence (it was always independently text-sourced — R1's "double reef" and the project's own BAM Nuttall photo captions, confirmed present verbatim in `03_images/web/borth-coastal-defence-reef.json` this pass — not icon-sourced), but the specific oval-vs-hook shape assignment and which mound is "northern" vs "southern" are downgraded from "medium confidence" to unconfirmed/low confidence throughout the file (Summary, Derivation table, `shape_type`, `orientation_notes`, `images_relied_on`, Gemini-comparison shape row).
