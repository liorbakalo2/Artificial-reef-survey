## Check 2 - design version / state drawn
Verdict: **state drawn = the single 2019 prototype as installed and torn (base footprint); correct, but three statements in the draft were wrong or unsourced and are fixed.**
- Only one full-size Airwave has ever been installed. No second full-size install found: ABC Listen 2022-10-06 (still "working on a stronger, improved prototype"); Bunbury Mail 2022-03-22 ("back in the water in time for Summer, 2024"); SW Times 2020-07-09 headline "Airwave back for second wave" (paywalled; the dossier's R18 says second prototype planned Dec 2020); RWR profile page still "Planning (Opening Date TBD)" with "examining repair options"; Waveco's own site now shows only Modular Concrete ASR and Granite ASR (no Airwave), and bunburyartificialreef.info (granite reef proposal) does not mention the Airwave. Searched 2026-10-05; nothing contradicts "no later install".
- **Fix 1 (date):** the draft said the seam tore "on 16 Dec 2019". ABC (published Mon 16 Dec) says reports emerged Monday and the diver spotted the tear "on Friday afternoon" = Fri 13 Dec 2019 (SurferToday 2019-12-17 and RWR agree on "last week"/"Friday"). 16 Dec is the report date. Corrected throughout.
- **Fix 2 (shape of the as-built):** Tracks (Luke Kennedy, 2021-10-08) quotes Bottegal: "We kind of had a bit of a skateboard ramp type thing happening but I'm now going for a more classic dome shape" and The Tradie (2019-01-31): "10-12 metre, perfectly circular base ... a very subtle, flattened dome with a steeply angled back ... rising to two metres". So the 2019 bladder had an asymmetric (ramp-like) profile on a circular base. The plan outline (what we draw) is the circular base; the page must not describe the drawn bladder as a symmetric hemisphere (Gemini's "Hemispherical Circular Dome" is wrong in profile, right in plan).
- **Fix 3 (unsourced claim removed):** the draft said the redesign is a "welded PVC/polyurethane bladder". Tracks 2021 says only "a stronger compound that can be welded", manufactured in Australia, valves relocated, "more classic dome shape"; PVC/polyurethane appears in none of the fetched sources. Reworded.
- The redesign ("Airwave 2.0") has only been scale-tested in the Queensland hydraulic tank (Tracks 2021); not installed, so there is no later plan outline to draw.
- Which state does img1 show? It was published as the header image of RWR's tear post (16 Dec 2019), so it is the installed, 90%-complete bladder around the tear, probably after it. The upper-left quadrant (dark band + paler lobe) is consistent with a slumped/damaged quadrant (sources: tear "in the back quadrant", pumping "too heavily in one quadrant"); the outer outline stays circular. Interpretation only, not stated by any source about this photo.
- Provenance fix: img1 (2019/12/Airwave-from-Above.jpg) is the header image of https://raisedwaterresearch.com/the-bunbury-airwave-artificial-reef-tears-during-installation/ , NOT of the site-profile page (that page shows a different file, 2019/09/Airwave-From-Above.jpg, 700x467 oblique; see Check 5). img5 credit: the RWR profile page captions it "Image: Unofficial Networks", not Waveco.

## Check 3 - scale, area, bbox, angles (recomputed with geom.py + own numpy)
Verdict: **numbers reproduce exactly; scale is text-calibrated, not measured; uncertainty in shape.json (15 %) is fair but the stated 12.07 x 12.09 m "agreement" is by construction.**
- geom.py on shape.json pixel polygons: img1 area 65,673 px2, bbox 306 x 281 px, max dim 310.46 px (vertices 8-23), centroid (446.3, 334.9); img4 area 12,813 px2, bbox 157.8 x 107.4; img5 area 39,097 px2, bbox 222.8 x 226.0. Canonical polygon: area 112.06 m2, bbox 12.069 x 12.092 m, max dim 12.181 m. All identical to shape.json (area_m2 112.1, bbox 12.07/12.09, max_dim 12.18).
- Independent ellipse refit reproduces a = 153.6, b = 137.4 px, tilt acos(b/a) = 26.5 deg, px/m = 2a/12.0 = 25.59, rectified area 112.06 m2, mean vertex radius 6.00 m. Unrectified area at 25.59 px/m is 100.3 m2 (-11 %), so the oblique correction is needed and correctly done.
- img1/img4/img5 have no scale bar, no EXIF, no geo-reference (checked: Esri imagery cannot contain the reef; Wayback release 4756 holds 2016 imagery at the site per the tracer; not re-run because the reef existed ~1 week in Dec 2019). So scale = known dimension (text 12 m), method "known dimension", correctly labelled.
- Assumption stress-test: major axis = true diameter holds for an orthographic view of a flat circular base. A 1.6-2 m dome shifts the silhouette only along the tilt (minor) direction, by up to h*sin(26.5 deg) = 0.7-0.9 m, so the scale from the major axis is unaffected; the rectified minor extent is forced equal to the major by construction. Edge reading: half-max edge would shrink the lower-right rim by 0.1-0.4 m (see Check 1), i.e. diameter -2 to -3 %.
- Circle-vs-oval test (the only independent shape evidence): an ellipse of ratio 1.118 could in principle be a true 12 x 10.7 m oval seen from above. The circular valve/rope ring on the crown is also flattened by about the same factor in both views: outer ring ~73 x 63 px (ratio 1.16, read at 9x on img1, x 425-498, y 297-360) vs disc ratio 1.118; outer ring ~35 x 25 px (ratio ~1.4, read at 13x on img4, x 635-670, y 344-369) vs disc ratio 1.498. Ring/disc ratio agreement within ~7 % in both -> the outline is a circle to roughly +-7 % in axis ratio (+-0.8 m on 12 m); reading error on the small ring is comparable, so treat as supportive, not exact. Text ("12-metre-wide", Tradie "perfectly circular base") and the design graphic img5 agree.
- Cross-image consistency: the orange marker buoy (pixel centroid by colour threshold: img1 (499.6, 164.7), img4 (635.5, 288.0)) rectifies, using each image's own ellipse and 12 m, to 7.8 m (img1) and 8.5 m (img4) from the disc centre at 84 deg / 79 deg from each image's major axis. 9 % distance and 5 deg bearing difference, within the parallax of a surface buoy over a bladder on the bottom. This supports the rectification for both tilts but is NOT independent of the 12 m (both use it).
- Independent size plausibility (not from the 12 m): (a) swimmer beside the disc in img4 spans ~17 px against a 157.8 px disc: at 12 m that is 1.3 m, plausible for a swimmer with arms/splash; a disc narrower than ~8 m or wider than ~24 m would be excluded (+-40 %). (b) 140 t of sand (Bunbury Mail 2022, Tracks 2021) needs a dome volume of at least ~80 m3 at ~1.7 t/m3; a spherical cap of height 2 m holds 80 m3 only if its diameter is >= ~10 m (cap volume pi*h*(3a^2+h^2)/6; 12 m x 2 m = 117 m3, 12 m x 1.6 m = 93 m3). Both are text/physics plausibility checks, not measurements.
- Angles: none defined (circle; no shoreline in img1/img4). Correct.
