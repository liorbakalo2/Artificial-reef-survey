The "All (compare)" view puts every built 3D model into **one three.js scene at one scale**. It is not a new model: the geometry is read from each model's own viewer, so it shows exactly what that viewer shows by default.

**How the geometry is obtained (build time, `src/export_combined.py`).** Each copied viewer (`3d/<slug>/index.html`) is opened in a headless Chrome of its own. A wrapper around `THREE.Scene` lets the script read the viewer's scene graph; no viewer file is changed. The visible mesh with the largest plan-view bounding box is the seabed patch, the meshes inside it are the reef. Whatever the viewer shows at load (its default state and, where models have them, its default outline version) is exported. Positions are stored as 16-bit integers (resolution 1.4 cm horizontally, sub-millimetre vertically) in `3d/combined/<slug>.js`, so the view works from a plain file without any fetch.

**Frame.** Every reef is rotated about the vertical axis so that "offshore" points the same way (a pure rotation: nothing is mirrored, stretched or re-scaled). The direction "offshore" is taken from the model's own frame note and re-checked against the seabed slope. The shared shoreline is z = 0 of each model (its own fitted waterline, see the model's methods for the date and tide of that line). Heights are metres above each site's own mean sea level (MSL), so water level, depth colours and the water plane are comparable; absolute datum differences between sites are not shown. Side by side, the patches are placed left to right with a 40 m gap, in the order of the selector. In **Overlay** every reef is centred on its own footprint centroid and the shore-normal axis is shared; seabeds are hidden and the reefs are translucent, so footprints, crest levels and orientation to the shore can be compared directly.

**What is common to all reefs and what is not.** Plan distances, the metre grid (10 m cells, bold every 50 m), the scale bars (50 m, 100 m), the depth colour ramp of the seabed and the vertical-exaggeration slider are shared. Each reef has its own hue; the confidence of its 3D model is written on its label (a reef with low confidence is drawn exactly like one with high confidence, so read the label). Seabed patches differ in size, resolution and quality of the underlying data (see each model's methods), and the reef shapes differ in how much of their form is surveyed or idealised.

**Checks.** After every export the meshes are read back and compared with the model's own `model.js`: the footprint area of the up-facing triangles against the area of the model's toe polygon, the footprint centroid against the polygon centroid (this also proves that the rotation and the handedness of the frame are right), the crest level against the model's crest value, the seabed height along the shoreline against 0 m, and the direction of the seabed slope.
